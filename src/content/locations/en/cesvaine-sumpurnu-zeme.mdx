Cesvaine in Vidzeme is linked to Šmits belief texts on *Sumpurņu zeme* (LP VII, I, 994,1; J. Karps). Informants described dog-snout people who speak and bark, wear leaf clothing, and share their land with one-eyed or one-limbed neighbours who, when paired, run like the wind.

These are oral-belief provenances for the dogsnout land motif, not mapped shrines or sacred groves.
