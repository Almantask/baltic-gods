The Eleja Hollow Stone (Elejas dobumakmens) is a significant archaeological and mythological monument located in the Zemgale region of Latvia. Historically situated near local homesteads, this massive glacial erratic was integrated directly into the stone masonry boundary wall of the Eleja Manor park during the estate's construction.

A distinct cup-mark (dobums) is hollowed into the upper surface of the boulder. This feature connects it to the wider Baltic tradition of cup-marked ritual stones (dobumakmeņi or kausakmeņi) dating from the Bronze to the Iron Age. In Semigallian and local Latvian folklore, the stone was treated as a sacred shrine. Peasants brought offerings of milk, grain, and beer, pouring them into the hollow basin to secure the blessings of fertility and a bountiful harvest.
