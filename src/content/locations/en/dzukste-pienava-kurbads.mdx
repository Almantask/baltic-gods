Džūkste–Pienava is the Semigallian countryside where Ansis Lerhis-Puškaitis recorded the long *Ķēves dēls Kurbads* cycle in 1891. In the underworld episode, meadows belong to a Sumpurnis who blinds a herdsman for illicit grazing, keeps a healing-herb jar, and commands a fierce guardian bird.

Kurbads seizes the monster by the dog throat and clubs it dead, then shields the bird’s nestlings from hail. Rihards Zariņš later etched the duel as *Kurbads un sumpurnis* (1908–1911). This landscape is a tale-recording provenance, not a cult shrine.
