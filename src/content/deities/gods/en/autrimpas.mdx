## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Autrimpas (also *Autrimpus*, *Autrimps*, *Antrimpas*, *Autrympus*, *Aurimpus*, *Autrymius*; related chronicle form *Natrimpe*) appears in sixteenth-century Prussian source lists as a sea deity.

- ***Sudovian Book* (*Sudauer Büchlein*, 16th c.)**: Lists *Autrimpus* as god of the seas and the great lagoons / large waters.
- **Jan Malecki (*Libellus de sacrificiis et idolatria*, 1551)**: Names *Antrimpas* as a sea god.
- ***Interpretatio romana***: In the same sixteenth-century corpus he is equated with Roman Neptune and named beside the flowing-water and seafaring gods of the Prussian lists.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In those lists Autrimpas governs the seas and large lagoons (waters of the *marių* / *grossen Sehe* type). Later chronicle expansion (Stryjkowski) also places ponds, pools, and lakes under the same water domain.

**Relations:**
- **Patrimpas / Potrimpas**: Named beside Autrimpas as the god of flowing waters within the Prussian water complex.
- **Bardaitis (Bardoaits)**: Named beside Autrimpas as the seafaring / ships god (*jūreivystės dievas*) of the same catalogues.

## 3. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Autrimpas’s recorded domain is the Old Prussian seas and great lagoons of the Baltic coastal world described in the sixteenth-century lists — open salt water together with large lagoon and inland waters.
