## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Linksminė (authentically recorded by 16th-century author Jan Łasicki as *Lymschnis*, invoked *pro gaudio*; also known in folklore as *Linksmybė / Linksmybių deivė*) is the Lithuanian tutelary deity of joy, festive merriment, communal laughter, and peaceful banqueting. The teonym derives directly from the Lithuanian adjective *linksmas* ("joyful, merry, cheerful", abstract noun *linksmybė* "merriment" < Proto-Baltic *\*linksmas*, cognate with Latvian *līksms* "merry, joyous" < Proto-Indo-European *\*lengʷʰ-* "light, agile, nimble").

Historical chronicles and comparative folklore research document the authentic domestic veneration of Linksminė:

* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum...*, 1582; publ. 1615)**: Distinctly recorded *Lymschnis* as the deity invoked for joy and happiness (*Lymschnis pro gaudio*), documenting her authentic place in the 16th-century Samogitian household and communal ritual calendar.
* **Aleksander Brückner (*Starożytna Litwa: lud i bogi*, 1904)**: Analyzed Łasicki's recording of *Lymschnis*, confirming its genuine linguistic status as an authentic Baltic personification of communal festive joy (*linksmybė*).
* **Modern Critical Baltic Scholarship**: Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, Vol. 2), Vytautas Ališauskas (*Jono Lasickio pasakojimas apie žemaičių dievus*, 2006), and Gintaras Beresnevičius examine Linksminė as a social genius loci who maintained harmony, laughter, and goodwill during communal feasts (*sambariai*) and wedding celebrations.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic social cosmology, joy (*linksmybė*) was not considered merely an emotional state, but an active sacred power that repelled malicious spirits, strengthened community bonds, and ensured abundant harvests.

### Sovereign of Festive Merriment and Communal Harmony

* **Patroness of Communal Feasts (*Sambariai / Talkos*)**: Presides over communal post-harvest celebrations, beer festivals, and mutual-aid work gatherings, ensuring uninhibited joy and friendly cooperation.
* **Guardian of Wedding Merriment**: Protects bridal banquets and dances from malice, sorrow, or quarrelling, infusing the gathering with cheerful songs (*dainos*).
* **Dispeller of Grief and Melancholy**: Banishes dark thoughts and sorrow from the domestic hearth, bringing light, laughter, and spiritual levity to the household.
* **Keeper of Social Goodwill**: Oversees ritual toasts and shared drinking horns, ensuring that hospitality fosters long-lasting peace among neighbors.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folklore and ethnographic accounts portray Linksminė through festive, radiant imagery:

* **Radiant and Smiling Lady**: Conceived as a beautiful, smiling maiden clothed in bright linen garments with amber and floral embroidery.
* **Carved Wooden Beer Cup (*Kaušas*)**: Holds an intricately carved two-handled wooden ale bowl used for circulating ceremonial toasts during communal feasts.
* **Festive Kanklės / Kokle**: Plays a carved multi-stringed Baltic zither (*kanklės*), filling the banqueting hall with melodies that inspire dance and singing.
* **Crown of Meadow Flowers**: Crowned with fresh summer wildflowers (cornflowers, daisies, and marigolds), embodying the lively bloom of festive gatherings.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Linksminė is centered in communal gathering areas across Lithuania:

* **Samogitian Feast Greens and Village Squares**: Open communal greens where villages assembled for seasonal dancing, games, and post-harvest feasts (*sambariai*).
* **The Farmstead Banqueting Hall (*Svetainė / Pirkia*)**: The central room of the timber homestead where long tables were set for wedding guests.
* **Communal Brewery Sheds**: Gathering places where seasonal beer was brewed collaboratively by the village.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic communities observed dedicated traditions to welcome Linksminė to their celebrations:

* **The First Festive Toast (*Pirmasis kaušas*)**: At the opening of a feast, the elder or host raised the first wooden cup of ale toward the sun, pouring a small libation on the floor in honor of Linksminė.
* **Song Competition and Dance Circles**: Singing lively polyphonic rounds (*sutartinės*) and dancing until dawn was believed to strengthen the presence of joy and ensure prosperity.
* **Ban on Quarrelling during Feasts**: Strict customary law forbade anger, fighting, or settling personal grudges during festive meals; disturbing the joy was considered an offense against the deity.
* **Hospitality Toasting**: Every guest, including wandering musicians and travelers, was welcomed with a full horn of ale to share in the communal merriment.
