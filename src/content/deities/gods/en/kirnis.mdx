## 1. Etymology & Historical Chronicles

Kirnis (also recorded as *Kirnus*) is a Baltic mythological figure and tutelary spirit of brushwood, marshy copses, cherry trees, and orchard flora. Linguistically, modern academic etymology (Kazimieras Būga, Valteris Jaskevičius, Norbertas Vėlius, and Vytautas Ališauskas) derives the theonym from the Baltic root *\*kirna-* (Lithuanian: *kìrna* / *kirnìs* — "waterlogged thicket, marshy brushwood, peat-bog root, submerged stump / pelkėtas krūmynas, lūžtvė"), indicating an original function as a guardian spirit of wetland copses and tangled undergrowth.

The figure is documented in Jan Łasicki's sixteenth-century Latin treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (written c. 1582, published in Basel 1615). Łasicki records:

> *"Kirnis caerasos arcis alicuius secundum lacum sitae curat. In quos, placandi eius causa, gallos mactatos iniiciunt, caereosque accensos in eis figunt."*  
> ("Kirnis takes care of the cherry trees of a certain fortress situated by a lake. To appease him, they throw slaughtered cockerels into them and fix burning wax candles in them.")

The source data was gathered by the royal land surveyor Jakub Laskowski (*Jokūbas Laskauskas*) during his territorial inspections in Samogitia, with the fortress identified by modern historians as the island castle of **Lake Plateliai** (*Platelių ežero Pilies sala*).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In the Baltic ecological world, Kirnis presides over the transition between wild wetland copses (*kirnos*) and cultivated fruit-bearing groves.

### Protection of Bushes and Fruit-Bearing Trees

* **Guardianship of Copses & Orchards**: Kirnis protects low brushwood, wild bird-cherries, sweet cherries, and domestic fruit trees from rot, pests, and unfavorable damp conditions.
* **Cultivation Balance**: Ensured that the delicate root systems of fruit trees and berry bushes flourished along moist lakeside soils.

### Divine Relations & Baltic Parallels

* **Forest Pantheon**: Operates within the Baltic vegetation sphere under supreme woodland deities such as **Medeina** (Lithuanian) and **Meža māte** (Latvian Mother of the Forest).
* **Latvian Parallel (*Krūmu māte*)**: Direct functional counterpart to Latvian **Krūmu māte** (Mother of Bushes/Thickets), invoked in ancient dainas alongside Meža māte to protect pastures and thickets.
* **Garden Mother (*Dārza māte*)**: Functional parallel to the Latvian guardian of cultivated gardens and orchard produce documented in seventeenth-century chronicles.
* **Subordinate Tree Spirits**: Paralleled in historical lists by *Biržulis* (birch stand spirit) and *Šilinyčius* (heath and moss spirit).

## 3. Character & Visuals

In Baltic folk perception, Kirnis is envisioned as an earthy, rustic woodland presence:

* **Brushwood and Orchard Guardian**: Conceived as an invisible nature numen residing among twisted roots, flowering branches, and dense lakeshore copses.
* **Ritual Attributes**: Associated with white and pink spring blossoms, wild cherries, dark marsh roots (*kirnos*), slaughtered sacrificial cockerels, and glowing wax candles affixed to tree branches.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

Kirnis's sacred sphere encompasses wetland thickets and ancient fortress gardens:

* **Waterlogged Copses & Thickets (*Kirnynai*)**: Wetland margins, peat-bog borders, and dense lakeside brushwood.
* **Lake Plateliai Fortress Complex**: The Castle Island (*Pilies sala*) and shoreline groves of Lake Plateliai in Samogitia, where the sixteenth-century cherry-tree rituals were recorded.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Folk Practices

Historical chronicles document distinct ceremonial practices performed to honor and propitiate Kirnis:

* **Rooster Sacrifices (*Gallos Mactatos*)**: Householders slaughtered cockerels and cast them into the boughs of the sacred cherry grove to secure the spirit's favor and ensure bountiful fruit.
* **Wax Candle Offerings (*Caereos Accensos*)**: Burning wax candles were carefully fixed upon living tree branches, illuminating the sacred grove during evening or nocturnal rites.
* **Baltic Bush Offerings (*Ziedojumi*)**: Paralleled in Latvian custom by casting ribbons, wool, and blossoms (*zieds*) at the base of flowering bushes to ensure family well-being.
