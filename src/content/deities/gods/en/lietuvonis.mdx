## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Lietuvonis (historically chronicled as *Lituwanis*) is the Baltic atmospheric deity of rainfall, cloud-pouring, and agricultural moisture.

Sixteenth-century chronicles and Baltic historical philology document this atmospheric tradition:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicle Attestations**: Documented in Jan Łasicki's treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (composed c. 1582, published Basel 1615, reprinted in BRMŠ Vol. 2) with the explicit gloss: *Lituwanis, qui pluvias dat* ("Lituwanis, who gives rain"). Maciej Stryjkowski's *Kronika polska, litewska, żmódzka* (1582, BRMŠ Vol. 2) likewise records *Lituwanis* among the deities invoked for weather control and seasonal showers.
* **Baltic Linguistic Etymology**: Derived directly from Proto-Baltic *\*lei-tu-* and Lithuanian *lietus* ("rain", genitive *lietaus*), Latvian *lietus*, and the Proto-Indo-European verbal root *\*leyH-* / *\*ley-* ("to pour, flow, drip"). The archaic agentive suffix *-uonis* / *-onis* designates "the one who pours / the rainmaker".
* **Comparative Folklore Parallels**: Corresponds in Latvian folk dainas (LD 28356-0, LD 33170-0) to the rain-bringing aspect of the sky realm, where showers (*auguma lietus*) are summoned through traditional rain chants (*lietus saukšana*).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic agricultural cosmology, Lietuvonis oversees the atmospheric release of moisture essential for crop fertility:

### Dispenser of Rain, Guardian of Crops, and Drought Dispeller

* **Dispenser of Soaking Showers**: Commands gentle, steady rainfall that penetrates dry soil without causing erosion or flood damage.
* **Cosmic Moisture Mediator**: Functions as an atmospheric intermediary between the upper sky vault (*Dangus*) and the receptive soil of the Earth Mother (**Žemyna**).
* **Protector Against Scorching Heat**: Invoked during summer heatwaves and prolonged droughts to avert crop failure and preserve spring sowings.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folk tradition depicts Lietuvonis through atmospheric and hydrological imagery:

* **Billowing Mist Garments**: Manifested as an aerial sky figure cloaked in grey cloud veils and trailing streamers of falling mist.
* **Earthen Water Pitcher (*Ąsotis*)**: Symbolized by the celestial pitcher or sieve pouring drops of life-giving water across the fields.
* **Sprouting Rye Shoots**: Represented by emerald-green crop shoots flourishing under fresh rainwater.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred terrain associated with Lietuvonis encompasses open fields, high weather-viewing hills, and springs:

* **Rain-Calling Hilltops (*Alkakalniai*)**: Prominent regional elevations used by farming communities for weather observation and seasonal prayers (`kernave-warrior-boundary`).
* **Sacred Springs and Waterways**: Forest springs and river valleys where moisture rituals and seasonal invocations were carried out.
* **Boundary Field Borders**: Cultivated field borders where communal prayers for rain were spoken before the summer solstice.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Historical sources and ethnographic archives describe established seasonal rain customs:

* **Offerings of Fowl**: Chronicle accounts document traditional sacrifices of white, black, or piebald chickens to summon or modulate rainfall.
* **Water-Pouring on Sacred Stones**: Pouring fresh spring water into the hollows of cup-marked stones (*duburuotieji akmenys*) during severe droughts.
* **Plowman Soaking Rituals**: Ceremonial dousing of the first plowman (*artojo apliejimas*) with well water to ensure abundant soil moisture throughout the growing season.
