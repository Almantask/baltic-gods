## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Perkūnas Tikras ("The True / Authentic Thunderer") represents the supreme, sovereign manifestation of the Baltic Thunder God, embodying cosmic justice, moral law, and ontological sovereignty.

Historical chronicles and comparative philology document the following foundations:

* **16th-Century Chronicle Attestations**: Recorded in Jan Malecki's *Libellus de sacrificiis et idolatria veterum Borussorum* (1551 ms., publ. 1563, BRMŠ Vol. 2) in the Prussian-Sudovian ritual formula *Perkunatelete / Percunos Tikros*. Hieronymus Malecki (1561, BRMŠ Vol. 2) similarly lists *Parkuns* as the sovereign god of thunder and lightning. Jan Łasicki (1582 / publ. 1615, BRMŠ Vol. 2) records *Percuna tete* as the celestial mother of thunder who washes the sun.
* **Etymology & Theological Semantics**: *Perkūnas* derives from Proto-Indo-European *\*per-kʷ-u-no-s* ("Lord of the Oak / Striker of the Heights", cognate to Latin *quercus*, Vedic *Parjanya*, Slavic *Perun*). The epithet *Tikras* (from Baltic *\*tik-* / PIE *\*tekʷ-* "to be fitting, legitimate, authentic") serves as a sacral disambiguator, distinguishing the supreme heavenly Thunderer from lesser weather daemons or demonic counterfeits.
* **Comparative Baltic Evidence**: Directly corresponds in Latvian folklore to **Īstais Pērkons** / **Pērkontēvs** (LD 33701-0, LD 33713-0), who strikes with nine bolts and wields a golden sword.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In the Baltic theological hierarchy, Perkūnas Tikras holds paramount cosmic authority:

### Supreme Celestial Sovereign, Moral Judge, and Enforcer of Cosmic Law

* **The Primary Mythic Duel (*Pagrindinis mitas*)**: Enforces cosmic order (*darna*) from the heights of the World Oak, striking down the chthonic adversary (*Velnias*) who conceals himself in rocks, hollow trunks, or cattle, thereby liberating blocked waters and hidden wealth.
* **The First Spring Thunder (*Pirmasis griaustinis*)**: The inaugural strike of Perkūnas in spring "unlocks the earth" (*atrakina žemę*), rousing slumbering vegetation, shattering winter ice, and fertilizing Žemyna.
* **Guardian of Sacred Oaths and Justice**: Punishes perjurers, murderers, oath-breakers, and tyrants with pinpoint lightning strikes, functioning as the incorruptible guarantor of truth (*teisybė*).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic myth and folklore portray Perkūnas Tikras as a majestic, vigorous warrior deity:

* **Majestic Copper-Bearded Warrior**: Depicted as a tall, imposing mature warrior with a fiery copper/red beard (*rudabarzdis*), blazing eyes, and radiant silver mail.
* **Fiery Chariot and Mounts**: Traverses the storm clouds in a rumbling two-wheeled copper or iron chariot drawn by fiery he-goats (*Perkūno ožiai*) or swift stallions.
* **Divine Weapons of Celestial Fire**: Brandishes a razor-sharp stone or flint axe (*Perkūno kirvulis*), fiery lightning arrows, a golden sword, and a devastating celestial hammer.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred geography of Perkūnas Tikras spans ancient oak groves, elevated sanctuaries, and lightning-cleansed waters:

* **Sacred Oak Hills and Sanctuaries**: Ancient elevated hilltops crowned by lightning-struck oak trees (*Perkūnkalniai*), such as Romowe in Nadruvia, Rambynas Hill, and the Šventaragis sanctuary in Vilnius.
* **Cosmic Crossroads and Mountain Summits**: High atmospheric realms where storm clouds gather and celestial thunder reverberates.
* **Lightning-Struck Boulders**: Enormous glacial erratics shattered by divine bolts, revered as sources of protective healing energy.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic tradition sustained rich sacrificial customs honoring the True Thunderer:

* **Perpetual Oak Fires (*Amžinoji ugnis*)**: Burning sacred oak logs tended day and night in elevated sanctuaries to maintain communion with the god.
* **The Sacrificial He-Goat Feast (*Ožio aukojimas*)**: Communal sacrifice of a black or red he-goat under an ancient oak during spring awakening or intense droughts.
* **Touching Earth and Stone**: Peasants touched the freshly awakened soil and bit a stone upon hearing the first spring thunder to receive lifelong health and physical vigor.
