## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Laima is a central Baltic goddess of fate, luck, childbirth, marriage, and the allotted human lifespan. Her name rests on the Baltic word *laimė* / *laime* (fortune, destiny, blessing); scholarly etymology further connects it with a *laid-me* formation from *leisti* / *laist* (“to let, allot, create”), rather than a folk link to *lieti* alone.

Written sources place her firmly in the seventeenth century:
- **1649 Paul Einhorn (*Historia Lettica*)**: Attests Latvian fate and fortune belief in the Livonian ethnographic record.
- **1666 Martini / Klein hymnbook tradition**: First clear written Lithuanian mention of *Laimele*.
- **Late seventeenth century Matthäus Praetorius (*Deliciae Prussicae*, finished about 1690)**: Describes Prussian–Lithuanian women’s birth rites addressed to *Layme*, including a post-birth table meal and the selection of a living hen as offering.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Laima governs the course of a human life from the birth threshold onward: she allots lifespan and fortune, blesses marriage and household success, and seals a destiny that folk belief treats as unalterable once spoken. Lithuanian tradition stresses spinning and weaving as the code of fate-making; Latvian *dainas* more often say that Laima *writes* or *lays* a life (*mūžu likt* / *mūžu raksta*) and may braid a crown of fortune.

**Relations:**
- **Dalia** (LT): companion allotting personal share of earthly goods beside Laima’s broader life-course.
- **Giltinė** (LT): sister and counterpart at death; folklore also seats the sisters riding the same horse.
- **Dēkla and Kārta** (LV): fate companions forming a comparative triad with Laima (rarely all three in one *daina*).
- **Veļu māte** (LV): antagonist who cuts short what Laima has set, rocking or untying the cradle of life.
- **Cuckoo (*gegutė* / *dzeguze*)**: ornitomorphic form and address; Lithuanian belief hears her call as a prophecy of remaining years or marriage fortune.
- **Hen**: zoomorphic offering-form bound to birth rites.
- **Linden (*liepa*)**: sacred tree and dwelling form.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folk tradition shows Laima as a woman who may appear young or as a kindly *māmiņa* / mother figure:
- **Looks**: Loose hair (*vaļējiem matiem*) in Latvian depiction; age shifts between youthful beauty and an old woman at the birthbed.
- **Clothing & footwear**: Velvet skirt (*samta svārki*); gold shoes (*zelta kurpes*) on festive appearances; silk cloth appears in birth bedding rather than as a fixed silk gown.
- **Accessories & tools**: Keys; a mythic belt (*Laimos juosta*, also linked with the rainbow); midwife broom (*Laimas slotiņa*) of linden, birch, or fir; knife or related birth-helper tools in Latvian attribute lists.
- **Transport**: Horse shared with Giltinė in Lithuanian lore; Latvian *Laimas rati* (cart) and, in Latgale, a silver-boat motif.
- **Forms**: Anthropomorphic woman; cuckoo; hen; full linden; snakes (*žalčiai*) kept for her favour in Lithuanian household belief.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Laima’s cult belongs to domestic and liminal female space as much as to outdoor sacred geography:
- **Bathhouse (*pirtis* / *pirts*)**: Primary birth sanctuary where offerings meet her at the threshold.
- **Sacred lindens and household corners**: Linden crowns and the home-fortune seat under the threshold / *krikštasuolė*.
- **Laima footprint stones (Lithuania)**: Stones marked as *Laimos pėdos*, where offerings of bread, wool, flax, and money were left.
- **Fate springs (Latvia)**: Local springs named for Laima in folk geography (distinct from general Turaida/Sigulda tourist cult sites).

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Birth, marriage, and household fortune frame her rites:
- **Post-birth table feast**: After delivery, women prepare a ritual table meal with bread, drink (often ale), and a cooked hen offered in thanks for mother and child.
- **Bathhouse delivery**: Birth takes place in the *pirtis*; midwives and mothers invoke Laima, leave offerings at the entrance, and bathe the newborn in *Laimos vanduo* (first bath water treated as sacred).
- **Hen offering**: A living hen is chosen and killed for Laima (wooden ladle *samtis* in Lithuanian midwife practice; colour bans against red birds appear in ethnographic notes).
- **Laimas slotiņa**: Linden, birch, or fir twigs kept as broom-amulets of health and blessing, especially in Latvian tradition.
- **Stone and spring offerings**: Bread, wool, flax, coins, and woven bands (*prievītiņa*) left at footprint stones and local Laima springs.
- **Dainas**: Latvian song tradition addresses Laima with exceptional frequency as *Laimiņa* / *māmuliņa*.
