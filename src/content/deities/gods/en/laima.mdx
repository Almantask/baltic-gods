## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Laima is the supreme Baltic goddess of fate, luck, childbirth, weaving, and lifespan. Her name derives from the Baltic word *laimė* (fortune, destiny, blessing), linked to the verb *lieti* / *laimėti* (to receive one's allotted portion). Historical accounts document Laima's cult in Matthäus Praetorius' 1690 *Deliciae Prussicae*, which describes ritual feasts (*Laimos stalas*) organized by mothers after childbirth. 17th-century records describe women sacrificing a hen or rooster to Laima in sacred bathhouses (*pirtis*).

In academic research by Marija Gimbutas, Algirdas Julius Greimas, and Haralds Biezais, Laima is identified as the paramount dispenser of human destiny, working alongside Dalia (lot) and Giltinė (death) from birth to death.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Laima's domain encompasses human birth, marriage, fate weaving, and lifespan allocation. In Baltic cosmology, she is envisioned sitting beneath a sacred linden or oak tree beside a spring, spinning golden threads of human destiny. At the moment of birth, Laima pronounces the child's unalterable destiny (*dalia*).

She is closely associated with the cuckoo bird: in spring, the first call of the cuckoo is interpreted as Laima's omen announcing remaining lifespan, marriage prospects, or economic fortune.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Rituals dedicated to Laima centered around childbirth, marriage, and domestic blessings:

- **Laima's Table (Laimos stalas)**: Following childbirth, women prepared a ritual table in the bathhouse or home laden with bread, cheese, ale, and a cooked hen or rooster in gratitude to Laima.
- **Bathhouse Delivery Rites**: Birth took place in the bathhouse (*pirtis*), a sacred female sanctuary where midwives invoked Laima to ease labor and protect the infant from dark spirits.
- **Linden Twig Amulets**: Sprigs of linden or fir (called Laima's brooms) were kept in homes and bathhouses as symbols of fertility and health.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

In Baltic folklore (thousands of Latvian *Dainas* and Lithuanian songs), Laima walks in silver shoes weaving silk threads of destiny.

In Lithuanian folk tradition:
> "Mother Laima, gentle weaver,  
> Spin the threads of my life,  
> Weave good fortune and blessing  
> Into my rue wreath."

In Latvian *Dainas*:
> "Laima walked along the hill,  
> Wearing a silk gown.  
> May luck be wherever I wander,  
> When Laima walks by my side."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Laima's cult is bound to sacred linden trees, springs, and bathhouses. Turaida Sacred Spring in Sigulda (Latvia) and Laima Stones in Lithuania remain venerated sites where offerings of flowers and woven ribbons are made to this day.
