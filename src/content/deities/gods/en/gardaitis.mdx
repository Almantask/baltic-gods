## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Gardaitis (authentically recorded in 16th-century Old Prussian and Lithuanian sources as *Bardoayts*, *Bardoyts*, or *Gardiun*, "the God of Ships / Guardian of Sailors") is the Baltic maritime deity presiding over sailing vessels, boats, coastal navigation, fishermen, and seafaring crews. 

Historical chronicles and critical philological studies record the authentic teonymic lineage:

* **The Sudovian Book (*Sudauer Büchlein*, c. 1520–1530)**: Provides the earliest authentic documentation of the teonym in Old Prussian, recording *Bardoayts dei schyffe Gott* ("Bardoayts, the god of ships"), who was invoked by coastal fishermen and navigators in the Sambian and Curonian regions.
* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum...*, 1582; publ. 1615)**: Recorded *Gardiun* as the protector of sailors and boats (*Gardiun pro nautis*), representing the Samogitian and Curonian continuation of the maritime cult.
* **Matthäus Praetorius (*Deliciae Prussicae*, c. 1690)**: Described Prussian and Curonian coastal offerings made before launching fishing boats into the Baltic Sea and the Curonian Lagoon.
* **Etymological and Philological Analysis**: Kazimieras Būga critically evaluated the forms *Bardoayts* and *Gardaitis*. While early scholars attempted to derive the teonym from an alleged root *\*garda* ("boat"), modern Baltic linguistics (Būga, Toporov, Mažiulis, Vėlius) establishes that *Bardoayts* likely derives from Baltic *\*barda* ("beard", denoting a bearded maritime elder), while Łasicki's *Gardiun* represents a scribal corruption or secondary derivative related to enclosed harbors (*gardas* "enclosure, pen, sheltered harbor cove").

The authentic history of Gardaitis / Bardaitis is thoroughly analysed by Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, Vol. 2), Vladimir Toporov (*Prusskij jazyk: Slovar'*, Vol. 1), Vytautas Ališauskas (*Jono Lasickio pasakojimas apie žemaičių dievus*, 2006), and the *Visuotinė lietuvių enciklopedija*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic coastal cosmology, seafaring across the Baltic Sea and the Curonian Lagoon was an unpredictable and dangerous endeavor fraught with sudden storms, shifting sandbanks, and treacherous currents.

### Sovereign of Ships, Coastal Navigation, and Seafaring Crews

* **Protector of Sailing Vessels (*Laivų globėjas*)**: Safeguards fishing boats, clinker-built wooden barques, and seafaring vessels from shipwrecks, structural fractures, and capsizing.
* **Master of Favorable Coastal Winds**: Calms turbulent waves and guides offshore sea breezes to fill sails and speed vessels toward shore.
* **Guardian of Fishermen and Seafarers**: Protects coastal fishermen against drowning and blesses fishing nets with plentiful catches of Baltic cod, flounder, and herring.
* **Harbor Sanctuary Genius**: Presides over natural coves, river mouths, and sheltered sandspits where boats seek haven during storms.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic maritime folklore and historical descriptions portray Gardaitis / Bardaitis through weathered nautical iconography:

* **Bearded Maritime Elder (*Bardoayts*)**: Conceived as a rugged, sea-weathered elder with a foaming white beard, salt-crusted hair, and piercing blue-grey eyes.
* **Steering Oar and Hemp Ropes**: Carries a massive carved wooden steering oar (*vairas / irklas*) and coils of strong Baltic hemp mooring rope.
* **Ship Prow Emblem**: Often appears standing at the carved wooden prow of a Baltic sailing vessel navigating cresting sea foam.
* **Seal-Skin Mantle and Amber Amulet**: Clothed in a waterproof seal-skin cloak and wearing a raw coastal amber pendant carved with ship markings.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Gardaitis is situated along the Baltic coastline and lagoon waters:

* **Curonian Lagoon and Spit (*Kuršių marios / Kuršių nerija*)**: The shallow, wind-swept waters and sand dunes where Baltic fishermen set nets.
* **Sambia Peninsula Coast (*Semba / Prūsijos pajūris*)**: The amber-rich coastal cliffs and natural harbors where Bardoayts was historically invoked.
* **Historic Harbor Mouths (Klaipėda / Memel & Venta)**: The strategic river mouths connecting mainland trade routes to the open Baltic Sea.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic fishermen and maritime travelers observed dedicated pre-voyage rituals:

* **First-Catch Fish Offering**: Casting the first captured fish of the season back into the waves as an unblemished offering to the sea spirit.
* **Pre-Departure Boat Consecration**: Pouring a libation of beer or fresh spring water over the ship's prow before pushing the vessel into the surf.
* **Miniature Boat Votives**: Carving small wooden boat models and placing them upon coastal shrine stones in thanksgiving for surviving a violent sea storm.
* **Silence on the Water Taboo**: Maintaining strict discipline on board, avoiding swearing or speaking ill of the sea, which was believed to provoke sudden squalls.
