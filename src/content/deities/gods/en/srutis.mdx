## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Srutis (in historical chronicles – *Srutis*, *Srutim*) represents the Baltic numen of the river current, flowing waters, hydrodynamic force, and stream movement.

Historical chronicles and comparative Baltic linguistics document the following foundations:

* **16th-Century Chronicle Attestation**: Documented in Jan Łasicki's treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (composed c. 1582, published Basel 1615, reprinted in BRMŠ Vol. 2, pp. 576, 592, 604): *"Srutim pro flumine"* ("Srutis for the river current / flowing stream"). Later analyzed in the historical commentaries of Vytautas Ališauskas (*Jono Lasickio pasakojimas apie žemaičių dievus*, 2012).
* **Etymology & Hydrological Semantics**: Derived from Proto-Baltic *\*sru-t-* ("to flow, stream, gush", Lithuanian *srūti*, *srovė*, *srautas*, *sruta*, Latvian *straume*, *sraut*, Old Prussian *pra-sreit*, from PIE *\*srew-* "to flow"). Semantically signifies the dynamic velocity and moving agency of water, distinguishing the active stream from standing waters.
* **Comparative Baltic Context**: In Latvian folklore and Baltic hydrological ecology, stream velocity and flowing water spirits are associated with **Ūdens māte** (Mother of Waters), **Upes māte** (Mother of the River), and the veneration of living mill streams (*dzīvais ūdens*, LD 30842-0, LD 30855-0).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmological thought, flowing water is an active living entity endowed with will and animating energy:

### Numen of River Rapids, Hydrodynamic Force, and Mill Watercourses

* **The Animating Force of River Currents**: Personifies the directional kinetic power of running water—the current itself that propels vessels, drives mill wheels, and cleanses the landscape.
* **Allied Pantheon Relationships**: Operates within the Baltic aquatic hierarchy alongside **Upinis** (lord of rivers and riverbeds), **Ežerinis** (guardian of lakes and deep waters), **Bangpūtys** (master of storm waves and coastal winds), and **Jūras māte** (mother of the open sea).
* **Protector of Fords and Millers**: Governed the vital boundary where human industry met the raw force of water, determining whether a ford was passable or a watermill ran smoothly.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folk tradition and aquatic animism envision Srutis through dynamic, translucent, and riverine imagery:

* **Agile Spirit of the Rapids**: Depicted as a swift, fluid male figure with surging, foam-white hair resembling rushing river spray, and translucent greenish-blue eyes reflecting deep whirlpools.
* **Riverine Raiment and Attributes**: Clad in garments woven from water reeds and river grasses (*meldai*), holding a carved wooden watermill paddle and a polished river stone.
* **Hydrodynamic Symbolism**: Associated with river rapids (*rėvos*), whirlpools (*sūkuriai*), water foam (*putos*), and swift migratory fish.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

Srutis's sacred realm encompasses running rivers, mill races, rapids, and traditional river fords:

* **River Rapids and Swirling Waters**: Rocky stretches of fast-flowing streams and cascades where the force of the current is most concentrated.
* **Mill Races and Watermill Dams**: Wooden mill flumes, waterwheel channels, and weir gates where water power is harnessed for grain milling.
* **Sacred River Fords (*Brastos*)**: Ancient crossing points over Lithuanian and Latvian rivers where travelers negotiated safe passage across the current.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Millers, fishermen, ferrymen, and travelers observed dedicated offerings to appease and honor the river current:

* **Wreath and Bread Offerings**: Ferrymen and travelers tossed small woven herb wreaths, slices of rye bread, or silver coins into the current upstream before navigating dangerous rapids.
* **Miller's First-Flour Libation**: Mill owners dedicated a measure of freshly ground first flour or poured beer into the mill race, seeking smooth wheel rotation and protection against spring flood washouts.
* **Reverence for River Agency**: Strict folk prohibitions forbade contaminating fast-flowing water or shouting insolently at river rapids, acknowledging the living will of the stream.
