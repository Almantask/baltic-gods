## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Kauriraris (recorded in sixteenth-century primary chronicles as *Chaurirari*) is a historical Lithuanian deity associated with horse breeding and wartime protection.

Historical sources and comparative philological research document the authentic chronicle lineage:

* **Primary Sixteenth-Century Chronicle**: Recorded by Maciej Stryjkowski in *Kronika Polska, Litewska, Żmódzka i wszystkiej Rusi* (Königsberg, 1582, Book 1, Chapter 4) as *Chaurirari*, identified as both a horse god (*koński bóg*) and a deity invoked for peace and defence against enemies during war (*bóg wojny*).
* **Comparative Linguistic Analyses**: Analyzed in Baltic linguistics (Kazimieras Būga, Vladimir Toporov, Norbertas Vėlius, Rolandas Kregždys). While earlier scholars attempted derivations from *kauras* ("fur, sorrel horse-coat") and agent suffix *-aris*, contemporary mytho-linguistic research categorizes the name as an early modern chronicle transcription reflecting localized domestic religious vocabulary.
* **Domestic Equine Cult Context**: Documented within early modern Lithuanian peasant traditions combining equine husbandry with hearthside household rites.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In sixteenth-century Baltic folk religion, Kauriraris occupies a unique intersection of domestic horse care and wartime defence:

### Guardian of Horse Multiplication and Wartime Peace

* **Patron of Equine Proliferation (*Koński bóg*)**: Invoked by horse owners and breeders to ensure the healthy breeding, vigor, and diverse coat colors of farmstead horses.
* **Protector in Wartime (*Wartime Defense and Peace*)**: Propitiated during times of military threat and conflict to seek protection and peace (*pokój od nieprzyjaciół*) from invading enemies.
* **Integration of Equine and Hearth Spheres**: Connects the outside world of horses, travel, and military defense directly to the domestic sanctuary behind the family hearth.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Sixteenth-century chronicle testimony describes the ritual material culture associated with Kauriraris:

* **The Rider's Saddle (*Siodło*)**: Symbolized by leather horse saddles brought indoors and placed behind the hearth for ceremonial seating during prayers.
* **Vigorous Roosters of Diverse Plumes**: Visualized through strong, healthy cockerels of varied colors (*pietuchy rosłe, czerstwe, różnej barwy*) chosen to mirror the desired coat varieties of thriving horses.
* **Hearthside Warmth**: Associated with the domestic clay stove where household rites were enacted.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Kauriraris centers on the domestic and equestrian areas of the Lithuanian farmstead:

* **Behind the Domestic Hearth (*Užkrosnis / Za piecem*)**: The private domestic area behind the masonry stove where worshippers conducted their rituals.
* **Stables and Horse Paddock Enclosures**: The farmstead paddocks where riding and draft horses were reared and sheltered.
* **Historical Farmsteads of Lithuania and Samogitia**: Rural communities where horse breeding was vital for both agriculture and regional defense.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Maciej Stryjkowski's 1582 chronicle provides explicit details of the historical ritual practice:

* **Saddle Seating Ceremony**: Peasant worshippers sat directly upon horse saddles placed behind the household stove while reciting prayers and offering petitions.
* **Rooster Sacrifices**: Sacrificing vigorous, healthy roosters of diverse colors to ensure a corresponding multiplication of strong horses.
* **Supplications for Peace**: Offering prayers during periods of military threat, petitioning for safety, victory, and peace from hostile forces.
