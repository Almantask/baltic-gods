## 1. Etymology & Historical Chronicles

Praamžius (also attested as *Pramžius*, *Praamžis*, *Praamžimas*, and linguistically related to *Prakorimas*) is the supreme Lithuanian theonym and epithet for the primordial celestial creator. Etymologically, the name is a transparent Baltic compound formed from the prefix *pra-* ("before, pre-, primordial") and the nominal stem *amžius* / *amžis* ("age, century, epoch, eternity"), signifying "The Primordial One" or "He Who Existed Before All Ages."

The figure of Praamžius became widely known through nineteenth-century historiography and Lithuanian mythological folklore. Teodor Narbutt in his 1835 work *Mitologia litewska* integrated *Pramžimas* into a structured pantheon as the supreme cosmic creator and primeval first cause. Comparative mythologists—including Algirdas Julius Greimas (*Apie dievus ir žmones*, 1979), Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, 1996), and Gintaras Beresnevičius (2004)—linked Praamžius to Maciej Stryjkowski's sixteenth-century chronicle record of *Prokorimos* (*Prakorimas*, "the very first god") and authentic Lithuanian flood folktales (*etiologinės sakmės apie tvaną*). In Latvian tradition, the corresponding celestial creator is revered as *Dievs*, *Debesu tēvs*, or *Vecais tēvs*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, Praamžius represents the primal architect of the universe who established the unalterable cosmic laws, celestial cycles, and moral order. He personifies the transcendent, eternal dimension of the supreme sky god, observing humanity and maintaining universal justice.

### The Great Deluge (*Didysis Tvanas*)

The core mythic narrative associated with Praamžius is the Baltic creation and deluge cycle:

* **Cleansing the Earth**: Distressed by human wickedness and ceaseless warfare, Praamžius unleashed the elemental giants of Wind (*Vėjas*) and Water (*Vanduo*) for twenty days and twenty nights to submerge the world.
* **The Celestial Nutshell**: Looking down from the heavenly window while eating hazelnuts from the celestial garden, Praamžius took pity on the drowning beings and dropped a nutshell (*riešuto kevalas*) onto the highest mountain peak, sheltering the righteous elderly couple and animal pairs.
* **The Rainbow Covenant**: After the flood subsided, Praamžius arched the cosmic rainbow (*Linksminė* / *Vaivorykštė*, the "comforter") across the sky and instructed the surviving couple to scatter stones behind them to bring forth new generations of people.

### Divine & Natural Relations

* **Dievas & Okopirmus**: Functions as an aspect of *Dievas*, matching the Old Prussian supreme sky creator *Okopirmus* ("The All-First").
* **Perkūnas & Žemyna**: Sovereign celestial authority operating above thunder and atmospheric weather (*Perkūnas*) and the fertile earth (*Žemyna*).
* **Flora & Fauna**: Associated with ancient sacred oaks (*ąžuolai*), the celestial hazelnut (*riešutas*), and the luminous rainbow.

## 3. Character & Visuals

In Lithuanian folklore and traditional narratives, the celestial creator manifests with distinct paternal iconography:

* **Appearance**: Portrayed as *Dievas Senelis*—a majestic, venerable celestial elder with long silver or white hair and beard (*žilas senelis*), embodying wisdom, dignity, and cosmic calm.
* **Wearables**: Dressed in simple, radiant white linen garments (*balti lininiai drabužiai*) or a modest grey woolen traveler's coat (*pilka sermėgėlė / pelēks mētelītis*), walking across peasant fields to test hospitality and righteousness.
* **Attributes**: A traveler's wooden staff (*ramstis / lazda*), the celestial nutshell of salvation, and the arching rainbow banner.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred sphere of Praamžius encompasses the highest cosmological and landscape elevations:

* **Celestial Sky Vault (*Dangaus Skliautas*)**: The upper dome of heaven and the celestial garden where the creator observes the cosmos through the heavenly window.
* **Sacred Altar Hills (*Alkakalniai*)**: Prominent high hilltops across Lithuania, such as Rambynas Hill on the Nemunas River, where prayers were directed toward the open sky.
* **Sacred Oak Groves (*Alkai*)**: Ancient oak sanctuaries where communities gathered for communal assemblies and sky invocations.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Practices

Reverence for the supreme sky creator in Baltic ethnic religion was integrated into seasonal prayers and moral observances:

* **Sunrise & Sky Invocations**: Prayers and greetings directed toward the rising sun, the morning sky, and the dome of heaven to express gratitude for cosmic harmony (*darna*).
* **Ritual Libations (*Nuliejimai*)**: Ceremonial libations of home-brewed mead, barley beer, and fresh spring water poured upon the earth before feasts and at sacred oak sanctuaries.
* **Harvest First-Fruits**: Presenting the first grains and honey of the season in reverence of the divine world order.
