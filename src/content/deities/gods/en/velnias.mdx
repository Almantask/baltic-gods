## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Velnias (also *Velinas*; Latvian *Velns*) is the Baltic chthonic figure of the underworld, the dead, moisture, cattle association, hidden wealth, and magic. The name belongs to the Proto-Baltic *\*vel-* cluster linked with the souls of the dead (Lithuanian *vėlė*, Latvian *velis* / *veļi*); comparative Indo-European parallels include Slavic Veles and, more loosely, the Old Norse *Val-* of Valhalla.

Old Prussian underworld gods *Patulas* / *Pikulas* (*Peckols*) occupy an analogous chthonic rank in Prussian sources; they are not simply alternate spellings of the Lithuanian theonym. The Wolfenbüttel Postil (1573) already records *velinas*. After Christianization the name was equated with the devil (*šėtonas* / Satan), while folklore kept an older chthonic and trickster profile.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology Velnias belongs to the underworld and to wet, low places: bogs, marshes, waters, caves, and spaces under stones. He guards water and cursed buried treasures, is linked with the world of the dead, and in Lithuanian tradition is also called a bog spirit and a smith. He grants extraordinary powers in some legends and appears as a cunning yet often outwitted trickster who builds bridges, moves great stones, and digs lakes or dams overnight.

The Perkūnas–Velnias pursuit is treated as the Indo-European “basic myth” axis in Baltic religion: the thunder god hunts his chthonic opponent with lightning. Latvian belief likewise records that Pērkons seeks and strikes Velns. Cosmogonic tales also pair God and Velnias creating land from mud brought up from the waters.

**Relations:** Perkūnas / Pērkons (celestial rival and pursuer); *vėlės* / *veļi* (etymological and underworld link to the dead); livestock (Lithuanian tradition: cattle protector; Latvian folklore often casts Velns as cattle thief rather than guardian); shapeshift forms and animal traits — cat, fox, bull, horse, snake/žaltys, birds (crow, raven, duck), and other beasts; rooster as adversary that drives him underground at dawn.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folklore presents Velnias as a half-human, half-animal figure and as a fully human disguise:
- **Looks**: Black skin / dark complexion; horns (often goat-like); horse or mixed animal feet / hooves; tail (cow or goat); fiery or glowing eyes; sometimes one eye in the forehead (Latvian belief) or a human face with a single nostril (Lithuanian belief); goat beard in Latvian descriptions; may appear as a small man in a green cap.
- **Clothing**: Well-dressed gentleman or manor lord — hat, fine boots, coat (including red German-fashion coats in Latvian belief).
- **Accessories & weapons**: Spear and noose in Lithuanian tradition; thick iron staff in Latvian belief.
- **Zoomorphic forms**: Cat (including black cat), fox, hedgehog, bull, horse, snake or grass snake, crow/raven/duck, and other animals; footprints left in stone.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Velnias is tied to liminal and chthonic landscape across Lithuania and Latvia:
- **Sacred geography**: Forests, marshes, bogs, caves, crossroads, mills, whirlpools, and stones.
- **Velnio duobė (near Aukštadvaris, Lithuania)**: A deep funnel-shaped hollow with devil legends and a sacralized place-name.
- **Latvian Velna- toponyms**: Velnezers, Velna ala, Velna purvs, and related caves, stones, and waters.
- **Devil stones and bridges**: *Velnio akmenys* / *Velna akmeņi* and bridge legends (including “devil’s bridge” motifs).
- **Velnių muziejus (Kaunas)**: The Devil Museum, a modern cultural collection of devil imagery.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Folk practice around Velnias centers on placation, avoidance, and landscape offerings rather than a single calendar feast in his name:
- **Place offerings**: Food and money left at devil stones, caves, and related sites; crossroads as meeting places at midnight.
- **Užgavėnės carnival**: Masked processions may include a devil (*velnias*) among other figures — a later carnival role beside older animal masks.
- **Taboos & apotropaics**: Activity mainly after sunset; the rooster’s crow drives him back underground; running water or bridges can break pursuit; Christian crosses and related charms repel him in later belief.
