## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Velnias (also *Velinas*, Latvian *Velns*, Old Prussian *Pikuls* / *Peckols*) is the ancient Baltic god of the underworld, the dead, cattle, crafts, music, and magic, later demonized during Christianization. His name originates from the Proto-Baltic root *\*vel-* (death, shade, underworld; cf. Old Norse *Valhalla*, Lithuanian *vėlė*). Unlike the Christian devil, Velnias was originally a chthonic deity associated with subterranean waters, hidden treasures, sacred boulders, and forest wildlife.

In Norbertas Vėlius' seminal work *Velinė* (1987) and studies by Algirdas Julius Greimas, the fundamental cosmic duality between Perkūnas (sky/order) and Velnias (earth/underworld) is established as the core structure of Baltic mythology.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, Velnias rules the underworld (*Vełyba* / *Veļi*), moisture, bogs, stones, and waters. He is the guardian of livestock (especially horses and horned cattle), granter of earthly wealth, master of cunning, blacksmithing, and music. He often manifests as a one-eyed or lame old man with small horns and hooves, capable of shapeshifting into a black dog, goat, cat, or stone.

The eternal duel between Perkūnas and Velnias symbolizes the dynamic equilibrium between sky (fire, drought, rain) and earth (water, mud, moisture). When Velnias steals Perkūnas' cattle or bride, Perkūnas pursues him with thunderbolts until the stolen fertility is returned and the sky releases rain.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Veneration and appeasement of Velnias occurred during autumn ancestral rites (Vėlinės) and winter carnival (Užgavėnės):

- **Vėlinės Ancestral Feasts**: In late autumn, Velnias was honored as shepherd of ancestral souls (*vėlės*). Families prepared graveside and home banquets to welcome and feed the deceased.
- **Crossroad and Boulder Offerings**: Offerings to Velnias (bread, ale, coins) were deposited at crossroads, near sacred devil stones (*Velnio akmenys*), or in deep forest swamps.
- **Patron of Fiddlers and Blacksmiths**: Musicians and smiths seeking extraordinary skill were said to visit crossroads at midnight to bargain with Velinas for mastery over tunes and metalwork.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Lithuanian and Latvian folklore contains thousands of folktales featuring Velnias. He is portrayed not as pure evil, but as a trickster figure—cunning yet outwitted by clever peasants, capable of building massive stone dikes, hurling giant boulders, or digging lakes overnight.

Stories recount *Velnias' weddings*, midnight dances at crossroads, and fiddle duels. Jonas Basanavičius collected hundreds of tales cementing Velnias as one of the most vibrant figures in Baltic oral narrative.

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Hundreds of natural sites across Lithuania and Latvia bear his name: the Devil’s Pit (*Velnio duobė*) in Aukštadvaris, Devil’s Castle Boulders, Velnezers in Latvia, and Devil’s Bridges. Velnias remains the principal masked character in Užgavėnės (Shrovetide) carnival processions. Kaunas houses the world’s only Devil Museum (*Velnių muziejus*).
