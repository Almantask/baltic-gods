## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

The fermentation household complex behind this entry is attested under several related theonyms. The core name-family is built on Lithuanian *raugas* / *rūgimas* — sourdough starter and the act of fermenting.

Jan Łasicki’s Samogitian treatise *De diis Samagitarum* (published 1615) records *Rauguzemapati* / Raugų Žemėpatis. Maciej Stryjkowski’s chronicle (1582) lists *Ruguczis* / Rūgutis as the god of sour and fermented foods. In the late seventeenth century Matthäus Prätorius describes *Raugupatis* as the power that makes beer ferment well and dough rise. The popular form *Ragutis* enters the printed pantheon in Teodor Narbutt’s nineteenth-century list as a patron of mead-makers, tree-beekeepers, brewers, distillers, and feast-goers, and is read in modern scholarship as a reworking of the earlier Rūgutis / Raugupatis line.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

This is a household fermentation deity: the quality and taste of bread and other soured foods, beer, and mead depend on him. When brewing “walks” well and dough rises, the cult name Raugupatis is the power credited for that success. The figure belongs with the domestic gods of the homestead and kin group — the same sphere in which a bride carries *rūgštis* (sourdough and beer starter) from her natal home into the husband’s household, joining the families’ living ferments as she joins their hearth fire.

**Relations:** Gabija (household-fire goddess; first baked loaf offered to her as the first draught of beer or mead is offered to Raugų Žemėpatis); speckled / mottled young hens (Stryjkowski’s sacrificial birds for Rūgutis); Latvian *Rauga māte* (functional brewing/fermentation parallel — overseer of hopped barley-malt wort — not the same theonym).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Cult identity is carried by fermentation itself and by the sacrificial birds:

- **Sacred signs**: Rising dough; fermenting beer; the first draught from the newly opened barrel.
- **Offering animal**: Speckled young hens (*raibos vištytės*).

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

The cult space is the farmstead kitchen and brew-house — the domestic zone of dough tubs, beer barrels, and family *rūgštis* — within the Lithuanian / Samogitian world named by the sixteenth- and seventeenth-century lists.

- **Samogitia (Žemaitija)**: Chronicle frame of Łasicki’s *De diis Samagitarum* for *Rauguzemapati*.
- **Lithuanian homestead**: Bread-sour and ale-starter kept and transferred with the bride as living household property.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

- **First draught (*Nulaidimos*)**: The first sip of beer or mead drawn from the barrel is offered to Raugų Žemėpatis, as the first loaf is offered to Gabija.
- **Father’s first jug**: The first ale jug dedicated to Raugpatis is drunk by the household father (or, in his absence, the mother).
- **Speckled hens**: Stryjkowski records offering mottled young hens to Rūgutis.
- **Bridal *rūgštis***: A bride carries bread-sour and beer-sour from her parents’ house and joins them to the husband’s family’s ferments, alongside the joining of hearth fires.
