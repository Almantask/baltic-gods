## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Pagirinis (also recorded as *Pagirnis*) is the Baltic domestic and chthonic guardian spirit dwelling beneath the household quern stone (*girnos*).

Sixteenth- and seventeenth-century historical sources and Baltic linguistic research document this tradition:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicle Entry**: Documented in Jan Łasicki's treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (composed c. 1582, published Basel 1615) with the succinct gloss: *Pagirnis pro mola* ("Pagirnis for the handmill").
* **Seventeenth-Century Jesuit Reports**: Attested in the *Annuae Litterae Societatis Jesu an. 1605* (Vilnius Jesuit College annual reports, reprinted in BRMŠ Vol. 2) recording rural Lithuanian and Samogitian veneration of guardian spirits and sacred grass snakes inhabiting the cavity beneath domestic quern stones.
* **Baltic Linguistic Etymology**: Formed from the Lithuanian locative prefix *pa-* ("under, beneath") combined with the noun *girnos* ("quern stones, handmill") and the suffix *-inis*, literally denoting "the one dwelling beneath the quern stone" (*būvantysis po girnomis*).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic domestic religion, Pagirinis safeguards the vital transformation of raw grain into edible flour:

### Guardian of Grinding, Household Flour, and Domestic Well-Being

* **Patron of the Handmill**: Protects the mechanical integrity of the stone quern, preventing the upper grinding stone from jamming, fracturing, or producing bitter grist.
* **Theriomorphic Manifestation as Žaltys**: Frequently embodied as the revered household grass snake (*žaltys*) residing in the warm, secluded hollow beneath the quern platform.
* **Domestic Pantheon Integration**: Operates alongside Dimstipatis (master of the homestead), Gabija (guardian of hearth fire), and Žemėpatis (lord of earth and livestock), safeguarding bread production from malicious spirits.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic household lore visualizes the sacred quern guardian through domestic and zoomorphic elements:

* **The Quern Stone (*Girnos*)**: Centered upon the paired heavy granite grinding stones and oak milling bench.
* **The Sacred Grass Snake (*Žaltys*)**: Manifested as a sleek, non-venomous grass snake with yellow or amber ear-spots.
* **Flour Dust and Milk Bowl**: Represented by fine rye flour dust and earthenware bowls filled with fresh unskimmed milk.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Pagirinis is located entirely within the domestic interior:

* **The Quern Corner (*Priegirnis / Girnų kampas*)**: The designated milling alcove situated near the hearth in traditional Samogitian and Aukštaitian farmsteads.
* **Under-Stone Cavities**: The dry earthen foundation space beneath the heavy timber quern post.
* **Historic Samogitian Homesteads**: Rural domestic complexes where 16th-century historical chronicles and Jesuit reports recorded ongoing quern rites.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Historical chronicles and ethnographic archives record precise milling customs:

* **Flour and Milk Libations**: Offering the first handful of freshly ground flour and pouring fresh milk into an earthenware dish placed under the quern stones.
* **Bride's Quern Initiation (*Jaunamartės auka*)**: A newlywed bride entering her husband's homestead deposited a handwoven sash or bread slice under the quern to greet the resident spirit.
* **Milling Taboos**: Strict prohibitions against grinding grain on Sundays, festival days, or after sundown, and a taboo against rotating the upper stone counter-clockwise (*prieš saulę*).
