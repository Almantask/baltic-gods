## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Jānis is the Latvian deity and personification of the summer solstice. In Baltic tradition, the name represents an archaic pre-Christian solar and fertility godhead upon which the name and feast of Saint John the Baptist (*Ioannes* / *Sv. Jānis Kristītājs*) were historically layered. Philological studies also connect the underlying Baltic theonymic formulas with Proto-Indo-European roots denoting arrival, passage, and riding.

Early modern historical documentation begins in the seventeenth century:
- **1636 Paul Einhorn, *Reformatio gentis Letticae in Ducatu Curlandiae***: Records the persistence of midsummer festivities, night fires, herb gathering, and pagan singing in Courland.
- **1649 Paul Einhorn, *Historia Lettica***: Provides the earliest comprehensive chronicle account of the Latvian midsummer festival (*Johannis-Tag* / *Johannis-Fest*), detailing nocturnal ritual bonfires (*Jāņuguns*), botanical decoration, and singing with the sacred *Līgo* refrain.
- **Late nineteenth to early twentieth century Krišjānis Barons, *Latvju dainas* (Vol. 3)**: Catalogues over three thousand midsummer songs (*Jāņu dziesmas / līgotnes*), forming the foundation for subsequent scholarship.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Jānis embodies the summer solstice (*vasaras saulgrieži*), the longest day and shortest night of the year, marking the zenith of solar light and the reproductive peak of nature. Mythologically framed as the sacred wedding of the Sun and Earth (*Saules un Zemes kāzas*), his arrival marks the cosmic turning point of the solar year.

He arrives once a year to survey the country, ride over the hills, inspect grain fields, bless growing crops, and multiply cattle and dairy yield. In traditional dainas he is addressed as a heavenly guest and *Dieva dēls* ("Son of God"), descending from the celestial hill to earthly farmsteads.

**Relations:**
- **Dievs / Dieva dēli**: Grouped among the celestial Sons of God; shares the divine horseman motif, heavenly wedding attendance, and pastoral oversight with Ūsiņš.
- **Saule**: Solar counterpart; Jānis arrives at the zenith of the Sun Goddess, meeting Saule as she dances across the morning sky in tripartite crowns.
- **Pērkons**: The Thunder God is co-invoked during midsummer songs, bringing fertilizing rain and striking malevolent spirits who seek to blight the fields.
- **Māra and Laima**: Co-invoked during midsummer rites to guard livestock, fill milk pails, weave flower wreaths, and bestow domestic luck.
- **Jāņa bērni**: The wandering singers and celebrants who travel from farmstead to farmstead (*aplīgošana*) represent Jānis's sacred earthly retinue.
- **Steed (*kumeļš / zirgs*)**: Jānis rides a divine horse (described as golden, yellow, or dapple-grey) equipped with celestial trappings.
- **Rooster (*gailis*)**: Associated with dawn vigilance and the sunrise crowning the solstice night.

## 3. Character and Visuals

- **Physique and Bearing**: Conceived anthropomorphically as an idealized celestial youth or noble lord riding over hills and resting on summits to gaze across the fields.
- **Hair**: Described in traditional song formulas with radiant, golden hair adorned with green leaves.
- **Wearables**: Wears an oak-leaf wreath (*ozollapu vainags*) upon his head; wears three ritual coats—green, white, and golden-tinsel embroidered silk—or a grey traveler's coat sweeping through the dew.
- **Footwear**: High leather riding boots equipped with ringing golden spurs (*zelta pieši*).
- **Accessories & Mount Trappings**: Rides with a golden saddle and silver bridle; carries fresh leafy branches, a wooden beer pitcher (*alus kanna*), a round cheese wheel, or a golden sword.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Jānis's domain spans the entirety of the rural Latvian cultural landscape rather than an isolated sanctuary:

- **Hilltops and Summits**: High landscape elevations (such as Zilaiskalns and Gaiziņkalns) where communal bonfires are kindled to broadcast protective firelight across all visible fields.
- **River Valleys and Corridors**: Sacred waterways (such as the Gauja and Daugava valleys) where night fires, boat processions, and morning bathing occur.
- **Farmstead Thresholds**: Farm gates (*sētas vārti*), residential doorways, and cattle byres where green boughs are fastened to receive the visiting deity and his retinue.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

- **Jāņi Solstice Festival (June 23–24)**: Begins on *Zāļu diena* (Herb Day) and continues through the night until the sunrise of Jāņi Day.
- **Midsummer Bonfires (*Jāņuguns*)**: Ground fires on hills and elevated tar-barrel fires atop long poles (*pūdelis*) kept burning from sunset to dawn; leaping over embers purifies participants, wards off misfortune, and ensures health and fertility.
- **Gendered Wreath Customs**: Men wear oak-leaf wreaths (*ozollapu vainagi*) symbolizing solar stamina and virility; women weave floral wreaths (*pļavas ziedu vainagi*) from clover, chamomile, and cornflowers.
- **Botanical Shielding (*Zāļošana / Pušķošana*)**: Adorning buildings, gates, and cattle with birch boughs, calamus, oak, rowan, and nettles to bar entry to milk-stealing witches (*raganas*).
- **Sacred Foods**: Round golden caraway cheese (*Jāņu siers*), representing the sun disk, and freshly fermented barley beer (*Jāņu alus*), served as hospitality to all visiting singers (*jāņabērni*).
- **Līgo Songs (*Līgotnes*)**: Continuous night-long singing of traditional dainas characterized by the *Līgo* refrain, conveying blessings and ritual dialogue.
- **Morning Dew Bathing (*Rīta rasa*)**: Washing the face or rolling in midsummer dawn dew before sunrise to secure health, clear skin, and vitality.
- **Quest for the Fern Blossom (*Papardes zieds*)**: Searching at midnight for the mythical luminous fern flower that confers wisdom, clairvoyance, fertility, and fortune.
