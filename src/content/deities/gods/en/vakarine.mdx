## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Vakarinė (Latvian *Vakara zvaigzne*) is the Baltic goddess of the evening star (the evening phase of Venus), sunset, dusk, and nighttime peace. Her name derives from *vakaras* (evening; cf. Latin *Vesper*, Greek *Hesperos*). She is documented in Jan Łasicki’s 1582 work *De Diis Samagitarum* alongside Aušrinė.

Mythological studies by Marija Gimbutas and Algirdas Julius Greimas portray Vakarinė and Aušrinė as twin celestial sisters opening and closing the solar gates of heaven.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Vakarinė governs dusk, twilight, home rest, and the reception of Saulė at sunset. She is depicted as a serene maiden spreading a dark velvet cloak across the evening sky.

In mythic tradition, Vakarinė welcomes mother Saulė as she descends into the western sea, bathing her golden steeds and preparing her couch for night rest.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Dusk rituals accompanied the end of daily labor:

- **Dusk Silence Rite**: Upon sighting the evening star, farmsteads ceased noisy chores, gathered indoors, and offered prayers of gratitude for the day's safety.
- **Night-pasture Invocations**: Herders invoked Vakarinė for a tranquil night over grazing livestock.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Folk songs describe Vakarinė making the bed for Saulė:
> "Vakarinė, evening star,  
> Rose softly in the west,  
> Preparing a velvet bed  
> For mother Saulė's rest."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Vakarinė's imagery survives in nocturnal lullabies, lullaby dainas, and stellar folk embroidery.
