## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Lazdona (recorded in sixteenth-century chronicles as *Lasdona*, and paralleled in Latvian tradition by *Lazdu māte*) is the Baltic deity of hazel groves and the autumn hazelnut harvest.

Historical sources and comparative philological studies establish her lineage:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicles**: Recorded by Jan Łasicki in *De diis Samagitarum* (1582, published 1615) as *Lasdona*, the goddess presiding over hazelnuts (*Lasdona nucum*).
* **Baltic Etymology**: The teonym derives from the Baltic root *\*lazd-* (Lithuanian *lazda* "rod, staff", *lazdynas* "hazel bush, *Corylus avellana*"; Latvian *lazda*) combined with the archaic feminine theonymic suffix *-ona* (cf. *Žvėrūna*, *Medona*).
* **Latvian Daina Tradition**: Paralleled in Latvian folklore by *Lazdu māte* ("Mother of Hazels"), recorded among the nature mothers (*mātes*) in Krišjānis Barons's *Latvju dainas*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic botanical and sacred cosmology, the hazel tree occupied a unique sanctuary status distinct from other forest flora:

### Guardian of Hazel Groves and Apotropaic Sanctuary

* **Protector of Hazelnut Sustenance**: Governs the flowering of hazel catkins and the ripening of hazelnuts, historically a critical nutrient-dense winter food supply.
* **Apotropaic Shield against Lightning**: In Baltic folk belief, hazel stands are immune to Perkūnas's lightning strikes (*į lazdyną žaibas netrenkia*), functioning as natural sanctuaries where pursued creatures take shelter.
* **Protective Ward against Vipers and Malice**: Hazel rods and switches were carried to repel vipers and placed over stable thresholds during Midsummer to ward off witchcraft.
* **Domain of the Forest Understory**: Operates in the woodland sphere alongside Medeina (Lithuanian) and Meža māte (Latvian), governing the deciduous shrub layer and wild harvested fruits.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folklore and ethnographic gathering traditions portray Lazdona through arboreal symbolism:

* **Living Hazel Canopy**: Conceived through the dense, leafy arches of blooming hazel thickets and golden catkins.
* **Hazelnut Clusters and Twin Nuts (*Keimeris*)**: Represented iconographically by clusters of ripe nuts, particularly fused twin hazelnuts symbolizing fertility and wealth.
* **Dowsing Wand and Staff**: Associated with the Y-shaped hazel dowsing fork (*virgulė*) and carved hazel walking staves.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Lazdona encompasses protected hazel stands and woodland groves:

* **Sacred Hazel Stands of Žemaitija (*Lazdynynai*)**: Protected hazel groves across western Lithuania where traditional felling taboos were strictly observed.
* **Hydronymic Sanctuaries**: River basins bearing the sacred root (such as the Lazduona river basin in Lithuania and the Lazdona parish in Latvia).
* **Forest Clearings and Edge Stands**: The sunny borders of mixed deciduous forests where wild hazel bushes flourish.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic communities practiced seasonal gathering and magical customs centered on the hazel:

* **Autumn Nut-Harvest Bee (*Riešutavimas*)**: Communal gathering expeditions in late August and September; finding twin nuts (*keimeris*) was celebrated as an omen of prosperity and good fortune.
* **Midsummer Dowsing Fork Cutting**: Harvesting Y-shaped hazel wands (*virgulės*) on Midsummer Night (Rasos / Jāņi) to locate underground water springs and hidden metals.
* **Protective Twig Affixing**: Placing hazel twigs in window frames and roof gables during summer thunderstorms to protect homes from lightning strikes.
