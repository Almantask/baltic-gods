## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Ratainyčia (recorded in sixteenth-century chronicles as *Ratainicza*) is a Samogitian deity documented in early modern historical catalogues of Baltic religion.

Historical sources and comparative philological research document the authentic lineage:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicle Catalogue**: Recorded by Jan Łasicki in *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (1582, published 1615) with the entry: *Ratainicza equorum habetur deus, ut Walgina aliorum pecorum* ("Ratainyčia is held to be the god of horses, as Walgina is of other livestock").
* **Baltic Linguistic Analyses**: Analyzed in Baltic linguistics (Kazimieras Būga, Norbertas Vėlius, Rolandas Kregždys, Vytautas Jaskevičius), identifying the name as the Lithuanian architectural and farmstead noun *ratainyčia / ratainė* (a cart shed or wagon shelter, derived from *ratas* "wheel" and plural *ratai* "wagon, carriage").
* **Samogitian Tradition**: Preserved in sixteenth-century historical lists reflecting early modern rural Samogitian homestead vocabulary and horse husbandry.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In sixteenth-century Baltic folk religion, Ratainyčia belongs to the sphere of farmstead transport and equine care:

### Guardian of Horses and the Farmstead Cart Shed

* **Patron of Horses (*Equorum deus*)**: In Jan Łasicki's record, functions as the guardian spirit of farmstead horses, parallel to Walgina's guardianship over other domestic livestock.
* **Sphere of the Wagon Shed (*Ratainyčia*)**: Connected to the farmstead outbuilding where wooden wagons, sledges, harnesses, and wheels were sheltered from rain and rot.
* **Complement to Pastoral Deities**: Operates alongside domestic and pastoral deities such as Walgina, Ūsinis, and Dimstipatis, providing spiritual coverage for the essential draft animals and transport equipment of the farm.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic farmstead material culture associates Ratainyčia with traditional woodworking and equine gear:

* **Timber Wagon Shed Architecture**: Conceived through the sturdy timber beams and thatch roofs of traditional rural carriage shelters.
* **Farm Wagons and Wooden Wheels**: Represented by hand-hewn wooden wheel hubs, spokes, rims, and axles crafted by village wheelwrights.
* **Horse Harness and Yoke**: Iconographically linked to leather bridles, wooden yokes, and woven reins used in everyday draft labor.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The historic domain of Ratainyčia is situated in the functional outbuildings of western Lithuanian farmsteads:

* **The Cart Shed (*Ratainyčia / Ratinė*)**: The dedicated shelter in traditional Samogitian homesteads (*sodybos*) housing wagons, harrows, and plows.
* **Stables and Paddock Enclosures**: The farmstead yards where working horses were stabled and harnessed.
* **Rural Farmsteads of Samogitia (*Žemaitija*)**: Historical sixteenth-century rural settlements documented by early modern chroniclers.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic communities observed traditional customs focused on horse care and equipment maintenance:

* **Seasonal Wagon Preparation**: Checking and greasing wooden axles before spring field transport and autumn harvest hauling.
* **Stable Consecrations**: Chanting traditional protective formulas when stabling working horses for the winter season.
* **Homestead Blessings**: Honoring the physical boundaries of farm outbuildings to protect draft gear and domestic animals.
