## 1. Etymology & Historical Chronicles

Kaupolis (Lithuanian: *Kaupolis / Kupolis / Kupolė*; Latvian: *Kaupolis / Kupole*; East Slavic chronicle gloss: *Koupal'*) is the Baltic deity of luxuriant vegetation, budding crops, medicinal herbs, and the summer solstice (*Rasos / Kupolinės*). The theonym is formed directly from the Baltic root *\*kup-* (attested in Lithuanian *kupėti* "to boil over, surge with vitality, grow luxuriantly", *kūpa* "abundance, swelling heap", and Latvian *kupēt* "to fume, surge"), expressing the exuberant crest of vegetative vitality at the summer solstice.

Historical chronicles and comparative folkloric sources document the deity across the Baltic cultural belt:

* **Hypatian Codex (*Ipatiy Chronicle*, 1262)**: Earliest medieval chronicle attestation in the Baltic-Slavic borderlands, recording *Koupal'* in connection with midsummer vegetative celebrations.
* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum...*, 1582, pub. 1615)**: Records *Kupolę* among the agricultural deities, noting that on the eve of Saint John the Baptist, communities assembled to gather vegetation and dedicate celebrations to the burgeoning growth of crops.
* **Comparative Baltic Context**: Direct Lithuanian counterpart to the Latvian Midsummer deities **Jānis**, **Līgo**, and **Zāļu māte** ("Mother of Herbs").

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, Kaupolis presides over the seasonal apex of plant life, when the Sun reaches its maximum height and all botanical organisms attain peak healing and reproductive potency.

### Sovereign of Vegetative Zenith & Solstice Vigils

* **Lord of Budding & Blooming (*Kupėjimas*)**: Infuses crops, grasses, and forest undergrowth with explosive vital energy, bringing rye ears into full blossom.
* **Patron of Medicinal Solstice Herbs**: Sanctifies the nine ritual herbs gathered at dawn during *kupoliavimas*, imbuing them with protective and curative virtues.
* **Sovereign of the Solstice Axis**: Embodied in the three-branched *kupolė* pole (*trišakė kupolė*), serving as a cosmic conductor connecting sky, earth, and underworld during the shortest night of the year.
* **Solar-Vegetative Synergy**: Works in harmony with **Saulė** (the Sun Maiden) during her solstice dance on the horizon, dispelling shadow forces through sacred bonfires.

## 3. Character & Visuals

Baltic folklore and festival traditions visualize Kaupolis and Kupolė as radiant youthful figures of nature's zenith:

* **Youthful & Radiant Stature**: Visualized as a tall, sun-bronzed youth crowned with golden rye ears, accompanied by Kupolė, a fair maiden adorned with nine kinds of wild meadow blossoms.
* **Emerald & Floral Garments**: Dressed in flowing unbleached linen garments draped with woven garlands of Saint John's wort (*jonažolės*), cornflowers, and wild thyme.
* **Emblematic Implements**: Carries the decorated three-branched *kupolė* staff, braided floral wreaths (*vainikai*), and a vessel of crystal morning dew (*rasa*).
* **Illuminated Night Presence**: Appears surrounded by rolling wheels of fire (*ugnies ratai*) and the golden glow of hilltop midsummer bonfires.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred domain of Kaupolis encompasses the blooming meadows, riverbanks, and solstice hills of Lithuania:

* **Historic Solstice Hills**: Sacred promontories near Kernavė, Rambynas, and the Šventoji River basin where communities gathered to erect the solstice staff.
* **Flowering Rye Fields (*Žydintys rugiai*)**: Expanses of blossoming grain where young people walked the field boundaries to bless crop growth.
* **Meadow Herb Valleys**: Lowland river valleys where ritual gathering of morning-dew flowers took place before dawn.
* **River Confluences & Lakes**: Waterways where flower wreaths were cast adrift at midnight for marriage divination and seasonal blessings.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Folk Practices

Communities observed dynamic, joyous rites during the summer solstice (*Rasos / Kupolinės*):

* **Ritual Herb Gathering (*Kupoliavimas*)**: Young women set out into the meadows before sunset, picking nine kinds of flowers without speaking (*tylomis*) to weave protective wreaths and divine the coming year.
* **Erection of the Solstice Pole (*Kupolės kėlimas*)**: Raising a tall, peeled spruce pole with three blooming birch branches at the top at field margins, defending the crops from evil eyes and hail.
* **Bonfire Vigils & Fiery Wheels**: Lighting hilltop bonfires that burned until sunrise, rolling flaming wooden wheels down hillsides into rivers to ensure continued solar vitality.
* **Morning Dew Washing (*Prausimasis rasa*)**: Washing faces in the solstice morning dew to receive health, youthfulness, and beauty from the blessed earth.
