## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In Baltic domestic religion, the sacred fire of the homestead was recognized as a conscious divine presence:
* **Jesuit Mission Documentation**: The 1583 annual letter of the Jesuit Order (*Annuae Litterae Societatis Jesu*) records that rural families maintained an unbroken hearth fire, spoken to with reverence, offered food, and directly named *Gabija*.
* **Linguistic Etymology**: Derived from Baltic *gaubti* (signifying "to cover, wrap, or shield", describing the ritual banking of embers beneath ash) or the archaic root *\*geb-* denoting nourishment and protective abundance.
* **Baltic Parallels**: Directly parallels the Latvian tradition of *Uguns māte* (the Mother of Fire), venerated across traditional dainas as the radiant protector of the domestic hearth.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

Gabijos Ugnis was the spiritual center and purifying heart of the traditional Baltic home:
* **Domestic Purity & Continuity**: Served as the perpetual guardian of household peace, health, and family lineage; letting the flame extinguish was viewed as a spiritual calamity.
* **Pantheon Relationships**: Functions as the physical presence of the goddess Gabija; operates in harmony with the earth mother *Žemyna* and homestead guardian *Dimstipatis*.
* **Zoomorphic Manifestations**: Folkloric accounts depict the spirit of fire appearing from hearth embers in the form of a red or white hen, a rooster, a red cat, or a sparking bird.

## 3. Character & Visuals

Traditional narratives portray the hearth fire in vivid visual forms:
* **Personified Countenance**: Visualized in folk tales as a serene elder woman or a youthful maiden clad in radiant red or shimmering garments.
* **Fiery Features**: Described with flowing reddish-gold hair and flashing eyes, reminiscent of the Latvian daina image of fire as a maiden in a golden bodice.
* **Cultic Attributes**: Accompanied by the hearthstones, glowing embers, the forged fire iron (*žarsteklis*), and the clean protective blanket of white ash.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred domain of Gabijos Ugnis is inseparable from the traditional homestead:
* **The Domestic Hearth**: Stationed permanently within the clay oven, open stone hearth (*židinys, krosnis*), or ceremonial fireplace (*ugnakuras*).
* **The Living Chamber**: The family living room and kitchen where meals were cooked, bread was baked, and evening gatherings took place.
* **Ancestral Farmsteads**: Documented across ethnographic regions of Lithuania and Latvia as the sacred center of rural community life.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

A strict customary code governed the care and feeding of the living flame:
* **Putting the Fire to Bed**: The evening ritual of *ugnies migdymas*, wherein embers were carefully swept together, banked beneath clean ash, and addressed with whispered prayers of peace.
* **Daily Offerings**: Offered the first pinch of salt, freshly baked bread crumbs, and bowls of clean water to quench the fire's thirst.
* **Ritual Taboos**: Absolute taboos prohibited spitting into the fire, kicking embers, throwing unclean debris into the hearth, or uttering harsh curses near the flames.
* **Homestead Relocation**: When moving to a new homestead, live coals were transferred in a sealed clay vessel accompanied by formal spoken invitations.
