## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Upinis is the Baltic deity of flowing rivers, streams, and freshwater currents.

Historical sources and comparative Baltic philology document the authentic lineage:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicle Catalogue**: Recorded in Jan Łasicki's treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (composed c. 1582, published Basel 1615) with the entry: *Upinis fluuiorum, huic porcellum candidum immolant, vt fluat aqua pura* ("Upinis of rivers; to him they sacrifice a white piglet so that pure water may flow").
* **Baltic Linguistic Etymology**: Derived from Lithuanian *upė* ("river, stream") with the adjectival suffix *-inis* ("pertaining to or dwelling in rivers"). Cognates include Latvian *upe* ("river") and Old Prussian *ape* (attested in the Elbing Vocabulary as *vlys* = *ape*), stemming from Proto-Indo-European *\*h₂ep-* / *\*h₂ōp-* ("flowing water").
* **Tripartite Water Cosmology**: Documented alongside *Ežerinis* (the deity of standing lakes, *Ezernis lacuum*) and *Šulninis* (the guardian of springs and wells, *Szullinnys fontium*).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic hydrological belief, Upinis presides over moving freshwaters and their ecological vital force:

### Guardian of Flowing Streams and Water Purity

* **Flowing Water and Current Vitality**: Oversees river channels, rapids, seasonal currents, and the transparent purity of freshwater streams.
* **Aquatic Ecosystem and Fishery**: Protectors of freshwater fish and seasonal spawning runs across river networks.
* **Complementary Aquatic Pantheon**: Operates in complementary polarity with Ežerinis (standing lake depths) and Šulninis (underground spring waters), paralleled in Latvian tradition by *Upes māte* (Mother of Rivers), *Ezera māte* (Mother of Lakes), and *Avota māte* (Mother of Springs).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folk religion conceptualizes the river numen through natural waterway elements:

* **The Sacred White Piglet**: Symbolized by the pristine white piglet offering consecrated to ensure crystal-clear water flow.
* **Flowing Currents and Reeds**: Associated with river ripples, water reeds (*nendrės*), and riverbed stone shallows.
* **River Confluences and Fords**: Marked by sacred river junctions where currents merge and pathways traverse the stream.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred geography of Upinis encompasses the historical river systems of the Baltic interior:

* **Sacred Fords (*Šventosios brastos*)**: Historic shallow river crossings where travelers halted to make protective offerings before venturing across deep waters.
* **River Confluences (*Santakos*)**: Confluences of major streams where waters merge, creating spiritual focal points.
* **Major Baltic Riverways**: Sacred river systems of Lithuania and Latvia, including the Dubysa, Nemunas, Nevėžis, Šventoji, and Daugava.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Chronicles and ethnographic field archives describe distinct river veneration customs:

* **Sacrifice for Water Purity**: Jan Łasicki (1582) records the dedication of a white piglet (*porcellum candidum*) to secure clean, unpolluted water flow for communities.
* **Fishermen's Libations**: Poured offerings of fresh ale, bread crusts, or the first catch returned to the stream before laying fishing nets.
* **Ford-Crossing Invocations**: Prayers and grain offerings tossed into river currents to protect travelers and livestock from treacherous whirlpools.
