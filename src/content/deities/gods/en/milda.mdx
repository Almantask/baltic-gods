## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Milda (Lithuanian: *Milda*; Latvian: *Milda*; literary personification of love, beauty, and courtship) is celebrated in Lithuanian Romantic mythography as the divine patroness of love, youthful affection, and springtime freedom. The theonym is connected to the Baltic root *\*mīl-* (Lithuanian *mylėti*, *milti* "to love, to cherish", Latvian *mīlēt* "to love", Prussian *mils* "dear").

The literary and historiographical development of Milda is recorded across key modern works:

* **Teodor Narbutt (*Dzieje starożytne narodu litewskiego*, 1835)**: Introduced Milda into nineteenth-century historiography as the Lithuanian goddess of love and beauty (*bogini miłości*), describing her temples in Vilnius and Aleksotas, her sacred month of April/May, and her retinue of white doves.
* **Józef Ignacy Kraszewski (*Anafielas*, 1840–1845)**: Further popularised Milda in Polish and Lithuanian Romantic literature, cementing her cultural image as the radiant sovereign of human hearts and courtship.
* **Academic Mythological Analysis**: Modern critical scholars analyze the figure as a nineteenth-century Romantic literary reconstruction modeled on Classical Greco-Roman pantheons (Aphrodite/Venus), noting that authentic pre-Christian chronicles and folklore connect love and marriage blessings to **Laima** and **Māra**.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Within nineteenth-century Romantic Baltic mythology and living cultural tradition, Milda governs the emotional, aesthetic, and matrimonial bonds of youth:

### Sovereign of Love, Youthful Courtship, and Spring Freedom

* **Patroness of Romantic Love**: Kindles affectionate longing and emotional devotion between lovers, guiding suitors through courtship and mutual devotion.
* **Guardian of Spring Awakening**: Associated with the blooming of flowers in May (*gegužė*), the return of migratory songbirds, and the renewal of life after winter.
* **Protector of Matrimonial Choice and Freedom**: Represents free romantic choice and emotional sincerity over forced or transactional marriage alliances.
* **Allied Mythological Figures**: Paired in Romantic lore with companion spirits such as **Kaunis** (the spirit of beauty and youthful grace) and attendants of love.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Nineteenth-century Romantic literature and national art depict Milda as an ethereal and radiant young maiden:

* **Radiant Maiden Demeanor**: Visualized as a gracefully proportioned young woman of extraordinary beauty, surrounded by an aura of gentle spring light.
* **Flowing Golden Tresses**: Endowed with long, flowing golden hair woven with wild springtime blossoms, and deep, clear cornflower-blue eyes.
* **Flowing White Gown**: Clothed in a light, flowing white linen robe embroidered with green ivy and myrtle patterns, fastened with a woven pastel sash.
* **Emblematic Attributes**: Adorned with a delicate crown of blooming myrtle (*rūtos*) and apple blossoms, attended by a flock of pure white doves pulling an aerial chariot.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The mythological and literary landscape of Milda centers upon scenic hills and riverbanks:

* **Aleksotas Hill in Kaunas**: Celebrated in Romantic accounts as the panoramic hill overlooking the Nemunas and Neris confluence where Milda's sanctuary supposedly stood.
* **Vilnius Hill of Three Crosses and Antakalnis**: Associated in local folklore with sacred spring groves and love sanctuaries along the Vilnia river valley.
* **Flowering Apple Orchards and Meadows**: Blooming orchards and meadowlands across Lithuania where young couples met to exchange courtship vows.
* **Sacred Love Springs**: Clear forest rivulets whose waters were believed to bestow youthful charm and enduring affection.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Cultural festivals and modern folkloric revivals celebrate Milda through joyful spring traditions:

* **Milda's Day (*Mildos diena*, May 13)**: Celebrated during the height of spring blossoms with outdoor gatherings, folk songs of courtship, and the weaving of floral garlands.
* **Myrtle and Wildflower Offerings**: Young maidens and youths wove wreaths of fresh spring blossoms and floated them on rivers to divine future marriage.
* **Courtship Songs and Dances**: Singing traditional love dainos and performing circle dances (*rateliai*) around spring bonfires.
* **Bridal Myrtle Blessings**: Placing sprigs of fragrant green rue (*rūta*) and myrtle into wedding bouquets as symbols of enduring fidelity and affection.
