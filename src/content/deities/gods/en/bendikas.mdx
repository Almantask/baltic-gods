## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In historical documentations of Samogitian religion, Bendikas is recognized as the protector of wayfarers venturing beyond known borders:
* **Chronicle Record**: Explicitly catalogued in Jan Łasicki's 1582 treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* as *Bendim*, glossed with the Latin definition *Bendim pro peregrinatione* ("Bendikas for travel abroad, journeys into distant territories, or pilgrimage").
* **Linguistic Derivation**: Etymologically derived from the Baltic root *\*bend-* (cognate with Lithuanian *bendras* meaning "companion, partner, shared", *bendrystė* meaning "fellowship", and *bendrija* meaning "community"), rooted in Proto-Indo-European *\*bhendh-* ("to bind, join together").
* **Historical Context of Mobility**: Unlike spirits confined to the farmstead boundary, Bendikas was invoked specifically when an individual crossed from the familiar, spirit-protected domestic sphere into unsponsored wilderness roads.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

Bendikas presides over the vulnerability of transit and long-distance travel:
* **Guardian of Distant Roads**: Protects travelers, merchants, and messengers undertaking multi-day journeys through extensive forest tracts, unmarked trails, and foreign regions.
* **Perimeter Transition**: Marks the sacred transition from domestic protection into the open world, warding travelers against ambush, disorientation, and sudden severe weather.
* **Pantheon Relationships**: Operates outside the domestic precinct where *Dimstipatis* (homestead guardian) ceases, collaborating with road spirits such as *Kelukis* (master of road paths) and *Bentis* (uniter of road companions).

## 3. Character & Visuals

The deity was perceived through physical symbols of journeying and departure:
* **Traveler's Staff**: Symbolized by the carved rowan walking staff (*keliauninko lazda*) used to test footing, cross swamp margins, and ward off perils.
* **Crossroads Departure Stone**: Represented by smooth boundary boulders where wayfarers rested their packs and deposited tokens before setting out.
* **Road Dust & Bound Pack**: Evoked through the packed linen bundle (*kelionės ryšulys*), open village gates, and tracks disappearing into ancient woodland.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The presence of Bendikas was venerated at settlement thresholds and long-distance transit routes:
* **Settlement Boundaries & Gates**: The physical gates and stone markers separating cultivated village lands from uncontrolled wilderness.
* **Major Trade Corridors**: Ancient overland routes crossing Samogitia and connecting Baltic communities with coastal trading centers (`sambian-journey-departure-shrine`).
* **River Crossings & Isolated Fords**: Unbridged river crossings where travelers gathered into groups to ensure mutual safety.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Customary practices honored Bendikas through departure rites and boundary invocations:
* **Threshold Offerings**: Depositing a small pinch of salt, bread crumbs, or a coin upon the boundary stone or gate threshold before stepping onto the high road.
* **Departure Blessing**: Uttering traditional spoken formulas (*„Laimingai nueik ir sugrįžk“*) while touching the walking staff to the boundary stone.
* **Safe-Return Thanksgiving**: Pouring a sip of ale or water onto the home threshold upon returning unscathed from foreign travel.
