## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Kempe (Lithuanian *Kempė*) is an entry in Baltic mythological and lexicographical studies reflecting early vernacular woodland botanical terminology.

Historical linguistics and mythographical scholarship document the authentic lineage:

* **Baltic Lexicography and Philology**: Recorded in standard Lithuanian lexicography (*Lietuvių kalbos žodynas*, LKŽ) as the common noun *kempė* (variants: *kempinė*, *kempis*), designating bracket fungi, tree polypores, and tinder mushrooms (*pintis, medžio grybas - Polyporaceae*).
* **Comparative Linguistic Analysis**: Analyzed by linguists and mythologists (Kazimieras Būga, Rolandas Kregždys, Norbertas Vėlius), tracing the term to Baltic roots associated with porous, fibrous, or dried woodland growths (*kempėti* "to dry up, wither").
* **Woodland Sphere Distinction**: Historically evaluated alongside genuine chronicled woodland and tree spirits such as *Kerpyčius* (spirit of tree moss and lichens), *Šilinyčius* (spirit of forest mosses), and *Kirnis* (guardian of cherry groves and marshland trees).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In traditional Baltic woodland lore, the tree fungus represents a vital element of forest ecology and rural material culture:

### Spirit of Woodland Growth and Tinder Harvesting

* **Domain of Tree Bracket Fungi**: Associated with the shelf-like polypores that grow upon living and fallen birch, oak, and deciduous trees throughout Baltic woodlands.
* **Tinder and Fire-Making Material**: Essential in traditional fire-crafting (*skiltuvas ir pintis*), where dried bracket fungi (*kempės / pintys*) served as the primary spark-catching medium for striking flint and iron.
* **Woodland Ecosystem Harmony**: Operates within the broader Baltic forestry complex alongside Medeina / Miško motė (forest mother) and localized spirits of tree bark, mosses, and undergrowth (*Kerpyčius, Šilinyčius*).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic forest traditions conceive the material form of the tree fungus through its natural organic texture:

* **Semicircular Hardened Brackets**: Characterized by thick, tiered, woody shelves clinging to mature tree trunks.
* **Earthy Grey and Brown Tones**: Visualized in the weathered ash-grey, charcoal, and moss-green hues of aged woodland polypores.
* **Fibrous Tinder Interior**: Recognized by the soft, porous, velvet-like inner core prized for catching sparks from struck iron.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The historic domain of Kempe is situated in the ancient mixed and deciduous forests of the Baltic region:

* **Birch Groves and Deciduous Stands**: Dense forests of birch (*beržynai*), oak (*ąžuolynai*), and alder where bracket fungi thrive.
* **Old-Growth Tree Trunks**: Standing ancient trees and decaying nurse logs across traditional rural woodlands.
* **Deciduous Woodlands of Samogitia (*Žemaitija*)**: Historical forest tracts in western Lithuania where peasants gathered woodland materials.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic communities practiced seasonal harvesting and careful stewardship of woodland resources:

* **Seasonal Tinder Gathering**: Harvesting dry bracket fungi in early autumn before the arrival of winter rains and frost.
* **Traditional Fire-Striking Lore**: Respectful handling of fire-making materials in accordance with sacred fire traditions (*Gabija*).
* **Forest Gathering Customs**: Leaving small seasonal offerings of bread or honoring tree trunks when harvesting forest products.
