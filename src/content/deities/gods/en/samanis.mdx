## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Samanis (deriving directly from Lithuanian *samanos*, "moss") personifies the living velvet carpet of the Baltic forest floor, bog peat mosses, and moisture-retaining woodland vegetation.

Historical sources and comparative folklore document the sacred forest floor tradition:

* **16th-Century Historical Chronicles (Jan Łasicki 1582 / 1615)**: Records the authentic historical deities of forest moss and lichens—**Kerpyčius** (*Kierpiczus*, from *kerpė* "lichen, tree moss") and his assistant **Šilinyčius** (*Siliniczus*, from *šilas* "pine forest heath")—noting that woodsmen made offerings to them before harvesting moss to caulk wooden log buildings (*uti Kierpiczus huiusque adiutor Siliniczus, musci in silvis nascentis: cuius in aedificiis magnus apud illos est usus. huic etiam muscum lecturi sacrificant*).
* **Lithuanian Folklore and Landscape Legends**: Preserves *Samanis* as a legendary nature giant (*milžinas*) associated with forest lakes (such as Lake Samanis in Aukštaitija) and ancient mossy boulders.
* **Latvian Forest Tradition (*Nacionālā enciklopēdija* & *Latvju dainas*)**: In Latvian tradition, the forest floor, moss-covered boulders (*sūnakmeņi*), and bog carpets fall under the sovereign protection of **Meža māte** (Mother of the Forest) and **Purva māte** (Mother of the Swamp).
* **Etymological Analysis**: The teonym derives from Proto-Baltic *\*sam-ana-* (*samanos* "moss"), related to Slavic *\*mъxъ* and Sanskrit roots describing damp ground growth.

The authentic historical and folkloric context is analysed by Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, Vol. 2) and Vytautas Ališauskas (*Jono Lasickio pasakojimas apie žemaičių dievus*, 2012).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic forest ecology, moss was revered as an indispensable environmental sponge that regulated humidity, protected dormant tree seeds, and cushioned woodland paths.

### Sovereign of Forest Moisture and Log-Caulking Lichens

* **Regulator of Forest Floor Moisture**: Protects deep sphagnum and peat moss beds that store spring rains and feed woodland streams during dry summers.
* **Preserver of Winter Soil Warmth**: Acts as an insulating blanket preventing the forest subsoil and tree roots from freezing solid during sub-zero winters.
* **Guardian of Log-Chinking Moss**: Blesses the harvesting of clean, dry wall-chinking moss used by Baltic builders to insulate traditional log farmhouses.
* **Retinue of Woodland Deities**: Operates within the sacred retinue of Medeina (forest ruler), Giraitis (grove spirit), and Žemėpatis (earth master).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic forest folklore portrays Samanis through lush, botanical woodland iconography:

* **Gentle Forest Floor Elder / Camouflaged Giant**: Conceived as a peaceful, silent woodland entity cloaked in vibrant emerald-green sphagnum moss.
* **Peat-Lichen Mantle**: Clothed in a seamless living mantle woven from green peat moss, silver reindeer lichens (*kerpės*), and dried pine needles.
* **Birch Bark Staff and Gathering Wedge**: Carries a smooth birch-bark staff and a wooden wedge used for harvesting clean moss cushions without harming tree bark.
* **Soft Leaf-Green Gaze**: Features warm, moss-green eyes and a beard tangled with delicate bilberry shoots and forest flower blossoms.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Samanis spans the deep pine forests and mossy wetlands of the Baltic lands:

* **Aukštaitija and Labanoras Coniferous Forests (Lithuania)**: Old-growth pine forests carpeted in thick sphagnum moss cushions near Lake Samanis.
* **Mossy Erratic Boulders (*Sūnakmeņi / Samanoti akmenys*)**: Ancient glacial boulders blanketed in deep moss, historically regarded as nature altars.
* **Bog Margins and Wetland Springs**: The saturated moss hummocks buffering clean forest springs from sediment.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic woodsmen, foragers, and builders observed respectful nature-gathering rituals:

* **Pre-Harvest Moss Gathering Libations**: Pouring fresh spring water or placing breadcrumbs upon a mossy stone before harvesting moss for log-house construction.
* **Berry and Mushroom Thanksgiving**: Leaving the first harvested wild bilberries or mushrooms upon a soft moss cushion as a reciprocal nature offering.
* **Footwear Reverence in Sacred Glades**: Woodsmen treading softly and avoiding tearing moss beds or disturbing fragile peat layers.
