## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Gabija (also *Gabėta* / *Gabieta*, *Gabie*) is the Lithuanian goddess and spirit of the household fire. Her name is derived from Lithuanian *gaubti* / *gobėti* — to cover or wrap — naming the nightly act of covering live coals with ash. Related historical forms include *Matergabija* and *Pelengabija*.

Jan Łasicki records her in *De diis Samagitarum* (late 16th century; published 1615) as *Gabie*, with the prayer *Gabie deuaite pokielki garu nuleiski kirbixtu* — asking the fire to raise heat without casting dangerous sparks. The theonym is richly attested in later Lithuanian folklore and ethnographic descriptions of hearth care.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Gabija governs the domestic hearth fire: warmth, cooking, bread baking, and protection of the home. The fire is treated as a living sacred being — fed, washed, put to bed, and woken. When respected, she stays in place; when insulted (spitting, fouling, trampling coals), she “goes for a walk” and burns the house.

**Relations:** *Matergabija* / *Pelengabija* (historical alternate names in the chronicle tradition); *Gabjauja* (barn / threshing-fire theonym in the same *gab-* fire complex); Latvian *Uguns māte* (functional parallel — not a Latvian Gabija theonym; attested in beliefs and narrative lore); cat or bird (zoomorphic forms).

## 3. Character and Visuals

- **Anthropomorphic form**: A woman clothed in red; sometimes described with wings.
- **Zoomorphic forms**: Cat or bird.
- **Sacred signs**: Live embers under ash; bread and salt offerings; a bowl of clean water left by the hearth so the fire may “wash.”

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Her primary sanctuary is the homestead hearth (*židinys* / stove). Communal renewal of household fire is tied to midsummer ritual bonfires: old hearth fire is extinguished and rekindled from a clean ceremonial flame carried home at night.

- **Pūčkorių hillfort (Vilnius)**: Modern Romuva spring rites keep a sacred Gabija hearth and feed the fire with salt.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Evening hearth care was the mistress of the house’s duty:

- **Putting the fire to bed (*gaubimas* / *guldymas*)**: Coals are banked and covered with ash so Gabija sleeps and does not wander.
- **Prayers**: Variants such as “Švinta Gabija, švinta Gabieta, neik iš vietos…” / “Šv. Gabieta, gyvenk spakainiai” ask her to stay quiet in her place.
- **Offerings**: Bread (and salt) to “feed” and satiate her; clean water left beside the hearth for washing.
- **Taboos**: Do not spit into the fire, throw filth on it, or trample fallen embers; offended fire walks and causes house fire.
- **Continuity**: Letting the hearth die was feared as household misfortune; at marriage, a bride could carry fire from her parents’ hearth in a pot to begin cooking in the new home.
- **Seasonal renewal**: At summer solstice, hearth fire was renewed from a ritually clean communal blaze.
