## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Gabija (also *Gabjeta*, *Gabi*, Latvian *Uguns māte*) is the sacred Baltic goddess of the hearth fire, domestic peace, and home protection. Her name derives from the Baltic verb *gaubti* / *apgaubti* meaning to cover or wrap (referring to covering live coals with ashes for the night). Historical records document Gabija in Jan Łasicki’s 1582 *De Diis Samagitarum* as *Mater Gabi*, describing evening hearth-covering rituals performed by Samogitian women.

In Daiva Vaitkevičienė’s research *Manifestations of Fire in Baltic Culture*, the hearth fire is revealed not as fuel, but as a living sacred entity requiring cleanliness, respect, and daily supplication.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Gabija governs the domestic hearth, cooking, bread baking, family harmony, and protection against destructive fires. She manifests as glowing embers, taking the form of a woman dressed in red garments hovering above the hearth.

When the hearth is kept pristine and fed with clean water and bread, Gabija brings health and harmony. However, if insulted (by spitting into coals, casting trash, or shouting near the fire), Gabija transforms into a destructive force, burning the house to ashes.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

The evening hearth-covering rite (*Gaubimas*) was a mandatory daily duty of the matriarch:

- **Hearth Covering Rite**: Each night, live coals were banked into the center of the hearth, covered with ashes, marked with a cross, accompanied by prayers: "Sacred Gabijeta, rest peacefully in the night, protect our home!"
- **Clean Water and Bread Offering**: A bowl of pure water and a crust of bread were placed beside the hearth so Gabija could wash and refresh herself overnight.
- **Hearth Fire Transfer at Weddings**: When a bride departed for her husband's home, live embers from her ancestral hearth were carried in a clay vessel to ignite the new family hearth with Gabija's blessing.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

In Baltic folk songs, Gabija is invoked as "Holy Gabijeta" or "Mother of Fire":
> "Sacred Gabijeta,  
> Mother of the hearth fire,  
> Do not leave our home,  
> Protect our children."

In Latvian *Dainas*, *Uguns māte* (Mother of Fire) is praised for winter warmth:
> "Mother of Fire, bright spark,  
> Warm our room through the cold night."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

The homestead hearth served as the primary sanctuary in ancient Baltic life. Outdoor altars and perpetual hearths (*Alkai*) remain central symbols of Baltic spiritual tradition.
