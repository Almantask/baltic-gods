## 1. Etymology & Historical Chronicles

Žvorūna (also attested as *Žvorūnė*, *Zvoruna*, and *Žvėrinė*) is the ancient Baltic goddess of wild beasts, predators, and the hunt. Etymologically, the name derives from Proto-Baltic *\*žvēr-* (Lithuanian: *žvėris*, Latvian: *zvērs*, Old Prussian: *swīrins* — "wild animal, beast of prey") combined with the divine agent suffix *-ūnė / -ūna*, translating as "The Mistress of Wild Beasts" or "Protectress of Game."

Žvorūna is among the earliest historically documented Baltic deities. In the thirteenth-century *Hypatian Codex* (recording events of 1252), she is explicitly listed alongside Medeina as one of the royal deities secretly venerated by King Mindaugas (*Medein i Zvorunu*). In the 1261 Slavic translation of the *Chronicle of John Malalas* (containing the Sovijus myth insertion), she is recorded as *Žvorūna* and glossed as the sovereign mistress of canines and beasts (*rekše sukoju / zvěrinica*). Fifteenth-century chronicler Jan Długosz later aligned the Baltic forest and hunting deity with Roman Diana. Modern critical analysis of Žvorūna has been conducted by Algirdas Julius Greimas (*Apie dievus ir žmones*, 1979; *Žvėrūna Medeina*, 1993), Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, 1996), and Dr. Vykintas Vaitkevičius (2003).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmological thought, Žvorūna presides over the animal kingdom of the primeval forest (*giria*). Rather than aiding hunters in excessive slaughter, she serves as the sovereign protectress of wildlife who regulates the hunt, shields game from wanton cruelty, and maintains the ecological and spiritual equilibrium of the wilderness.

### Medeina & Žvorūna: Dual Sovereignty of the Forest

Baltic mythologists distinguish between the complementary domains of the woodland realm:

* **Medeina**: Derived from *medis / medė* ("tree, forest"), governing the vegetal growth, sacred trees, timber, and forest canopy.
* **Žvorūna**: Derived from *žvėris* ("beast"), presiding specifically over the animal inhabitants, predatory fauna, and the ethics of the hunt.
* **Dual Hypostasis**: Semiotic and structural studies (Greimas, Beresnevičius) demonstrate that Medeina and Žvorūna form a dual hypostasis of wild nature—the botanical grove and the dynamic animal hunt.

### Divine & Natural Relations

* **Meža māte**: The direct Latvian counterpart who pastures her wild flock (*"Meža māte gana savus jēriņus"*) and whose wolves are addressed as her holy dogs (*"Meža mātes suņi"*).
* **Animal Retinue**: Guarded by a sacred retinue comprising wolves (*vilkai* — her holy hounds in the *vilkmergė* mythologem), hares (*kiškiai*), bears (*lokiai*), red deer (*elniai*), and elks (*briedžiai*).
* **Sacred Groves**: Operates within untouched virgin forests, where breaking living branches or disturbing the peace was forbidden.

## 3. Character & Visuals

In authentic Baltic folk narratives and early chronicle records, Žvorūna manifests in distinct zoomorphic and anthropomorphic forms:

* **Wolf-Maiden (*Vilkmergė*)**: Portrayed as an untamed, swift forest maiden who runs freely through old-growth woodlands, surrounded by a protective pack of wolves.
* **Zoomorphic Manifestation**: Recorded in archaic chronicle glosses as appearing directly in the form of a female wolf or hound (*kalė / vilkė*), embodying the primal spirit of the predator.
* **Numinous Presence**: Envisioned gliding silently through the forest undergrowth, turning hunters' arrows aside and guiding lost travelers toward safety or into bogs depending on their respect for the wild.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred geography of Žvorūna is tied to ancient Lithuanian and Baltic old-growth forests and cult stone complexes:

* **Aukštaitian Royal Sanctuaries**: Toponymic and archaeological investigations by Dr. Vykintas Vaitkevičius identified the core historical cult of Žvėrūna-Medeina in eastern Lithuania (Aukštaitija), marked by sacred boulders known as *Kiškių bažnyčios* ("Hare Churches") and *Vilko pėdos* ("Wolf Footprints") in the Sirvėta, Švenčionys, and Trakai regions.
* **Primeval Woodlands (*Gojai & Alkai*)**: Dense virgin forests and wetland boundaries across Lithuania and Latvia where animals sought sanctuary during winter months.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Hunting Ethics, & Folk Practices

Baltic hunters maintained strict customary observances to honor Žvorūna and ensure safe passage through the forest:

* **First-Kill Offering (*Pirmoji Auka*)**: Hunters were required to leave the first portion of game (a piece of meat, fat, liver, or the first drops of blood) at the base of an ancient tree or mossy stone before taking any carcass home.
* **Hunting Ethics & Taboos**: Strict religious prohibitions forbade killing pregnant or nursing animals, taking more game than necessary, or shouting and swearing in sacred woods.
* **Wolf-Binding Charms (*Užrišti Vilkui Dantis*)**: Villagers recited ancient verbal incantations asking Žvorūna to lock the jaws of her wolves (*užrakinti vilkui nasrus*) to safeguard pastured livestock.
* **Votive Offerings**: Offerings of raw meat, bread, salt, honey, and porridge were placed upon sacred cult stones (*alkakmeniai*) and tree hollows to secure the benevolence of the forest sovereign.
