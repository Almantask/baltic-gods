## 1. Etymology & Historical Chronicles

Okopirmus (Old Prussian: *Occopirnys / Ockopirmus / Ukapirmas*; Lithuanian: *Okopirmas / Ukapirmas*; Latvian: *Okopirms*) is the supreme sky god and primordial creator of the Old Prussians. The theonym is an authentic Western Baltic compound derived from the Old Prussian superlative intensifying prefix *ucka- / uka- / oka-* (meaning "most, all-, highest, very") united with *pīrms / pirmas* ("first", originating from the Proto-Indo-European ordinal root *\*pr̥h₃-mó-s*). The compound literally translates to "The Absolute First", "The Very First", or "The Foremost One".

In comparative Baltic mythology, Okopirmus represents the Western Baltic manifestation of the supreme Indo-European celestial sovereign (*Summus Deus*), corresponding directly to the East Baltic sky-father **Dievas / Dievs** and the chronicled Lithuanian primordial creator epithets **Prakorimas**, **Andajas**, and **Nunadievis**.

The deity is attested in foundational 16th-century Prussian historical documents:

* **Constitutiones Synodales (1530)**: Promulgated during the Lutheran synods of the Sambia and Pomesania dioceses, listing him at the head of the Prussian pantheon: *"Occopirnys, quem primum Deum Coeli et Stellarum appellant, quasi Saturnum"*.
* **Sudauer Büchlein (*Sudovian Book*, c. 1520s–1530, printed c. 1561)**: Opens its canonical catalogue of Prussian deities with: *"Ockopirmus der erste Gott Himmels vnd des Gestirnes / Saturnus"*.
* **Jan Malecki (*Libellus de sacrificiis et idolatria veterum Borussorum*, 1551/1563)**: Notes him as *"Occopirmus, primus coeli ac terrae Deus"*.
* **Maciej Stryjkowski (*Kronika Polska, Litewska, Żmódzka...*, 1582)**: Identifies *Okkopirmas* as the highest god of heaven and earth, equating him with Lithuanian *Prakorimas*.
* **Matthäus Praetorius (*Deliciae Prussicae*, c. 1690)**: Synthesizes regional Prussian records of *Ukapirmas*.

His etymology, pantheon position, and theological typology have been analyzed in foundational monographs by Vladimir Toporov (*Prusskij Jazyk*), Vytautas Mažiulis (*Prūsų kalbos etimologijos žodynas*), Rolandas Kregždys (*Baltų mitologemų etimologijos žodynas II*), Gintaras Beresnevičius (*Prūsų religija ir mitologija*, 2004), Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, Vol. 2), Wilhelm Mannhardt (*Letto-Preussische Götterlehre*), and *Visuotinė lietuvių enciklopedija*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, Okopirmus occupies the supreme celestial apex, presiding over the primordial creation, celestial order, daylight, and the stars.

### Supreme Sky Sovereign & Primordial Cause

* **Head of the Pantheon**: Listed consistently as Deity #1 in 16th-century Prussian theological catalogues, embodying the prime origin of the cosmos and the sovereign heavens (*coeli et stellarum*).
* **The Transcendent Creator (*Deus Otiosus*)**: Functions as the transcendent celestial orderer who established cosmic laws, moral boundaries, and seasonal rhythms. Following the initial act of creation, he remains elevated in the highest firmament, delegating active terrestrial and meteorological governance to lower functional deities.
* **Position Above the Active Triad**: Standing above the active cultic triad of **Perkūnas** (thunder, rain, warfare, justice), **Patrimpas** (rivers, springs, agricultural fertility), and **Patulas / Peckols** (chthonic depths, death, the ancestral realm), Okopirmus represents the supreme celestial source from which all natural forces derive their authority.
* **Guarantor of Cosmic Equilibrium**: Oversees the eternal balance of the cosmos, maintaining order between the upper celestial sphere, the living earth, and the underworld.

## 3. Character & Visuals

Baltic theological tradition conceptualizes Okopirmus through aniconic celestial grandeur rather than physical idol worship:

* **Aniconic Celestial Presence**: Unlike lower deities who were represented by physical symbols or grove idols, Okopirmus was worshipped as an unconfined, transcendent spiritual presence residing in the highest heavens.
* **Radiance of the Sky Vault**: Manifests through the boundless expanse of the daylight sky, the clear celestial dome, and the immutable paths of the stars.
* **Serene Patriarch of the Cosmos**: Envisioned as a venerable, all-seeing cosmic father possessing absolute authority, elevated above the conflicts and passions of earthly life.
* **Symbols of Transcendence**: Symbolized by the eternal open sky, the sun-path, and the sacred undying flame kept under open firmaments.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred geography of Okopirmus encompasses the celestial dome and historical Western Baltic sanctuary regions:

* **The Celestial Vault (*Dangaus skliautas / Coelum*)**: The highest firmament of the sky, encompassing the sun, moon, and stellar constellations.
* **Sambia Peninsula (*Semba / Samland*)**: The historic coastal heartland of Old Prussia where 16th-century synodal decrees and folklore treatises documented his premier position.
* **Sudovia (*Sūduva / Sudauen*)**: The southeastern Baltic region whose traditions and religious hierarchy were recorded in the *Sudovian Book*.
* **Sacred Open-Air Mounds & Alkai**: Elevated sanctuary hilltops and ancient open-air sanctuaries where sacred fires were maintained under the open sky.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Practices

Historical chronicles document the distinct ceremonial reverence accorded to Okopirmus:

* **Inaugural Priestly Invocations**: In communal assemblies and sacred festivals (such as the *Ožys* goat sanctification recorded in the *Sudovian Book*), the presiding priest (*Wurschait / Vuršaitis*) commenced the ceremony by reciting the pantheon list, invoking Okopirmus strictly in the first position.
* **Premier Libations (*Pirmasis Nuliejimas*)**: The opening draught of mead or ceremonial beer was poured out in honor of Okopirmus before any other deity was toasted or sacrifices were consumed.
* **Honor Without Direct Animal Sacrifice**: Reflecting his nature as a transcendent *deus otiosus*, Okopirmus received no direct animal slaughter or individual sacrificial altars; physical offerings were directed to active intermediary deities (Perkūnas, Potrimpas, Peckols, Pergrubrius).
* **Veneration of the Sacred Flame**: Revered through perpetual flames maintained on elevated open-air sanctuaries, symbolizing the primordial creative light.
