## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Perkūnas is the active Baltic god of thunder, lightning, and atmospheric weather. His name is usually linked to Proto-Indo-European *\*perkʷu-* (“oak”) and/or *\*per-* (“strike”); the etymology is not fully settled. Historical name variants include Lithuanian *Perkūnas*, Latvian *Pērkons* (folk diminutive *Pērkonītis*), and Old Prussian *Percunis*; *Parkuns* is attested as Yotvingian. Lithuanian folk epithets include *Dundulis*, *Tarškulis*, *Grumužis*, and *Dievaitis*; Latvian tradition prefers *Pērkoņtēvs* and *Vecais tēvs*.

Historical chronicles document Perkūnas more frequently than any other Baltic deity:
- **1252 Hypatian Codex**: Records King Mindaugas offering sacrifices to *Diviriks*, commonly reconstructed as *Dievų rikis* (“Ruler/Leader of Gods”) and identified with Perkūnas.
- **1261 Volhynian / Malalas Chronicle Gloss**: The legend of Sovijus lists Perkūnas alongside *Teliavelis*, *Andajus*, and *Žvorūna*.
- **13th-Century Livonian Rhymed Chronicle**: Around verse 1430 records the Baltic god under the name *Perkune* (termed an *apgot* or pagan idol).
- **1326 Peter von Dusburg (*Chronicon Terrae Prussiae*)**: Describes the supreme sanctuary of Romuva in Nadruvia and its *krivis*; the evergreen oak, triadic idols, and perpetual oak-wood fire are first detailed in the early-16th-century Prussian Romuva tradition.
- **15th-Century Jan Długosz (*Annales*)**: Compares Perkūnas to Roman Jupiter and notes sacred fire.
- **16th–17th c. drought rites**: *Percunos* appears among atmospheric gods; communal goat / black-calf offerings with beer libation for rain are recorded in early-17th-century ethnographic notes.
- **Prussian Romuva tradition**: Describes Perkūnas among a triad in a multi-trunked evergreen oak, with a perpetual flame tended by *vaidelotės*; later chronicle summaries mainly characterize weather functions.

Perkūnas is cast as guardian of cosmic order, defending the mortal realm against subterranean chaos (the Perkūnas–Velnias “basic myth”).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Perkūnas occupies the active middle and upper atmosphere in the Baltic tripartite cosmology (Sky, Earth, Underworld). Lithuanian tradition places him hierarchically below Andajus / Dievas as the active weather deity; Latvian tradition more often stresses **Dievs–Pērkons function overlap** rather than an Andajus title.

His primary functional domains include:
- **Meteorological & Seasonal Awakening**: Controls storm clouds, lightning, and rain. In spring, his first thunderbolt marks the awakening of the frozen earth (in Lithuanian tradition linked with Žemyna), opening the season for planting.
- **Divine Retribution**: Folklore depicts Perkūnas punishing evil spirits and wrongdoers; chronicles preserve oaths sworn by Perkūnas, including a fire-oath formula.
- **Cosmic Dualism**: Represents the primary celestial counterpart in the eternal duel against chthonic Velnias (Velns), who seeks refuge under stones, in hollow oaks, or among cattle.

**Relations:** Andajus/Dievas (hierarchical superior / function-overlap); Žemyna (earth awakened by first thunder, LT); Teliavelis, Andajus, Žvorūna (co-listed in the 1261 Sovijus pantheon); Velnias/Velns (rival); horses as chariot mounts.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folklore and traditional dainas depict Perkūnas as a mature, stern figure with a red or brown beard (*rudabarzdis* / *ugninė barzda*):
- **Looks**: Mature but still strong / stern (*rūstus*); red-brown beard (*rudabarzdis*).
- **Clothing**: Fiery garments (*ugniniai rūbai*).
- **Chariot & Steeds**: Rides across the sky in a chariot drawn by horses, producing the sound of thunder. Latvian dainas often specify black, lead, or white horses (silver chariot: *sidraba ratiņi*).
- **Weapons & Tools**: Wields thunderstone projectiles (*Perkūno kulkos* / *Pērkona lode*; folk identification with belemnites and stone axes), axes and hammers (*milna*), and lightning bolts — including the motif of striking with nine thunderbolts (*Deviņiem zibeņiem*).
- **Sacred Symbols**: The sacred oak (*Quercus robur* / *ąžuolas* / *ozols*); axe motifs / thunderstone axes.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Perkūnas' cult was centered on elevated terrain, waterside oak groves, and lightning-struck landmarks across Lithuania, Latvia, and Old Prussia:
- **Sacred Geography**: Mountain tops, hillforts (*piliakalniai*), sacred groves (*alkai*, *elkai*), and hilltop altars; drought rites on wooded hills.
- **Romuva Sanctuary (Nadruvia / Old Prussia)**: Romuva and the *krivis* in Nadruvia; a multi-trunked evergreen oak housing the sacred fire and divine triad (Prussian Romuva tradition).
- **Rambynas Hill (Pagėgiai, Lithuania)**: Sacred *alkvietė* / Skalvian hill.
- ***Pekas kalns* (Latvia)**: A Beverīna-tradition hillfort.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

The cult of Perkūnas involved both public sanctuary rites and family protection customs:
- **Perpetual Flame (Prussian tradition)**: *Vaidelotės* maintained an unbroken fire of oak wood; extinguishing the flame was treated as a grave offense, and the *vaidilutis* could pay with his life.
- **Drought & Rain Supplications**: Communal offerings of a black goat, she-calf, and rooster/hen with beer poured into the fire to invoke rain. Latvian dainas separately mention barley (*mieži*) among storm-related offerings.
- **Seasonal Festivals & Taboos**: First spring thunder unlocks the earth in folklore (tilling often delayed until then in Lithuanian tradition). The neotraditional feast *Jorė* is sometimes linked to this motif; Latvian *Jurģi* belong primarily to Ūsiņš/horses. Thursdays (*Ketvirtadienis*) were dedicated to Perkūnas in Lithuanian tradition (farm-work bans); Latvian folk calendars mark *Pēterdiena* (29 June) as *Pērkona diena*.
- **Home Protection Rites**: Stone axes and thunderstones (*Perkūno kulkos* / *Pērkona lode*) were kept as protection against lightning; Latvian belief also records carving crosses on rafters (*spāres*).
