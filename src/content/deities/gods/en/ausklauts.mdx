## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Ausklauts (also attested through the chronicle theonyms *Prigirsztius* and *Auschauts*) is the Baltic deity of divine auditory attentiveness, spoken truth, and moral accountability before oaths.

Sixteenth-century chronicles and Baltic structural semiotics document this tradition:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicle Entries**: Documented in Jan Łasicki's treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (composed c. 1582, published Basel 1615, reprinted in BRMŠ Vol. 2) under the auditory theonym: *Prigirsztius, qui audire facit* ("Prigirstis, who makes one hear / enables hearing and listening"). Jan Malecki's *Libellus de sacrificiis et idolatria veterum Borussorum* (1551, BRMŠ Vol. 2) and the *Sudauer Büchlein* (1530) separately record the Prussian/Sudovian hearing and healing patron *Auschauts* (*Aušautas*).
* **Baltic Linguistic Etymology**: Formed as an agent noun compound from Lithuanian *ausis* ("ear", Proto-Indo-European *\*ōus-*) and *klausyti* / *išklausyti* ("to listen out, hear, heed", Proto-Indo-European *\*ḱlew-*), denoting "the attentive listener / the one who hears spoken words".
* **Comparative Folklore Parallels**: Corresponds in Latvian folk dainas (LD 2437-0, LD 33748) to the all-hearing, moral capacity of **Dievs** (*"Augšā tevi Dieviņš dzird"*), who witnesses the cries of orphans, uncovers perjury, and maintains cosmic justice.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic customary law (*paprotinė teisė*) and moral theology, Ausklauts oversees verbal covenants and sensory communication:

### Guardian of Spoken Truth, Sworn Oaths, and Prayer Transmission

* **Divine Witness to Oaths (*Priesaikos*)**: Attends to spoken vows, boundary treaties, marriage covenants, and deathbed promises, ensuring perjury does not escape cosmic retribution.
* **Transmitter of Prayers**: Acts as a sensory mediator transmitting human whispers, laments, and invocations from earth to the supreme celestial deities.
* **Moral and Sensory Balance**: Functions within the specialized communicative tier alongside *Prigirstis* (overhearing/hearing), ensuring justice is grounded in authentic spoken testimony.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folk tradition conceives Ausklauts as an aniconic, omnipresent divine presence:

* **The Attentive Ear**: Symbolized by the human ear, spiraling seashells, and open cupped hands raised during invocations.
* **Whispering Air and Water Resonance**: Evoked through the murmur of wind through reeds and the bubbling voice of subterranean springs.
* **Open Scroll of Truth**: Represented symbolically by verbal covenants uttered under the open sky.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Ausklauts encompasses communal assembly grounds, boundary crossroads, and springs:

* **Sacred Oath Springs**: Clear forest springs where solemn vows and legal agreements were spoken aloud (`sambia-oath-spring`).
* **Village Assembly Stones and Crossroads**: Boundary boulders where community elders gathered to settle land disputes and witness spoken pacts.
* **Sacred Grove Clearings**: Open-air ritual sanctuaries where prayers were uttered directly to the heavens.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Historical accounts and ethnographic studies document precise customary practices:

* **Solemn Oral Covenants**: Sealing agreements by clasping hands and swearing aloud before the sky, invoking divine witnesses to punish deceit.
* **Whispered Spring Prayers**: Reciting petitions into clear running spring water or blowing a feather into the wind to carry prayers upward.
* **Votive Offerings for Healing**: Dedicating flax strands or wax tokens at sacred sites when praying for recovery from deafness and ear afflictions.
