## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Teliavelis (also *Kalvis*, *Dievo kalvis*) is the heavenly smith of the Baltic pantheon who forged the Sun and cast her into the sky, as well as crafting Perkūnas’ thunderbolts and weapons. He is documented in the earliest written accounts of Baltic religion: the 1252 Hypatian Codex states that King Mindaugas sacrificed to *Teliavelis*, who forged the sun and placed it in the heavens. The 1261 Slavonic Malalas chronicle entry confirms his mythic role.

In research by Algirdas Julius Greimas and Gintaras Beresnevičius, Teliavelis is portrayed as the divine craftsman (demiurge) whose smithy produces celestial lights and magical ironwork.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Teliavelis governs blacksmithing, fire, metallurgy, the forging of the sun, divine weapons, and golden rings. His smithy sits at the edge of the sky or atop a sacred mountain.

In Baltic creation myth, Teliavelis forged the sun (*Saulė*) and tossed her into the firmament to illuminate creation. He also crafts Perkūnas’ bronze axes and the silver bridles for the Sons of God (*Dievo sūneliai*).

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Blacksmithing was revered as a sacred art:

- **Sacred Smithy Rites**: The smithy held a semi-sacred status in Baltic villages, and the blacksmith was respected as a holy craftsman mediating between fire and metal.
- **Anvil Consecration**: When installing a new anvil or bellows, smiths poured ale libations over glowing embers, invoking Teliavelis' guidance.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Folk songs describe the divine smith forging golden crowns for Saulė's daughters:
> "Smith, O smith, my brother,  
> Forge a golden ring for me!  
> Hammer out a sunbeam,  
> Weave a pearl from the deep."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Teliavelis' legacy survives in traditional Lithuanian iron cross craftsmanship (*kryždirbystė*) and folk tales of magical smiths.
