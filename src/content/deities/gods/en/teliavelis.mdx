## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Teliavelis (chronicle forms *Teliavel*, *Teliavik*; scholarly *Kalevelis* / *Kalvelis*) is an early Lithuanian god attested as a mythical smith. The name’s etymology is unsettled: proposals include Vėlius’s link to *tel-* (“earth”), Brückner’s *telias* (“calf”) / road-herd god reading, and Toporov’s Scandinavian *Tialfi* loan; the second element *vel-* is widely tied to smithing and the chthonic *vel-* sphere.

He appears in the earliest written Baltic pantheon lists:
- **1252 Hypatian Codex**: King Mindaugas, after political baptism, continues private sacrifices to ancestral gods; *Teliavelis* is named second after *Nunadievis*, with no function stated.
- **1261 Slavonic Malalas chronicle insertion**: Lists *Andajus*, *Perkūnas*, *Žvorūna*, and *Teliavelis* (fourth/last). Here he is called the smith who forged the sun and cast it into the heavens.
- **Fifteenth-century Aeneas Silvius Piccolomini (*Europa*)**: Preserves a related Lithuanian tradition of worshipping the Sun together with an enormous iron hammer that freed the imprisoned Sun — a cult memory of the sun–hammer myth cycle.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Teliavelis is the divine smith of the early Lithuanian pantheon. In the Malalas gloss he forges the Sun (*Saulė*) and hurls her into the sky so that she may shine. Scholarship places him by list-position and by the *vel-* complex in the chthonic sphere, near *Velnias* / *Velinas*, while his sun-forging deed is a cultural-hero / demiurge act rather than proof of a sky-god identity. His domain is mythical blacksmithing and the creation of celestial fire as a wrought object.

**Relations:**
- **Saulė**: cosmogonic product of his forge (Malalas).
- **Andajus, Perkūnas, Žvorūna**: co-listed with him in the 1261 Sovijus pantheon.
- **Nunadievis**: co-listed ahead of him in the 1252 Hypatian list.
- **Velnias / Velinas**: scholarly proximity through chthonic list-position, *vel-* etymology, and folk belief that smithing was first invented by velniai.
- **Kalvelis / Kalevelis**: scholarly name-variant for the same mythical smith (Greimas).
- **Latvian Debesu kalējs** (comparative): Latvian *dainas* preserve a sky-forge motif — forging crowns, rings, and horse-gear for *Saules meitas* and *Dieva dēli*, with coals falling into the Daugava as silver — but Latvian tradition identifies *Debesu kalējs* with *Pērkons*, not as a separate Teliavelis.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Sources portray Teliavelis through strength and craft, not through facial or costume detail:
- **Physique**: A giant / immensely powerful smith (*kalvis milžinas galiūnas*).
- **Held attribute**: Divine / iron hammer (*geležinis kūjis*), remembered in the Piccolomini sun-liberation cult and in the forge myth itself.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

His mythic workplace is the forge that produces the sun-metal. Pantheon lists seat him at the chthonic end of the divine order (second opposite Nunadievis; last in Malalas). The related fifteenth-century notice of Sun-and-hammer worship points to a local cult place in Lithuania.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

- **Royal ancestral sacrifice (1252)**: Mindaugas’s private offerings include Teliavelis among the gods still honoured after baptism.
- **Pantheon sacrifice (1261 Malalas)**: Lithuanians are said to sacrifice to Andajus, Perkūnas, Žvorūna, and Teliavelis the smith-god.
- **Sun and iron hammer cult (15th c.)**: Worship of the Sun together with a huge iron hammer that smashed the tower imprisoning her — a rite tied to the same sun–forge mythic cycle.
