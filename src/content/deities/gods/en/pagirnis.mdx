## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Pagirnis (attested in seventeenth-century historical sources as *Pagirnis* / *dii Pagirnii*) is the Lithuanian chthonic-domestic deity dwelling beneath the household handmill quern stones, safeguarding the grinding of grain, flour blessing, and domestic bread sustenance.

Historical documentation and ethnographic research establish his authentic lineage:

* **Seventeenth-Century Jesuit Annual Reports**: First recorded in the *Annuae Litterae Societatis Jesu* (1605) by Jesuit missionaries in Lithuania, who reported that rural peasants maintained dedicated household enclosures beneath handmills for the domestic deity *Pagirnis*, comparing his agricultural role to Ceres.
* **Baltic Etymology**: Morphological compound of the locative prefix *pa-* ("beneath, under, adjacent to") and the noun *girnos* ("quern stones, handmill" — cognate with Latvian *dzirnas*, Old Prussian *girnoy*, PIE *\*gʷr̥h₂n-*), literally designating "The One Dwelling Beneath the Quern" (*būvantysis po girnomis*).
* **Comparative Pantheon**: Functions within the domestic circle of hearth and homestead numina alongside Dimstipatis (farmstead guardian), Gabija (sacred fire), and Žemėpatis (earth master).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic domestic religion, grain grinding was a sacred threshold activity connecting raw chthonic soil harvests (*Žemyna*) with human culinary sustenance:

### Guardian of the Quern and Household Bread Sustenance

* **Presider over Domestic Flour Grinding**: Safeguards daily handmill labor, ensuring that heavy granite stones turn smoothly, grind fine meal, and do not crack or bind.
* **Protector of Bread Prosperity (*Skalsa*)**: Guarantees the nutritional and spiritual abundance of the family's baked bread supply; neglecting the spirit was believed to cause flour to sour or domestic blessings to vanish.
* **Chthonic-Theriomorphic Manifestation**: Manifests primarily as the sacred household grass snake (*žaltys*) dwelling in the cool earth and stone crevices beneath the quern bench, serving as a conduit for ancestral vitality.
* **Collaborator of Homestead Numina**: Works harmoniously with Dimstipatis at the threshold and Gabija at the hearth oven, forming the complete protective triad of traditional Baltic homestead life.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic domestic tradition perceives Pagirnis through chthonic and animal iconography:

* **Sacred Grass Snake (*Žaltys*)**: Manifests as a gentle, amber-crowned household grass snake coiling peacefully in the dark crevices beneath the lower quern slab.
* **Chthonic Invisible Presence**: Conceived as an invisible guardian spirit burrowed in the earth floor directly under the handmill bench.
* **Granite Millstones and Flour**: Represented iconographically by circular granite quern stones, fresh white meal, and the wooden turning handle.
* **Miniature Enclosure**: Associated with the miniature wooden picket enclosure (*tvorelė*) historically erected by peasants in the quern corner.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Pagirnis is located within the interior architecture of the farmstead:

* **The Quern Corner (*Girnų kampas / Priegirnis*)**: The dedicated handmill corner of traditional Samogitian and Aukštaitian farmsteads (*pirkia*, *troba*, *maltuvė*).
* **Quern Bench Sub-Structure (*Po girnomis*)**: The earth and stone foundation beneath the heavy timber grinding bench.
* **Rural Farmsteads of Kražiai and Samogitia**: Historical 17th-century rural settlements documented in early Jesuit mission accounts.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Historical Jesuit reports and folklore document precise domestic rituals dedicated to Pagirnis:

* **First Flour Libation**: Scattering the first pinch of newly ground meal into the stone base crevices before each grinding session.
* **Bridal Initiation Rite (*Jaunamartės auka*)**: A newly wedded bride had to present a consecrated loaf of bread and a hand-woven linen towel (*duonos kepalas ir rankšluostis*) beneath the quern stone before assuming mistress authority over the homestead.
* **Periodic Communal Sacrifices**: Historical records document three annual major offerings (men sacrificing an ox or piglet, women sacrificing a rooster) to secure continued household abundance.
* **Handmill Taboos**: Strict prohibitions against grinding grain on Sundays, holy festival eves (such as Kūčios), or turning the quern stone backward.
