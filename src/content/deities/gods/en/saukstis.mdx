## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In historical documentations of Samogitian folk religion, Šaukstis is recorded as the divine guardian and governor of swarming pest insects:
* **Chronicle Record**: Explicitly chronicled in Jan Łasicki's 1582 treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* as *Szuksztim*, defined with the Latin gloss *Szuksztim pro muscis* ("Šaukstis for the flies").
* **Linguistic Derivation**: Etymologically analyzed by Kazimieras Būga and Rolandas Kregždys as deriving from the onomatopoeic Baltic root *\*šukšt-* (attested in Lithuanian dialectal *šukštys*, *šukštėti* meaning "to rustle, buzz, swarm as insects"), reflecting the drone of insect swarms, with secondary folk associations to *šaukti* ("to call, summon") or *šluostyti* ("to sweep away").
* **Ecological Niche**: Represents the highly specialized Baltic religious practice of assigning divine oversight to every critical ecological interaction impacting household subsistence.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

Šaukstis presides over the fragile equilibrium between domestic livestock and the insect kingdom:
* **Governor of Pests**: Oversees the activity of flies (*muscae*), horseflies, stable biting flies, and gnats during the stifling heat of midsummer.
* **Livestock Preservation**: Invoked by pastoralists and dairy farmers to keep biting swarms from driving cattle into panics, depressing milk yields, causing flystrike, or spreading livestock sickness.
* **Pantheon Integration**: Operates within the sphere of agrarian protective spirits, working in parallel with *Dimstipatis* (guardian of the homestead) and the Latvian livestock mother *Lopu māte* to preserve pastoral wealth.

## 3. Character & Visuals

The presence of Šaukstis is embodied through atmospheric and sensory tokens of midsummer pastoral life:
* **The Swarm Cloud**: Visualized in folk imagination as the shimmering haze and low, vibrating hum of insect clouds hovering over sun-drenched pastures.
* **Herbal Defenses**: Tangibly associated with fragrant bundles of bitter dried herbs—wormwood (*pelynas*), wild tansy (*bitkrėslė*), wild thyme, and elder leaves.
* **Sensory Ambiance**: Characterized by thick, aromatic smoke from smoldering herbal embers drifting through timber cattle sheds to dispel swarming pests.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

Šaukstis was venerated and propitiated directly within agrarian working grounds:
* **Barnyards and Manure Heaps**: Farmstead middens, compost piles, and stable thresholds where flies gathered and multiplied (`sambian-pest-altar-field`).
* **Cattle Pens and Stables**: Enclosed wooden shelters where dairy cows, draught oxen, and sheep were brought in from midday heat.
* **Summer Grazing Meadows**: Low-lying pasturelands, wetlands, and river meadows where dense swarms attacked grazing herds.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Customary practices addressed to Šaukstis combined apotropaic herbal fumigation with ritual propitiation:
* **Barnyard Smudging (*Rūkymas*)**: Igniting bundles of dried tansy, wormwood, and juniper branches inside livestock enclosures to purify the air and banish biting flies under the god's name.
* **Dairy Libations**: Splashing small draughts of sour whey, skimmed milk, or buttermilk at the corners of the cattle stalls and manure heaps to appease the spirit and restrain the insect swarm.
* **Seasonal Invocations**: Pronouncing customary protective charms at midsummer solstice and harvest time to safeguard herds during the peak insect season.
