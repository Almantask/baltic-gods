## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Perkūno Kulka (also *Perkūno kirvelis*, *Perkūno strėlė*, *Perkūno pirštas*; Latvian: *Pērkona lode*, *Pērkona akmens*, *Pērkona bulta*, *Pērkona cirvītis*) is the sacred thunderstone in Baltic folk belief. The term denotes both prehistoric polished stone axes and fossilized belemnite rostrums, which Baltic tradition identified as literal projectiles hurled to the earth by the thunder god Perkūnas during thunderstorms.

The folkloric, linguistic, and archaeological reality of the thunderstone is documented throughout Baltic scholarship:
- **Lexicography & Folkloric Archives**: Documented in the *Lietuvių kalbos žodynas*, Latvian dictionaries (*Tēzaurs.lv: pērkona lode*), and the folkloric registers of Pēteris Šmits (*Latviešu tautas ticējumi*, Nos. 23337–23340).
- **Academic Research**: Monographed by Jonas Balys in his foundational studies on Perkūnas and by Nijolė Laurinkienė in Baltic mythological scholarship.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, the thunderstone serves as the tangible earthly relic of celestial combat and storm purification:
- **Relic of Celestial Fire**: Conceived as an object imbued with the active power (*sacrum*) of divine lightning, retaining the ability to repel dark forces and fire.
- **Duel with Velnias & 7-Year Resurfacing**: Folklore accounts relate that Perkūnas hurls these projectiles to strike Velnias (or chthonic spirits); the stones that miss sink deep into the earth and slowly rise back to the surface, emerging after seven or nine years.
- **Pantheon & Adversary Relations**: Direct instrument of Perkūnas used against Velnias and malevolent spirits hiding under stones, in hollow trees, or among livestock.

## 3. Character and Visuals

The thunderstone encompasses two distinct physical and morphological forms:
- **Belemnite Fossils**: Conical, cylindrical fossil rostrums (*belemnitai*, *velnio pirštai*) tapering to an acute point, exhibiting amber-translucent, honey-brown, or dark brown hues.
- **Neolithic Polished Stone Axes**: Symmetrical, polished wedge axes and chisels (*keltai*) crafted from flint, diorite, or dark metamorphic rock.
- **Physical Traits**: Peasant belief held that genuine thunderstones remain cool to the touch and resist burning.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Thunderstones were traditionally gathered from exposed geological and agricultural sites:
- **Plowed Fields & River Bluffs**: Discovered in freshly plowed furrows or eroded riverbank strata after torrential downpours.
- **Lightning Strike Grounds**: Gathered at locations where lightning bolts struck trees or open knolls, where topsoil was cleared away by rainfall.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Baltic households utilized thunderstones for domestic protection and traditional healing:
- **Apotropaic Household Placement**: Embedded beneath house thresholds (*po slenksčiu*), tucked under roof rafters (*po spāres galiem*), or placed in granaries (*svirnuose*, *klētīs*) to prevent lightning strikes and ward off wealth-stealing spirits (*pūķis*).
- **Infant Protection**: Placed inside baby cradles to shield newborn infants from nocturnal fright (*išgąstis*) and malevolent eyes.
- **Folk Medicine & Healing**: Scrapings of belemnite mineral powder or water in which the stone was soaked were administered to treat sudden fright, skin eruptions, and cattle bewitchment.
