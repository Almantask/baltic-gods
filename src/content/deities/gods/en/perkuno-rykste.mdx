## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Perkūno Rykštė (also *Dievo rykštė*, *ugninė lazda*; Latvian: *Dieva pātaga*, *Pērkona pātaga*) is the Baltic mythological personification and celestial metaphor for the lightning flash and the rainbow as instruments of divine justice. The term compounds *Perkūno* ("of thunder") with *rykštė* ("whip", "rod", "scourge"), reflecting both poetic weather imagery and the moral authority of the thunder god.

Historical records and linguistic scholarship document this symbolic motif across Baltic traditions:
- **Lexicography and Dialectology**: In Lithuanian and Latvian dialect records, *Perkūno rykštė* and *Dieva pātaga* are documented as archaic folk names for the rainbow (*vaivorykštė*, *varavīksne*) slicing through storm clouds.
- **Folktales and Mythological Studies**: Documented in Lithuanian mythological legends (*sakmės*) where Perkūnas wields a fiery staff (*ugninė lazda*) or cracks his whip (*botagas*) to release rain, and in Latvian mythology cataloged by Pēteris Šmits, listing the whip (*pātaga*) and iron rod (*dzelzu rīkste*) among Pērkons's attributes.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, the lightning scourge represents the active, purifying enforcement of cosmic order:
- **Divine Retribution & Purification**: Represents the celestial stroke that dispels dark forces, purifies the atmosphere, and releases fertile rain onto the earth.
- **Duel Against Velnias**: Folklore relates that the lightning stroke pursues Velnias relentlessly into hiding spots—beneath boulders, within hollow trees, or among cattle—striking down subterranean chaos.
- **Pantheon Relations**: Operates under the supreme authority of Dievas as the active striking hand of Perkūnas, traversing the heavens in a two-wheeled chariot drawn by fiery steeds or goats.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folklore and traditional visual culture express the lightning scourge through symbolic forms:
- **Visual Manifestation**: Conceived as a blinding streak of celestial fire, a zigzag flash across storm clouds, or an arching multi-colored band (*vaivorykštė* / *varavīksne*).
- **Weapons & Attributes**: Imagined as a fiery staff (*ugninė lazda*), an iron scourge (*dzelzu rīkste*), or a celestial whip whose cracking echoes as thunderclaps.
- **Apotropaic Motifs**: Reflected in traditional wood carvings and ceiling beam markings as six-petal solar rosettes (*Perkūno kryžius*) and protective crosses.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

The realm of the lightning scourge encompasses the upper atmosphere and struck landmarks:
- **Atmospheric Domain**: Storm clouds (*debesys*), celestial vault, and the active skies above the Baltic landscape.
- **Perkūnas Oak Groves**: Sacred oak stands across Lithuania and Latvia where lightning strikes marked the presence of the storm god.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Baltic communities practiced specific domestic protective customs during violent thunderstorms:
- **Apotropaic Rafter Crosses**: Carving three crosses (*trīs krustus*) or the fire-cross (*ugunskrusts*) into roof rafters (*spāres*) and building lintels to protect homesteads from lightning strikes.
- **Apotropaic Storm Candles**: Lighting blessed wax candles (*graudulinė žvakė*) in windows during thunderstorms to ward off and deflect lightning from dwellings.
