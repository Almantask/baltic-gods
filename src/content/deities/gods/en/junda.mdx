## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Junda is a Lithuanian teonym popularized during 19th-century Romantic historiography as a goddess of war, martial victory, and warrior courage. The name entered historical literature through Teodor Narbutt's monumental work (*Dzieje starożytne narodu litewskiego*, Vol. 1, 1835), where he synthesized a complete pantheon of Lithuanian divinities inspired by classical and medieval chronicles.

Historical texts and modern critical source analyses document the textual origins of the figure:

* **Hypatian Chronicle (1258 Codex Entry)**: The chronicle records a 13th-century Lithuanian military campaign where Baltic warriors attacked while shouting a battle invocation (*"кличюще янда своимъ богомъ"* / "calling *yanda* to their gods"). The primary chronicle text specifically named the authentic historical deities Andajas (*Ondai*) and Diviriks (*Perkūnas*).
* **Teodor Narbutt (*Dzieje starożytne...*, 1835)**: Interpreted the chronicle's recorded battle cry *yanda / janda* as the proper name of an independent female deity of war and conquest, assigning her sacred battlegrounds, victory emblems, and weapons rituals.
* **Modern Critical Baltic Philology**: Renowned linguists and historians (Kazimieras Būga, Norbertas Vėlius, Gintaras Beresnevičius, Algirdas Julius Greimas) conducted thorough critical analyses demonstrating that *janda* was an authentic Baltic battle-cry or exclamation (related to Lithuanian *judėti*, *jundėti* "to surge, stir, move violently into battle"), rather than an ancient teonym for an independent goddess.

The historiographical trajectory of Junda is thoroughly catalogued by Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, Vol. 1 & 4), Kazimieras Būga (*Rinktiniai raštai*, Vol. 1), and the *Visuotinė lietuvių enciklopedija*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Within the 19th-century Romantic literary synthesis, Junda was conceptualized as the divine personification of martial fury and heroic defense of the homeland.

### Literary Sovereign of Battle Fury and Victory

* **Inspiration of Martial Valor**: Embodied the unyielding spirit of Baltic warriors assembling to defend hillforts against crusader expansion.
* **Consecration of Weapons**: Presided over the pre-campaign dedication of spears, battle-axes, and swords on assembly mounds (*piliakalniai*).
* **Arbiter of Victory and Conquest**: Represented the triumphant resolution of righteous warfare, rewarding heroic endurance on the battlefield.
* **Toponymic Presence in Lore**: Associated in local folklore with strategic assembly mounds and hillfort sites along the Nemunas river defense lines.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Nineteenth-century Romantic literature and artistic engravings depict Junda through martial classical imagery:

* **Armed Warrior Maiden**: Visualized as a formidable, helmeted woman clothed in chainmail or bronze armor, holding a drawn sword and spear.
* **Round Shield with Sun Insignia**: Carries a round wooden and bronze shield adorned with ancient solar rays and column symbols.
* **Wreath of Oak and Laurel**: Crowned with oak leaves, symbolizing victory, steadfast courage, and sacred devotion.
* **Battle Steed**: Depicted riding a swift warhorse across the ramparts of fortified strongholds.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The folklore and literary traditions surrounding Junda associate her with prominent military sites:

* **Jundakalnis Hillfort (Panemunė / Kaunas)**: A historical earthen mound overlooking the Nemunas river associated in local romantic lore with warrior gatherings.
* **Kernavė and Pajauta Assembly Ridges**: Ancient tribal assembly grounds where medieval Lithuanian armies mustered for military expeditions.
* **Frontier Defense Ramparts**: The network of wooden and earthen fortresses guarding the borders of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Nineteenth-century accounts describe ceremonial practices attributed to the warrior cult:

* **Weapon Consecration on Hillforts**: Warriors gathered before military campaigns to hold their blades over sacred fires, dedicating their weapons to victory.
* **Battle Acclamation (*Karo šūksnis*)**: Shouting rhythmic war-cries (*"Janda!"* / *"Andaj!"*) while clashing shields together to instill courage and terrify approaching enemy lines.
* **Post-Victory Spoils Dedication**: Offering a portion of captured enemy weapons, banners, and war gear upon hillfort mounds in thanksgiving for triumph.
* **Vow of Warrior Brotherhood**: Drinking ritual mead from a shared horn before departing for campaign, swearing mutual protection on the battlefield.
