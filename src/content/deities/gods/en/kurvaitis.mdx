## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Kurvaitis (recorded in sixteenth-century chronicles in conjunction as *Kurvvaiczin Eraiczin*, normalized as *Karvaitis* and *Ėraitis*) is the Baltic pastoral guardian of newborn calves and lambs.

Historical sources and comparative philological research document the authentic lineage:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicle Catalogue**: Recorded by Jan Łasicki in *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (1582, published 1615) with the entry: *Kurvvaiczin Eraiczin agnellorum est deus* ("Kurvvaiczin [and] Eraiczin is the god of lambs").
* **Baltic Linguistic Analyses**: Analyzed in Baltic linguistics (Kazimieras Būga, Norbertas Vėlius, Rimantas Balsys, Rolandas Kregždys). Philologists established that Łasicki conflated two distinct diminutive theonyms: **Karvaitis** (derived from Lithuanian *karvė* "cow" and Old Prussian *curwis* "ox, bull", meaning guardian of calves and horned cattle) and **Ėraitis** (derived from Lithuanian *ėras / ėriukas* "lamb", meaning protector of lambs).
* **Pastoral Tradition**: Preserved in sixteenth-century historical lists reflecting the essential animal husbandry vocabulary of Samogitian and Prussian-Lithuanian farmsteads.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic pastoral religion, Kurvaitis operates within the vital domain of spring livestock fecundity and newborn animal care:

### Guardian of Calves and Lambs in the Barnyard

* **Protection of Calves (*Karvaitis*)**: Oversees the health, suckling, and survival of young calves born in early spring.
* **Guardian of Lambs (*Ėraitis*)**: Protects vulnerable newborn lambs and sheep flocks from cold, predatory animals, and seasonal ailments.
* **Domestic Fertility Hierarchy**: Operates in the farmstead barnyard alongside Žemėpatis (patron of the homestead), Dimstipatis (guardian of the hearth), and Gausinė (spirit of cattle abundance).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic pastoral culture conceives these guardians through the gentle imagery of spring livestock:

* **Newborn Calves and Lambs**: Symbolized by healthy young calves resting on fresh straw and frisky lambs in spring pens.
* **Warm Straw Bedding and Byres**: Visualized within the clean, secure timber enclosures of rural cattle byres (*tvartai, veršidės*).
* **Woven Salt Pouches and Bread**: Associated with protective salt pouches and barley loaves used in livestock blessing charms.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The historic domain of Kurvaitis encompasses the pastoral spaces of the traditional Baltic homestead:

* **The Farmstead Byre (*Tvartas, Veršidė*)**: The timber cattle shed and calf pen where cows calved in late winter and early spring.
* **Sheep Folds and Pasture Pens**: Sheltered paddocks where ewes and their young lambs grazed near the farmstead.
* **Traditional Farmsteads of Samogitia (*Žemaitija*)**: Rural agricultural communities where cattle and sheep husbandry formed the backbone of household prosperity.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic communities practiced seasonal protective customs during the spring calving and lambing season:

* **Calving Celebrations (*Atgaugai*)**: Preparing special dairy treats and sweet barley porridge for family and neighbors to celebrate the safe birth of calves and lambs.
* **Apotropaic Salt and Bread Charms**: Placing coarse grain salt and consecrated bread on barn thresholds to shield newborn animals from spiritual harm and sickness.
* **First-Grazing Blessings**: Chanting protective formulas and sprinkling clear well water over young livestock when first driven to spring pastures.
