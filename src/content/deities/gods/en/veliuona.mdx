## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Veliuona (Lithuanian: *Veliuona / Vielona*; Latvian: *Veļu māte*; chthonic goddess of death and ancestral souls) is the Baltic sovereign of the spirit realm, the departed (*vėlės*), and the transitional threshold between physical life and the otherworld (*Dausos*). The theonym is deeply rooted in the Proto-Baltic root *\*vėl-* ("spirit of the deceased, ghost, shade"), originating from Proto-Indo-European *\*wel-* ("to strike, to wound, to die", cognate with Norse *Valhöll / Valkyrie*, Slavic *Veles*, and Lithuanian *Vėlės*, *Vėlinės*, and *Velnias*).

Historical chronicles and comparative Baltic studies record her veneration across the Nemunas basin and Samogitia:

* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum...*, 1582; publ. 1615)**: Documents *Vielona* (*Velluonam*) as the deity of departed souls and funerary offerings (*deus animarum*), to whom ritual banquets were dedicated in honour of dead ancestors.
* **Maciej Stryjkowski (*Kronika Polska, Litewska...*, 1582)**: Notes the ancestral cult and sacred funeral gatherings associated with the historic town and earthworks of Veliuona along the Nemunas river.
* **Norbertas Vėlius (*Chtoninis lietuvių mitologijos pasaulis*, 1987)** and **Gintaras Beresnevičius (*Dausos*, 1990)**: Analyze Veliuona as the core feminine sovereign of the Baltic chthonic realm, operating as the direct Lithuanian counterpart to the Latvian **Veļu māte** (Mother of the Dead).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, death was understood not as annihilation, but as a transition into the ancestral collective (*vėlių bendruomenė*):

### Mistress of the Dead, Ancestral Spirits, and the Otherworld Threshold

* **Guardian of Departed Souls**: Receives the newly departed spirit (*vėlė*), guides it across the threshold of the earthly world, and settles it peacefully in the ancestral meadows of **Dausos**.
* **Overseer of the Autumn Ancestor Communion (*Vėlinės / Ilgės*)**: Governs the sacred autumn period when the veil between realms grows thin and ancestral souls return to visit their living descendants.
* **Preserver of Clan Memory and Moral Justice**: Ensures that ancestral wisdom is respected by the living and that spirits receive their rightful reverence.
* **Chthonic Pantheon Relations**: Operates alongside the chthonic sovereign **Velnias**, the earth goddess **Žemyna**, the fate deity **Giltinė**, and corresponds to the Latvian **Veļu māte** and **Kapu māte**.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folk tradition and mythographic records depict Veliuona with solemn, dignified, and nocturnal majesty:

* **Solemn Veiled Matron**: Visualized as a tall, stately woman with a pale, tranquil countenance, exuding serene silence and timeless grace.
* **Deep Night-Dark Eyes**: Possesses deep, dark eyes that perceive the invisible spiritual world, and long, dark hair covered by a flowing veil.
* **Dark Shrouding Mantle**: Clothed in a heavy, flowing dark blue or midnight-black woolen mantle worn over a white unbleached linen gown, fastened with a silver bow brooch (*lankinė segė*).
* **Guiding Spirit Lantern and Spindle**: Carries a soft-glowing bronze spirit lantern to illuminate the pathway of souls and a carved wooden spindle holding the silver thread of ancestral memory.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred presence of Veliuona is anchored in ancient funerary landscapes and liminal terrain:

* **Veliuona Mounds and Castle Hill on the Nemunas**: The historic complex of earthworks, hillforts, and burial grounds along the Nemunas river that bears her teonym.
* **Ancient Clan Barrows and Tumuli (*Pilkapiai*)**: Pine-covered sandy knolls and burial mounds across Aukštaitija, Dzūkija, and Samogitia.
* **Sacred Cemetery Groves (*Velynės / Alkauskai*)**: Old stands of birch, fir, and oak where ancestors were laid to rest.
* **Village Crossroads at Midnight**: Liminal points of departure where the paths of the living and the departed intersected during autumn festivals.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic communities performed elaborate seasonal banquets to honor Veliuona and welcome visiting ancestors:

* **The Feast of Vėlinės (*Ilgės / Dziady*)**: Families gathered in autumn at dusk, heating the farmstead bathhouse (*pirtis*) and laying tables with warm porridge, roast meats, freshly baked bread, beer, and mead for visiting *vėlės*.
* **Silent Table Setting and Toasting**: The elder of the household solemnly called each deceased forebear by name, pouring libations onto the floor and inviting the ancestors to feast with the family in peace.
* **Grave Mound Candle Offerings**: Lighting sacred fires and candles on burial mounds to warm departed spirits and light their way.
* **Solemn Spirit Escort (*Vėlių išlydėjimas*)**: At the conclusion of the feast, the family expressed gratitude to the ancestors and politely escorted them back to Veliuona's care with peaceful blessings.
