## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Saulė is the Baltic goddess of the sun, light, warmth, life, and fertility. Her name continues Proto-Baltic *\*saulē* / Proto-Balto-Slavic *\*sáuˀlijāˀ*, from Proto-Indo-European *\*sóh₂wl̥* (“sun”). Cognates include Latvian *Saule*, Old Prussian *saule*, Sanskrit *Sūrya* / *svàr*, Latin *sōl*, and related Germanic forms. Folk epithets address her as *Saulelė*, *Saulužė*, and the tender mother *māmuliņa* / *Saulīte māmuliņa*.

Historical sources record solar cult and cosmogony rather than a long theonym list:
- **1261 Slavonic Malalas Chronicle insertion (Sovijus legend)**: People are told to sacrifice to the mythical smith *Teliavelis*, who forged the Sun and cast it into the sky.
- **Jerome of Prague / Enea Silvio Piccolomini (published 1477)**: A Lithuanian community worshipped the Sun and kept a hammer with which the Zodiac signs had freed her from an evil king’s tower.
- **Folklore corpus**: Lithuanian songs and legends and especially Latvian *dainas* preserve the anthropomorphic mother-sun as a central celestial figure.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Saulė governs the bright sky: daylight, warmth, growth, the seasonal cycle, and motherly care. She is protector of the unfortunate, especially orphans. Each day she rises along the solar path, often imagined via a solar tree (*Saulės medis* / *Saules koks*), and at dusk descends toward the western sea, where she washes her horses and rests; Latvian tradition also places her path toward an apple garden (*ābeļu dārzs*) beyond the waters.

Her daughters (*Saulės dukterys* / *Saules meitas*) share household and light-work with her—adorning themselves, weaving cloths with gold and silver threads, and taking part in the heavenly-wedding cycle with *Dieva dēli* (Sons of God). Morning dew and ray-cloths belong to this solar household imagery; the daughters are variously identified with dawn/dusk light, rays, or the morning and evening stars.

**Relations:** Mėnulis / Mēness (spouse); Aušrinė (lover of the Moon in Lithuanian song tradition); Auseklis and Saules meitas (Latvian suitor / bride-theft variants); Perkūnas / Pērkons (judge who cleaves the unfaithful Moon in Lithuanian Rėza tradition; Latvian legends also have Saule herself cut Mēness); Dievs / Dieva dēli (heavenly wedding partners and helpers); horses (solar steeds); Laima (rescuer/helper in some daughter-crossing-the-sea songs).

The core celestial drama explains day and night: Saulė and Mėnulis were wed, but the Moon strayed—loving Aušrinė in Lithuanian tradition, or seizing a Saules meita betrothed to Auseklis in Latvian tellings. Thunder then splits the Moon, and the pair are condemned never to walk the sky together.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folklore depicts Saulė as a radiant maternal woman:
- **Looks**: Long golden or flax-coloured hair; tender motherly bearing (*meilioji Saulelė* / *Saulīte māmuliņa*).
- **Headgear**: Flax-flower wreath or golden crown (Latvian songs also give a silver crown / crown with gold leaves).
- **Clothing**: Long golden garments and a shining cloak (LT); white, gold, or silver shawls (*sagšas*), silk dress, skirts and apron (LV).
- **Accessories**: Brooch (*sakta*) and rings in Latvian dainas; shining ornaments shared with the Saules meitas.
- **Vehicles**: Golden chariot (*rati*) drawn by yellow/golden horses; golden boat (*zelta laiviņa*) across the sea; fishbone sled (*laša kaula kamaniņas*) in some Latvian songs.
- **Symbols**: Solar wheel / *saulutė* / *Saules zīme*; linden (belt hung on the leafy lime); amber as cultural “sun-stone” material in Baltic craft.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Saulė’s domain is the open sky and the solar road over land and sea:
- **Solar path & western sea**: Daily descent into the sea to bathe horses; coastal and maritime imagery dominate Latvian solar dainas.
- **Solar tree**: Morning dwelling in an oak, linden, or apple tree where she adorns her daughters.
- **Cultural landscape**: Lithuanian cross-crafting (*kryždirbystė*) preserves solar rosettes (*saulutės*) on roadside crosses and roof ornaments; the tradition is inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Solar veneration peaks at the summer solstice (*Rasos* / *Joninės* / *Līgo* / *Jāņi*):
- **Solstice fires**: Bonfires on hills; fire-wheels rolled downhill (and toward water or fields) as solar fertility rites.
- **Morning dew**: At sunrise, girls wash in *Jāņu* / Rasos dew for health and beauty and greet the rising sun with songs.
- **Protective solar signs**: Solar rosettes carved on distaffs, architecture, and crosses as apotropaic ornaments.
- **Daily taboos & respect**: Pointing at the sun, mocking her, or finishing work after sunset were treated as dangerous; major journeys and rites were timed to sunrise.
