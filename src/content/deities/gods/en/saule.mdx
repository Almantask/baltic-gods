## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Saulė is the Baltic goddess of the sun, light, warmth, and life. Her name descends from Proto-Baltic *\*saulē* (cf. Latvian *Saule*, Old Prussian *Saule*, Sanskrit *Sūrya*, Latin *Sol*). She stands among the oldest deities in the Baltic pantheon, mentioned in the 1261 Slavonic translation of Malalas' Chronicle during the legend of Sovijus, as well as 14th-century Teutonic Order records.

In mythological studies by Marija Gimbutas, Norbertas Vėlius, and Haralds Biezais, Saulė is portrayed as the benevolent celestial mother who rises each morning over the World Tree and descends at dusk into the western sea to bathe her golden horses.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Saulė governs the celestial sphere, light, motherly affection, vegetation growth, and the agricultural cycle. She resides in a golden castle at the edge of the sky, accompanied by her daughters (Saules meitas) who weave golden solar threads and dew-drenched meadow ribbons.

Her mythological relationship with Mėnulis (the Moon) forms one of the central Baltic celestial myths: Saulė and Mėnulis were wedded in early creation, but Mėnulis strayed and fell in love with Aušrinė (the Morning Star). Enraged by this breach of cosmic order, Perkūnas cleaved the Moon in two. Thus, Saulė and Mėnulis are condemned never to walk the sky together.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Veneration of Saulė reaches its zenith during the Summer Solstice (Rasos / Joninės / Līgo):

- **Solstice Fires**: On the shortest night of the year, wooden wheels wrapped in straw are ignited on hilltops and rolled down into crop fields to bless the soil with solar energy.
- **Greeting the Dawn**: At sunrise on Rasos morning, worshippers gather on hilltops, washing their faces in morning dew to gain health, beauty, and spiritual purity while singing greeting hymns.
- **Solar Woodcarvings**: Solar rosettes (*saulutės*) were carved into roof gables, distaffs, and roadside crosses as protective amulets against dark spirits.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Baltic folklore preserves thousands of solar songs (over 4,000 Latvian *Dainas* and thousands of Lithuanian songs). Saulė is depicted as a tender mother comforting orphans, warming the poor, and guiding lost travelers.

In Lithuanian folk song:
> "The sun arose,  
> Bright and golden,  
> Illuminating the fields,  
> Warming our hearts."

In Latvian *Dainas*, Saulė sails across the sea in a golden boat:
> "Saule rode across the sea,  
> In a golden chariot with silver wheels."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Sacred solar sites are associated with coastal dunes (Curonian Spit, Palanga), Šatrija and Rambynas hills, and ancient stone alignments. Lithuanian wrought-iron solar crosses (*saulutės*) are inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
