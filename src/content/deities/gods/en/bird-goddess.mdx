## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

The Bird Goddess (*Paukščių deivė*, Latvian equivalent — *Putnu māte*) represents the prehistoric Baltic and Old European avian divine archetype, serving as celestial mediator, fate messenger, and carrier of life and soul journeys.

Archaeological reconstructions and ethno-ornithology document the following foundations:

* **Archaeomythological Reconstruction**: Formulated by archaeologist Marija Gimbutas in *The Language of the Goddess* (1989) and *The Living Goddesses* (1999) from Neolithic bird-shaped figurines, bird-headed ceramic vessels, and zoomorphic amber amulets found across the Baltic and Old Europe.
* **Ethno-Ornithological Studies**: Documented in Pranė Dundulienė's monograph *Paukščiai senuosiuose lietuvių tikėjimuose ir mene* (1982/2006) and Rimutė Rimantienė's *Akmens amžius Lietuvoje* (1984), examining the prehistoric substrate of sacred birds as mediators between celestial spheres and human life.
* **Comparative Baltic Context**: In Latvian folklore, this archaic layer is preserved in **Putnu māte** (Mother of Birds, LD 2675-4), who summons and protects winged creatures, as well as in the avian avatars of **Saules meitas** (swans / *gulbji*, LD 26064-1) and **Laima** (cuckoo / *dzeguze*, LD 33596).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, birds are sacred messengers bridging the cosmic realms:

### Celestial Mediator, Harbinger of Seasonal Rebirth, and Fate Prophesier

* **Mediation Between Worlds**: The Bird Goddess connects the upper celestial realm of **Dievas** with the mortal earth, carrying spiritual messages, seasonal vitality, and souls of the departed along the Milky Way (*Paukščių Takas*).
* **Prophetess of Human Fate**: Operates through the voice of the cuckoo (*gegutė / dzeguze*), whose first spring calls announce remaining years of life, marriage prospects, and harvest fortunes as a prophetic emissary of **Laima**.
* **Precursor to Solar and Sky Avatars**: Avian iconography was later inherited by major Baltic goddesses: **Saulė** (waterfowl and solar ducks), **Aušrinė** (the morning star swallow), and **Saules meitas** (swans swimming on cosmic waters).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Archaeological artifacts and folklore depict the Bird Goddess with combined anthropomorphic and avian features:

* **Hybrid Avian-Feminine Form**: Portrayed as a graceful feminine figure with wing-patterned garments, chevron body markings, and beak-like facial contours.
* **Sacred Avian Avatars**: Manifests through the cuckoo (*gegutė*), swan (*gulbė*), duck (*antis*), swallow (*kregždė*), and white stork (*gandras*).
* **Neolithic Adornments**: Associated with carved amber bird pendants (as found in the Neolithic Juodkrantė hoard), clay egg rattles, and chevron-incised bone amulets.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The Bird Goddess is honored across wetland, coastal, and forest nesting territories:

* **Coastal Lagoons and Deltas (Nemunas Delta & Curonian Lagoon)**: Vast migratory resting grounds where flocks gather during spring and autumn migrations.
* **Sacred Groves and Nesting Trees (*Šventgirės*)**: Ancient linden, oak, and pine stands where sacred birds build their nests.
* **Skyways of Migration (*Paukščių Takas*)**: The open autumn skyway through which birds journey toward the celestial south (*Dausos*).

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic communities welcomed migratory birds with dedicated seasonal rites:

* **Spring Arrival Pastries (*Paukštelių kepimas*)**: Baking small bird-shaped ritual bread rolls on the feast of the forty birds to greet the returning migratory flocks.
* **Cuckoo Fate Divination**: Listening with clean pockets and money in hand to the first spring cuckoo call (*gegutės kukavimas*) to ensure prosperity and count allotted years of life.
* **Votive Amber Offerings**: Depositing miniature amber bird figurines in sacred waters and coastal shallows to seek spiritual protection and safe seasonal passage.
