## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Aspelenie (also attested in Lithuanian scholarship as *Aspelėnė*, *Užpelenė*, or *Aslapelė*) is the Samogitian guardian numen of the hearth corner and domestic fire.

Historical sources and comparative philological research document the authentic lineage:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicle Catalogue**: Recorded by Jan Łasicki in *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (1582, published 1615) with the Latin gloss *Aspelenie angularis* ("Aspelenie of the corner").
* **Baltic Linguistic Analyses**: Analyzed in Baltic linguistics (Kazimieras Būga, Walter Jaskiewicz, Norbertas Vėlius, Rolandas Kregždys) as a Polonized rendering of Lithuanian *Užpelenė / Ažpelenė* (from prefix *už- / až-* "behind" and *pelenai* "ashes", meaning "the space behind the ash recess") or compound *asla* ("earthen clay floor") + *pelenai* ("ashes") yielding dialectal *aslapelė / aspelė* ("corner behind the oven").
* **Samogitian and Aukštaitian Tradition**: Preserved in vernacular farmstead architectural lore designating the domestic sacred corner where fire embers were tended and household offerings were placed.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic domestic religion, Aspelenie presides over the inner sanctum of the family home:

### Guardian of the Hearth Corner and the Domestic Serpent

* **Domain of the Ash Recess (*Užpelenė*)**: Oversees the quiet space behind the clay oven and hearth where glowing coals were banked under ashes overnight (*migdyti ugnį*).
* **Sanctuary of the Sacred Grass Snake (*Žaltys*)**: Protects the habitat of the non-venomous house snake (*žaltys*), the living emblem of domestic vitality, good fortune, and family fertility.
* **Domestic Pantheon Synergy**: Operates within the household sphere alongside Gabija (goddess of sacred fire), Dimstipatis (master of the homestead and hearth guardian), and Žemėpatis (patron of the homestead soil).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic vernacular religion represents Aspelenie through the sacred elements of the hearth and domestic animal lore:

* **Theriomorphic Manifestation**: Manifested through the sleek, dark green form of the grass snake (*žaltys*) with yellow collar markings resting near the warm stones.
* **Warmth of Glowing Coals**: Visualized in the soft amber glow of embers nestled beneath grey hearth ashes.
* **Clay and Earthen Hearthstone**: Associated with the smooth clay plaster of the traditional stove and the hard-packed earthen floor (*asla*).

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Aspelenie is located at the center of traditional Lithuanian rural architecture:

* **The Hearth Corner (*Užkrosnis, Aslapelė*)**: The warm recess behind or beside the central masonry stove in traditional chimneyless log homes (*dūminė troba / pirkia*).
* **Under-Hearth Cavities**: Small hollows beneath the stone hearth foundation where domestic snakes nested.
* **Traditional Farmsteads of Samogitia and Aukštaitija**: Historic rural settlements across western and central Lithuania centered on ancestral hearths.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Lithuanian households observed daily protective rites to maintain domestic harmony:

* **Evening Ash-Covering Ritual**: Carefully covering live coals with white ash before sleep with quiet prayers for fire safety and household peace.
* **Offerings of Fresh Milk and Bread**: Pouring small bowls of sweet milk (*pienas*) and leaving soft bread crusts behind the stove for the house snake and corner numen.
* **Taboos of the Hearth**: Prohibiting harsh words, spitting, or stepping forcefully near the hearth corner to maintain spiritual concord.
