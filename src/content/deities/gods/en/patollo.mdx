## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Patollo (also *Patulas*, *Patolls*; often identified with *Peckols* / *Pikulis* / *Pikuolas*) is the Old Prussian god of the underworld and death. The theonym *Patulas* is commonly explained from Prussian *pa* / *po* (“under”) + *tula* (“earth”) — “the underground one.” Early modern writers sometimes conflated Patollo with *Pikulas* / *Peckols* and with related forms such as *Poklius*.

Written record:
- **1418 *Collatio episcopi Warmiensis***: First mention as *patollu* in the Warmia bishop’s letter to the pope, paired with *Natrimpe*.
- **Simon Grunau, *Preussische Chronik* (c. 1510–1529)**: Names Patollo (*Patollo*) as the first of three chief Prussian gods with Patrimpas and Perkūnas, and describes him on Widewuto’s banner and in the Rikojotas / Romuva oak niches.
- **Sudovian Book / 1530 synodal tradition**: Lists *Peckols* (*Pecols*) as a deity of hell and darkness, equated with Pluto.

Grunau’s vivid triad tableau is a sixteenth-century chronicle construction that popularized the three-idol sanctuary; the individual names appear earlier or in parallel Prussian sources.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Patollo rules the underworld and death and is cast as a fierce night power who haunts the living, sometimes frightening a person to death. In Grunau’s system he anchors the end of the life cycle — age and decline — opposite Patrimpas’s youth and growth, while Perkūnas holds the middle of order and storm.

**Relations:**
- **Perkūnas / Perkūns**: triad companion; the eternal fire at the oak belongs to Perkūnas’s niche.
- **Patrimpas**: triad companion and functional opposite (youth / fertility vs age / death).
- **Velnias** (LT): comparative chthonic counterpart in Lithuanian folklore and scholarship.
- **Pikulas / Peckols** (and sometimes *Poklius*): closely related or identified underworld name-forms in Prussian lists.
- **Human, horse, and cow skulls**: his cult attributes before the oak idol (not companion animals).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Grunau and later summaries present Patollo as a grim elder:
- **Looks**: Corpse-pale / deathly white face; aged man (*sirmgalvis*); long grey (*žila*) beard.
- **Head covering**: White cloth wrapped around the head (turban-like burial cloth / *līķauts*).
- **Clothing**: Black or red garments in Latvian and Lithuanian retellings of the banner image.
- **Banner form**: One of three half-figures on Widewuto’s flag — the aged pale figure with the white head-wrap — not a skull motif on the cloth itself.
- **Idol attributes**: Human, horse, and cow skulls set before his image in the oak niche.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

- **Rikojotas / Romuva (Romowe)**: Legendary chief sanctuary of the Prussian triad, placed by tradition in Nadruvia; exact archaeological site remains debated.
- **Sacred evergreen oak**: Trunk divided into three niches for Patrimpas, Perkūnas, and Patollo.
- **Eternal fire**: Sacred fire burned beside the oak as Perkūnas’s sign within the shared sanctuary.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

- **Precious gifts and blood**: Costly offerings and human blood were dedicated to Patollo in Grunau’s account.
- **Appeasement of haunting**: He appears as an angry god who haunts houses; offerings reconcile the offended deity.
- **High priest Brutenis / Prutenis**: Founding *krivis* of the Rikojotas cult who, with Widewuto, institutes worship of the triad including Patollo.
- **Oak attributes**: Human, horse, and cow skulls mark his niche; the snake jar belongs to Patrimpas, the perpetual fire to Perkūnas.
