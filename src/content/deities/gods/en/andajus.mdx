## 1. Primary Chronicle Records (13th Century)

**Andajus** (also recorded as *Andajas* or *Andojus*) is the supreme celestial deity epithet recorded in 13th-century East Slavic and Byzantine historical chronicles.

* **Hypatian Codex (1252 & 1258)**: In the 1252 entry, the chronicle notes that King Mindaugas of Lithuania, despite accepting Christian baptism, privately maintained sacrifices to his ancestral deities. In 1258, during military campaigns in Rus lands, Lithuanian warriors entered battle invoking the paired divine guardians: **"Andaj and Diviriks"** (*Andaj i Diviriks*).
* **Malalas Chronicle Interpolation (1261)**: In the Slavonic translation of John Malalas' chronicle (inserted alongside the myth of Sovijus), Andajus heads the official Lithuanian divine tetrad: **Andajus, Perkūnas, Žvorūna, and Teliavelis**. Appearing first in the list highlights his position as the supreme sovereign of the sky vault.

## 2. Sacred Name-Taboo (*Tabuizacija*)

Modern mythological studies, spearheaded by Gintaras Beresnevičius and Dainius Razauskas, establish that *Andajus* is not a distinct subordinate deity, but rather a sacred taboo epithet (*eufemizmas*) for **Dievas** (the Supreme Sky Father).

Under Baltic sacral traditions, directly pronouncing the true secret name of the highest sky deity during war, royal oaths, or everyday conversation was strictly tabooed (*tabuizacija*). Warriors and rulers addressed the supreme creator using reverent epithets that invoked his transcendent nature.

## 3. Etymological Debates & Scholarly Reconstructions

Linguists and mythologists have proposed several key etymological interpretations:

1. ***an-deivas / *an-daevas* (Būga, Razauskas, Beresnevičius)**: Derived from Proto-Baltic *\*an-deivas* ("the God on High" or "The Primordial God"), describing the distant, all-encompassing sky father looking down from celestial heights.
2. **Ant-dievis (Toporov)**: Reconstructed by Vladimir Toporov as *Ant-deivas* ("Over-God" / "Supreme Sovereign"), denoting the deity who presides over all cosmic and earthly spirits.
3. **Vanduonis & Cosmogonic Waters (Greimas)**: Semiotician Algirdas Julius Greimas associated *Andajus* with *vanduo* (water), positing a primordial creator who organized the world out of chaotic primeval waters.
4. **Angis / Serpent Hypothesis (Volter)**: An early 20th-century suggestion linking the name to *angis* (serpent), which has been universally discarded by contemporary mythologists.

## 4. Cosmic Function & Divine Duality

In the royal pantheon of 13th-century Lithuania, Andajus functioned as the transcendent sky sovereign (*Deus otiosus*), ensuring cosmic order and ultimate fate. He operated in divine partnership with **Diviriks** (a royal title for Perkūnas), who represented active divine justice, thunder, and military power (*Deus actuosus*).

Together, Andajus and Diviriks formed the sacred duality invoked by King Mindaugas' royal court to guarantee both cosmic legitimacy and victory on the battlefield.
