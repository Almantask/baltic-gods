## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Austėja is the Lithuanian goddess of bees. Her name is linked to Lithuanian *austi* / *ausyti* — to weave, to fly swiftly back and forth, and to work the honeycomb as bees “weave” comb.

She is recorded in Jan Łasicki’s Samogitian god-list *De diis samagitarum* (c. 1580; published 1615) as *Austheia*, the goddess of bees (*apum est dea*), whom people called on especially when bees swarmed. That chronicle entry is the historical anchor for the theonym; later writers expand her role from the bee cult, language, and pairing with Bubilas.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Austėja’s core domain is bees. Around that theonym sits the wider Baltic sacralisation of the hive: bees are spoken of with human death vocabulary (*bitė mirė*), nest-trees are treated as holy, and beekeepers share hives in the kinship custom *bičiulystė* (joint care of a stock, mutual support, and shared honey harvest). Honey and beeswax bind the hive to food, medicine, and candlelight in that same bee complex.

**Relations:** Bubilas (paired bee deity / male counterpart in modern scholarly reconstruction); honey bee (sacred cult animal); Ūsiņš (Latvian spring/horse figure sometimes also cast as bee patron — functional parallel, not the same theonym); Latvian bee lore under Dievs (*Dieva kalponīte*, bees called *putniņi* / *saldie putniņi*).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Iconography centers on the honeybee and the woven comb.

- **Symbols**: Honeybee, honeycomb, flowering meadows that feed the hive.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Her sphere is the apiary edge: hollow trees and hive-stocks, meadows in bloom, and Samogitian / Lithuanian beekeeping country named in the Łasicki list.

- **Samogitia (Žemaitija)**: The chronicle frame of *De diis samagitarum*.
- **Stripeikiai Ancient Beekeeping Museum (Aukštaitija National Park)**: Modern commemorative home of the bee cult — outdoor mythological sculpture and an exposition space named for Austėja (*Austėjos klėtelė*).
- **Žemaitiu alka (Šventoji)**: Contemporary Romuva shrine figure dedicated to Austėja as goddess of bees.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Cult practice clusters around swarming and the honey harvest:

- **Swarming petitions**: Łasicki records calling on the bee goddess when swarms rise.
- **Bičkopis / honey-taking feast (mid-August)**: Kin and neighbours gather; secondary rite descriptions have prayers to Austėja and Bubilas before opening hives, water sprinkled on stocks, and the first comb shared with the poor, neighbours, and children as thanksgiving.
- **Bičiulystė observances**: Shared-hive partners visit in spring, support one another, and feast on fresh comb honey, cheese, cucumbers, and barley ale at the harvest gathering.
