## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Devintos (also chronicled as *Dewingis*) is the Baltic mythological hypostasis and personification of the sacred number nine ($3 \times 3$), representing cosmic completion, ritual amplification, and herbal healing power.

Historical texts, philology, and folklore archives record this sacral tradition:

* **Chronicle and Early Source Attestations**: Recorded in 16th-century Prussian and Lithuanian religious descriptions (Jan Malecki / Hieronymus Malecki *Libellus de sacrificiis et idololatria*, 1551/1563, and Christoph Hartknoch *Alt- und Neues Preussen*, 1684, reprinted in BRMŠ Vol. 2) documenting the ritual ninth measure, sacrificial completion, and the personified ninth day of offerings.
* **Baltic Linguistic Etymology**: Derived from Proto-Baltic *\*dewin-* ("nine", Lithuanian *devyni*, ordinal *devintas*, Latvian *deviņi*, *devītais*, Old Prussian *newīnts* / *\*dewīnts*, Proto-Indo-European *\*(d)h₁néwn̥*). The variant *Devingis / Dewingis* incorporates the archaic possessive/denominal suffix *-ingas / -ingis* ("the one endowed with the power of nine").
* **Comparative Folklore Parallels**: Corresponds in Latvian folk dainas and incantations (*vārdojumi*) to the cosmic formula *Trejdeviņi* ($3 \times 9 = 27$), the nine sons of the Thunder God (LD 33704-0: *"Pērkoņa tēvam deviņi dēli"*), and the sacred healing concept of nine-man strength (*deviņvīru spēks*).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmological thought, Devintos embodies threefold perfection ($3 \times 3$) and magical totalization:

### Spirit of the Sacred Nine, Cosmic Totality, and Nine-Herb Healing

* **Principle of Threefold Perfection**: Functions as the mathematical and ritual multiplier through which sacred acts, prayers, and spells reach definitive potency and closure.
* **Master of Nine-Fold Space and Time**: Presides over cosmic thresholds structured in nines—the nine heavenly spheres, nine sacred rivers, and the nine-horned celestial stag (*devyniaragis elnias*).
* **Guardian of Herbal Synergy**: Governs the botanical power of nine sacred herbs (*devynžolės*), conferring comprehensive apotropaic and restorative potency against all maladies.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folk tradition conceives Devintos through sacred numerical symbols and botanical regalia:

* **Elder with Nine-Branched Staff**: Envisioned as a wise ritual elder holding a staff branching into nine shoots or nine-tined antlers.
* **Nine-Herb Wreath (*Devynžolių vainikas*)**: Symbolized by the braided wreath composed of nine distinct healing plants gathered from nine fields.
* **Nine-Pointed Solar Star (*Devyniukė*)**: Represented by geometric nine-point sunbursts and nine sacred hearth stones.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Devintos encompasses herbal meadows, crossroads, and cosmological springs:

* **Nine-Spring Confluences**: Forest sanctuaries where nine subterranean springs converge into a healing stream.
* **Midsummer Gathering Meadows**: Wild flowering meadows visited at dawn before the summer solstice to harvest the nine sacred plants.
* **The Threshold of Nine Gates**: The cosmological boundary separating the earthly world from the ancestral otherworld across nine waters.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Ethnographic collections and folklore archives document established numerical rites:

* **Gathering the Nine Herbs (*Devynžolių skynimas*)**: Women harvested nine distinct plants from nine different field boundaries on Saint John's Eve (*Rasos*) in absolute silence.
* **Nine-Fold Chants and Incantations**: Traditional healers recited spells nine times in succession, tying nine knots in flaxen thread to bind sickness.
* **Devintinės Festival**: Celebrating the ninth week following the spring equinox, marked by blessing the fields with nine wreaths of green rye and birch.
