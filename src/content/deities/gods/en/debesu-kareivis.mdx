## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In the Baltic celestial pantheon, thunderheads and atmospheric tempests were perceived as manifestations of divine cosmic warfare:
* **The Celestial Rider Motif**: Vernacular traditions associate summer storm fronts with celestial riders (*dangaus raiteliai*) commanding the thunderclouds in the service of Perkūnas.
* **The Weapons of the Sky**: Archaic folk belief identified lightning flashes and thunderclaps with divine projectile weapons—flint axes, stone wedges (*Perkūno kulkos*), and fiery arrows propelled from the heavens.
* **Latvian Parallels**: In Latvian folklore and dainas, celestial thunder forces are embodied by *Pērkons* and his sons (*Pērkona dēli*), who ride through the clouds on fiery steeds, forging divine weapons and beating celestial drums.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, thunderstorms performed a vital dual role of cosmic defense and agricultural renewal:
* **Enforcing Cosmic Order (*Darna*)**: The celestial storm vanguard traverses the atmosphere to strike down chthonic adversaries and chaotic spirits attempting to disrupt the world's harmony.
* **The Pursuit of Velnias**: The thunder forces relentlessly seek out the chthonic adversary hiding in hollow trees, under boulders, or within cattle, driving lightning strikes into the landscape to eliminate corruption.
* **Fertilizing & Cleansing Rains**: The tempest is not merely destructive; the torrential downpours following the celestial battle cleanse the air and quicken the soil, ensuring the prosperity of crops.
* **Celestial Mounts**: The riders of the storm are traditionally mounted on swift, tireless grey or dapple horses (*širmi žirgai*), whose pounding hooves echo as rolling thunder.

## 3. Character & Visuals

Folkloric testimonies picture the celestial thunder force with formidable military majesty:
* **The Towering Cloud Warrior**: Glimpsed within dark, towering cumulonimbus clouds as a majestic rider enveloped in storm mists and flashing light.
* **Armor and Flaming Weapons**: Depicted brandishing fiery swords, shimmering helmets, and arrow quivers wrought of atmospheric fire, wearing mantles woven of dark storm clouds.
* **The Thunder Chariot**: Often perceived driving heavy copper or iron chariots whose wheels spark along the edges of cloud banks, shaking the foundations of heaven.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The realm of the storm riders spans the vast atmospheric vault above the Baltic plains:
* **Summer Cumulonimbus Fronts**: Vast, towering storm formations that build up across Lithuania and Latvia during midsummer heat.
* **Sacred Oak Groves & Boulders**: Ancient hills, oak sanctuaries, and erratic boulders traditionally recognized as primary terrestrial landing points for thunderbolts.
* **The Celestial Ridge**: The upper dome of heaven (*Dausos* threshold) where celestial warriors assemble to patrol the cosmic horizon.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Baltic communities maintained elaborate protective customs to greet the storm with solemn reverence:
* **Lighting the Blessed Candle**: During severe thunderstorms, householders lit the consecrated Candlemas candle (*graudulinė žvakė* / *pērkona svece*) and placed it in the window facing the approaching front to ward off lightning strikes.
* **St. Agatha's Bread & Salt**: Placing blessed bread and salt upon windowsills or throwing a piece into the stove fire as an apotropaic offering against house fires.
* **Silence & Reverence During Storms**: Strict taboos prohibited loud whistling, shouting, running, or leaving doors and chimneys open while the celestial battle raged above.
