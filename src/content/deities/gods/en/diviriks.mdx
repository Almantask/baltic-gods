## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Diviriks (Proto-Baltic *\*Deivarīkīs*, "Leader / Sovereign of the Gods") is the royal theonym and sovereign martial title of Perkūnas attested in thirteenth-century historical chronicles.

Historical records and comparative mytho-linguistic research document the authentic lineage:

* **Thirteenth-Century East Slavic Chronicles**: Recorded in the Hypatian Chronicle under the year 1252 as *Diveriks* (*Диверикъзоу*), cataloguing the pagan deities secretly worshipped by King Mindaugas following his baptism.
* **Malalas Slavonic Chronograph (1261/1262)**: Corresponds structurally to the thunder god *Perkūnas* in the parallel royal pantheon insertion listing Andajus, Perkūnas, Žvorūna, and Teliavelis.
* **Baltic Etymology**: Compound of Proto-Baltic *\*deiva-* ("god") and *\*rīkīs* ("ruler, commander, master, leader" — cognate with Old Prussian *rikijs*, Lithuanian *rikis / rykis*, Gothic *reiks*, Celtic *rīx*, Latin *rēx*), literally translating as "Leader / Ruler of the Gods" (*Dievų valdovas / Dievų rikis*).
* **Scholarly Consensus**: Identified as the sovereign warrior epithet of Perkūnas within King Mindaugas's early Grand Ducal state pantheon.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In thirteenth-century Baltic state theology and royal cosmology, Diviriks personifies active sovereign authority, military triumph, and cosmic justice:

### Sovereign Patron of Kingship, Warfare, and Atmospheric Thunder

* **Patron of Royal Power and Military Expeditions**: Invoked by Baltic rulers and warriors to secure victory in battle, protect state sovereignty, and sanctify solemn political oaths.
* **Active Sovereign of the Pantheon**: Occupies the Second (martial/sovereign) Function in the trifunctional structure of Baltic religion, balancing the remote celestial authority of Nunadievis/Andajus.
* **Subduer of Chthonic Antagonists**: Embodies Perkūnas's eternal cosmic combat against chthonic adversaries, defending cosmic order and releasing atmospheric fertility.
* **Entourage of Mindaugas's State Pantheon**: Cooperates within the royal tetrad alongside Nunadievis (supreme remote sovereign), Teliavelis (divine celestial smith), and Medeina/Žvorūna (mistress of forests and game).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Historical chronicles and Baltic mythic traditions portray the divine sovereign through martial and atmospheric symbolism:

* **Stature of Divine Warlord**: Conceived as a powerful sovereign elder with a copper-fiery beard (*rudabarzdis*) and blazing eyes.
* **Thunderstone Weapons and Arrows**: Wields flint thunder-axes (*Perkūno kulkos / akmenėliai*), fiery arrows, and a gleaming fire sword.
* **Rattling Chariot and Fiery Steeds**: Traverses the storm clouds in an iron or copper chariot drawn by swift steeds or he-goats.
* **Sacred Oak Canopy**: Associated with ancient storm-struck oaks serving as terrestrial lightning altars.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Diviriks encompasses state administrative seats and hilltop sanctuaries:

* **Royal Power Centers of King Mindaugas**: Historical thirteenth-century residences and hillforts across Lithuania (Voruta, Kernavė, Vilnius).
* **High Hilltop Shrines and Oak Groves**: Elevated sanctuaries across Aukštaitija where open-air thunder altars stood.
* **Central Sanctuary of Vilnius (*Šventaragis Valley*)**: The historic proto-urban sacred compound at the confluence of the Neris and Vilnia rivers.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Thirteenth-century historical chronicles record specialized royal and military rituals:

* **Secret Royal Sacrifices**: Sacrifices of steeds, bulls, and choice hunt animals offered by King Mindaugas before military campaigns and long journeys.
* **Military Oaths and Consecrations**: Swearing martial treaties and battle alliances under the open sky in the name of the thunderous leader of the gods.
* **Oak Altar Libations**: Pouring beer and dedicating first battle spoils at the roots of lightning-struck sacred oak trees.
