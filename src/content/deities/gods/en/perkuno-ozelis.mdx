## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Perkūno Oželis (also *dangaus oželis*, *bekosas*; Latvian: *Pērkona kaza*, *Pērkona kazlēns*, *debesāzis*) is the sacred mythological bird and thunder herald of the Baltic storm god Perkūnas. The name is an authentic compound combining the genitive of *Perkūnas* ("of thunder / of Perkūnas") with *oželis* (diminutive of *ožys*, "kid / little goat"), reflecting the goat-like drumming sound the bird produces in spring.

The mythological role of the Common Snipe (*Gallinago gallinago*) is documented across Baltic folklore and ethnological scholarship:
- **Comparative Folklore Records**: Documented in Lithuanian folklore archives (LTRF) and Latvian folk beliefs (*ticējumi*) recorded by Pēteris Šmits, identifying the bird as Perkūnas's celestial messenger (*Perkūno pasiuntinys*).
- **Academic Mythological Studies**: Analyzed in depth by Nijolė Laurinkienė in her studies on the Perkūnas complex and by Pranė Dundulienė in Baltic avian mythology.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Perkūno Oželis acts as the atmospheric scout and living omen of the storm god's return:
- **Herald of Spring Storms**: The arrival and display flight of the bird in April and May announces the awakening of the celestial storm realm after winter.
- **Signaling the Unlocking of Earth**: The bird's flight call serves as the primary seasonal omen that the first spring thunder is imminent, signaling that frozen soils will be unlocked for agricultural cultivation.
- **Pantheon & Zoomorphic Relations**: Closely associated with the he-goats (*ožiai*) that draw Perkūnas's heavenly chariot across the clouds; shares a chthonic folk counterpart in wetlands designated as *velnio oželis*.

## 3. Character and Visuals

The physical bird and its mythic perception exhibit distinct characteristics:
- **Zoological Looks**: Identifies as the Common Snipe (*Gallinago gallinago*), measuring 23–28 cm with a long, straight bill and cryptically mottled dorsal plumage of black, brown, and buff patterns.
- **Acoustic Drumming**: During its steep aerial mating dive from heights of over 100 meters, its vibrating outer tail feathers generate a resonant, goat-like bleating sound (*mekenimas*).
- **Zoomorphic Conception**: Conceived in folklore as a chimeric "sky goat" (*dangaus oželis*) carrying the vocal presence of Perkūnas's steeds.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Perkūno Oželis inhabits the wetlands and marshy river valleys of Lithuania and Latvia:
- **Wetland Ecosystems**: Lowland peat bogs, waterlogged meadows, sedge marshes (*žemapelkės*), and river bluffs across the Baltic basin.
- **Breeding Grounds**: Dense tussock meadows and wetland edges where the birds perform their dusk and dawn courtship flights.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Rural communities observed specific phenological signs and taboos linked to the bird:
- **Phenological Sowing Augury**: Hearing the first bleating of Perkūno Oželis in early spring signaled to farmers that the soil had thawed and that spring plowing and sowing could begin.
- **Reverence for the Thunder Herald**: Regarded in archaic tradition as a sacred harbinger whose voice warned of approaching storms, aligning agrarian work with seasonal weather cycles.
