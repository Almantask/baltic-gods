## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Dievas (also *Dievas Senelis*, Latvian *Dievs*, Old Prussian *Deywis* / *Okopirmus*) is the supreme sky father and creator deity of the Baltic pantheon. His name stems from Proto-Baltic *\*deivas* and the Proto-Indo-European root *\*dyeu-* meaning to shine or day sky (cf. Sanskrit *Deva*, Latin *Deus*, Greek *Zeus*). Historical chronicles record Dievas from the earliest accounts: the 1261 Slavonic Malalas chronicle, Martynas Mažvydas' 1547 *Catechism*, Maciej Stryjkowski (1582), and Jan Łasicki (*Deus Summus* / *Okopirmus*).

In studies by Gintaras Beresnevičius and Algirdas Julius Greimas, Dievas is described as the serene sovereign of the celestial sphere (*Deus Otiosus*), establishing cosmic laws and delegating active earthly governance to Perkūnas, Žemyna, and Laima.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Dievas governs the clear day sky, light, wisdom, moral order, and agricultural blessings. He is portrayed as a wise elder clad in silver-white garments, riding a gray horse across the heavens or walking the earth as a humble traveler inspecting crop fields and human hearts.

Dievas has twin sons (*Dievo sūneliai* / *Dieva dēli*), who ride silver steeds across the sky, courting the daughters of the Sun (*Saulytės*) and forging golden rings in the celestial smithy.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Cult practices dedicated to Dievas were connected to daylight, harvest blessings, and hospitality:

- **First-Harvest Elevating Rites**: Before harvesting, the first loaf of bread and the first draught of ale were raised high toward the sky in supplication for Dievas' blessing.
- **Hospitality to Travelers**: Baltic tradition held that any wandering beggar or traveler must be welcomed with extreme reverence, as Dievas often walked the earth in disguise.
- **Field Inspection Blessing**: Prior to spring sowing, elders walked the boundaries of fields asking Dievas for clear skies and gentle rain.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

In Baltic songs and folktales, *Dievas Senelis* (Father God) appears as a compassionate judge helping the oppressed, humbling the proud, and rewarding honest labor.

In Lithuanian folk song:
> "Dievas walked  
> Through the green forest,  
> Bringing fortune,  
> Bringing light."

In Latvian *Dainas*:
> "Dievs was, Dievs remained,  
> Dievs accomplished all labor.  
> Dievs was my father,  
> I am Dievs' cherished child."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Worship of Dievas occurred on high hillforts, open sanctuaries, and beneath ancient oaks. His name remains the foundational word for God across the Baltic languages.
