## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Dievas (also *Dievas Senelis*; Latvian *Dievs* / *Dieviņš*; Old Prussian *Deywis* / *Deiws*) is the Baltic sky father and moral sovereign of the bright day sky. His name continues Proto-Baltic *\*deivas* from Proto-Indo-European *\*dei̯u̯o-s* / *\*dyeu-* (“to shine,” day sky). Cognates include Sanskrit *deva* / *dyaus*, Latin *deus*, and Greek *Zeus*.

Early written Baltic evidence preserves the common noun and theonymic stem rather than a long chronicle biography under this exact name:
- **Elbing Vocabulary (c. 13th–14th c.)**: Old Prussian *Deywis* glosses German *Got* (“God”).
- **Folk and scholarly reconstruction**: Lithuanian *Dievas Senelis* and Latvian *Dieviņš* name the same sky-moral figure in etiologic tales and dainas; medieval Lithuanian chronicles more often list *Andajus* / *Nunadievis* among “first” gods, while Prussian lists head with *Okopirmas* as a separate high-sky title.

In the living tradition Dievas remains a remote celestial lawgiver whose active weather work is carried out through Perkūnas / Pērkons.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Dievas governs the clear day sky, light, cosmic and moral order, wisdom (*padoms*), and harvest blessing. He dwells in the heavenly homestead (*Debesu kalns* / *Dižā sēta* in Latvian song) and may descend to walk the earth as an old traveler testing hospitality.

**Relations:**
- **Father**: *Dievo sūneliai* / *Dieva dēli* (Heavenly Twins / horsemen), who court the Sun’s daughters (*Saulės dukterys* / *Saules meitas*) and forge bridal wreaths for them.
- **Ally / co-ruler of fate**: Laima (Latvian daina formula *Nedod Dievs, nedod Laima*).
- **Earth counterpart (LV)**: Māra as the material / earthly partner in Latvian song cosmology.
- **Functional overlap**: Perkūnas / Pērkons as the active atmospheric executor beneath the remote sky sovereign.
- **Adversary**: Velnias / Velns in etiologic contests and tales.
- **Mounts**: Grey / white horses (*pelēks kumeliņš*, *balts zirgs*) belonging to Dievas and his sons.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folklore depicts Dievas as a powerful elder who looks frail but remakes the world with ease:
- **Looks**: Aged elder (*senelis* / *vecs vecītis*); grey / white hair (*žilas*).
- **Clothing**: Grey cloak (*pelēks mētelis* / *Ar pelēku mētelīti*); also silver mantle (*sudrabiņa mētelītis*).
- **Mount**: Rides a grey or white horse across hills and sky; his sons saddle celestial steeds.
- **Sacred sign**: Upward triangle (*Dieva zīme*) as a folk sky-roof mark.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Dievas is tied to the open bright sky and to high places imagined as his homestead:
- **Heavenly homestead**: *Debesu kalns* / *Dižā sēta* in Latvian dainas.
- **Earthly paths**: Village roads, farm thresholds, and field edges where the disguised elder asks lodging.
- **Named hills**: Folklore toponyms such as *Dieva kalns* (e.g. near Lielvārde / Alūksne) preserve the sky father’s name in the landscape.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Cult memory around Dievas is moral and domestic more than temple-centered:
- **Hospitality test**: Any wandering beggar or traveler must be received with reverence; those who welcome the disguised elder are rewarded, those who refuse are punished.
- **Field blessing presence**: Dainas show Dievs walking the field boundaries (*pa ežu ežām*), pressing grass and lifting rye ears.
- **Harvest and livelihood blessing**: Folk song asks Dievs for good flax, clean bread, and rightful gain earned by labor (*Dievs rokā neiedeva*).
- **Protector of the oppressed**: He aids the downtrodden and rewards kindness and honest work.
