## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In sixteenth-century documentary records of Samogitian religion, Šlaitis is documented as an animistic genius loci of sloping terrain:
* **Chronicle Record**: Explicitly recorded in Jan Łasicki's 1582 treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* as *Szlaitym*, defined with the Latin gloss *Szlaitym pro declivitate* ("Šlaitis for the hillside, slope, or declivity").
* **Linguistic Derivation**: Etymologically derived directly from the Lithuanian noun *šlaitas* ("hillside, slope, incline, embankment"), from the Proto-Indo-European root *\*ḱlei-* ("to lean, incline, slope", cognate with Lithuanian *šlieti*, Latvian *sliet*, Latin *clivus*, and English *lean* and *slope*).
* **Landscape Animism**: Represents the micro-topographical stratum of Baltic nature veneration, wherein specific landscape contours and gradients were acknowledged as possessing autonomous divine guardianship.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

Šlaitis governs the distinct physical and agricultural character of sloping ground:
* **Guardian of Gradients & Slopes**: Presides over moraine hillsides, river bluffs, ravine walls, and terrace slopes where terrain angle alters soil moisture, sun exposure, and wind resistance.
* **Soil Stability & Agricultural Auspiciousness**: Invoked to preserve hillside earth from washing out or slipping under torrential rainfall, ensuring that plowing and grazing along inclines proceed without mishap.
* **Pantheon Relationships**: Operates within the territorial network of natural place spirits alongside *Giraitis* (sacred grove spirit), *Šilinytis* (pine-heath spirit), and *Upinis* / *Srutis* (river and stream current spirits).

## 3. Character & Visuals

The spirit is manifested not through heroic figurative narrative, but through the tactile qualities of hillside terrain:
* **Contoured Earth & Terraced Furrows**: Visualized through the distinctive tiered curves of glacial moraine slopes and contour plow lines.
* **Hillside Flora**: Symbolized by wild creeping thyme (*čiobreliai*), hillside grasses, exposed gravel and clay strata, and dew catching the morning sun on angled slopes.
* **Boundary Slope Stones**: Represented by ancient boulders embedded in the hillside brow, marking natural property partitions and earth anchors.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The veneration of Šlaitis was situated directly on topographical slopes across Western and Central Lithuania:
* **Glacial Moraine Slopes**: Characteristic undulating hills of Samogitia and the Baltic Uplands (`samogitian-hillside-cult-site`).
* **River Bluff Embankments**: High inclined river banks along the Dubysa, Nevėžis, and Minija valleys where pasture met water.
* **Hillfort Slopes (*Piliakalnių šlaitai*)**: Steep defensive gradients of ancient Baltic fortified settlements where earthworks were carved into natural hillsides.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Agrarian customs addressed Šlaitis through practical field observances and protective gestures:
* **Plowing Libations**: Pouring a small offering of ale or sweet milk at the upper rim of the slope before turning the first spring furrow on steep inclines.
* **Slope Stone Setting**: Placing unhewn stones at vulnerable points of the slope to symbolically anchor the hillside against erosion and sliding.
* **Harvest Offerings**: Leaving an unharvested bundle of rye or oats at the steepest section of the incline as an acknowledgment of the slope's spirit.
