## 1. Chronicle Attestations and Historical Context

Cerklicing (Ceroklis) is an East Latvian (Latgalian) agricultural and kitchen hearth deity recorded in early 17th-century historical sources. Jesuit Father Joannes Stribingus documented Ceroklis during his 1606 missions to Latgale (*Relatio de initio et progressu missionis Livonicae*), describing how farmers sacrificed black oxen, black roosters, and ale to Ceroklis for field fertility. Paul Einhorn confirmed the tradition in his 1649 *Historia Lettica*.

## 2. Academic Evaluation

Folklorists Pēteris Šmits and Haralds Biezais proved Ceroklis to be a genuine 17th-century Latgalian agricultural spirit derived from *cera* (turf/grain shoot). Early chroniclers' attempts to link him to Roman Ceres were false folk-etymologies.
