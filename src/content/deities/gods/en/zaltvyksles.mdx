## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In Baltic chthonic folklore, marsh lights and sacred grass snakes form an interconnected mythological domain:
* **Linguistic Composition**: The name *Žaltvykslės* combines *žaltys* (the harmless grass snake, *Natrix natrix*) with *vykslė* (a flickering flame, ignis fatuus, from the verb *vyksėti*, to flicker), denoting spectral bog lights.
* **Jesuit Mission Documentation**: The 1612 annual letter of the Jesuit Order (*Annuae Litterae Societatis Jesu*) records the widespread veneration of domestic grass snakes (*serpentes*), which were sheltered in houses, fed milk and honey, and honored as auspicious domestic guardians.
* **Latvian Parallels**: Corresponds in Latvian folk tradition to *maldugunis* (wandering swamp fires) and the widespread sacred status of the *zalktis* (grass snake) in dainas and folk tales.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

Žaltvykslės represents the threshold between the chthonic underworld and the human world:
* **Chthonic Mediators**: The grass snake acted as the living liaison between the regenerative power of the earth (*Žemyna*) and the welfare of the domestic homestead.
* **Wandering Soul-Fires**: The flickering marsh lights were interpreted in traditional beliefs as unquiet wandering souls (*vėlės*), spirit lights, or supernatural beacons marking hidden chthonic treasures.
* **Celestial Adversary**: In Baltic mythic cosmology, chthonic serpent entities are pursued and struck by the thunderer *Perkūnas*, sustaining cosmic balance between sky and earth.

## 3. Character & Visuals

The manifestations of Žaltvykslės appear in distinctive natural forms:
* **Spectral Marsh Lights**: Characterized by cold, flickering blue, pale white, or greenish flames that dance silently over bogs and wetlands at twilight.
* **Reptilian Form**: Embodied zoomorphically as the graceful grass snake, distinguished by dark olive-black scales and vivid yellow or orange half-moon collars behind the temples.
* **Sacred Accessories**: In folk tales, old guardian serpents are described bearing tiny gold crowns upon their heads, symbolizing subterranean sovereignty.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred ecology of this tradition spans wetlands and farmstead interiors:
* **Peat Bogs & Wetlands**: Extensive marshlands (*pelkės, balos*), misty reed-beds, and riverside meadows along the Nemunas river basin.
* **The Domestic Hearth & Threshold**: Quiet corners beneath floors, spaces behind baking ovens, and threshing barn foundations where household snakes nested.
* **Wetland Sanctuaries**: Remote bog islands and alder thickets known in folklore as meeting places of spectral lights.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Customary practices enforced strict respect toward serpents and wetland phenomena:
* **Milk Offerings**: Homemakers set out shallow clay bowls of fresh milk and honey near hearth corners or threshold stones to invite good fortune.
* **Severe Taboo Against Harm**: Harming or killing a grass snake was considered grave sacrilege, believed to bring sudden bereavement, cattle illness, or fire upon the house.
* **Reverent Avoidance**: Travelers observing marsh lights maintained respectful silence and turned their clothing inside out to avoid being led into treacherous bogs (*paklydimas*).
