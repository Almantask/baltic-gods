## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In historical documentations of Samogitian religion, Vetušis is recorded as the demon and personification of violent storm winds:
* **Chronicle Record**: Explicitly recorded in Jan Łasicki's 1582 treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* as *Vetuszim*, defined with the Latin gloss *Vetuszim pro tempestate* ("Vetušis for the tempest, storm, or violent wind squall").
* **Linguistic Derivation**: Etymologically derived from the Baltic root *\*vēt-* (cognate with Lithuanian *vėtūza* meaning "tempest, storm wind", *vėtra* meaning "gale, severe storm", and *vėtyti* meaning "to toss, winnow in wind"), originating from Proto-Indo-European *\*wē-* ("to blow", root of Lithuanian *vėjas*, Sanskrit *vāta*, and Latin *ventus*).
* **Atmospheric Dualism**: Contrasts with the benevolent, orderly wind presided over by *Vėjopatis*, representing atmospheric energy in its uncontrolled, destructive manifestation.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

Vetušis commands the chaotic and adversarial aspects of meteorological turbulence:
* **Spirit of the Tempest**: Presides over sudden squalls, whirlwinds (*viesulai*), and destructive gale-force winds that flatten ripening grain fields, unroof wooden farm buildings, and capsize fishing vessels.
* **Destructive Seasonality**: Feared particularly during midsummer thunderstorms and late autumn coastal surges when violent gusts brought sudden economic devastation.
* **Pantheon Relationships**: Functions as an elemental adversary to the orderly governance of *Vėjopatis* (Lord of the Wind) and *Perkūnas* (Thunder ruler), closely paralleling the Latvian destructive wind demons (*Vēja māte*'s wild sons and *Vējas pērts*).

## 3. Character & Visuals

The entity is embodied through sensory manifestations of atmospheric fury:
* **The Whirlwind Column**: Visualized as a twisting vortex of wind, spiraling dust, and torn foliage tearing through open fields.
* **Tokens of Destruction**: Symbolized by shattered oak and pine boughs, flattened stalks of rye, flying thatch, and dark, low-hanging storm clouds.
* **Elemental Sound**: Characterized by howling gales through chimneys, roaring forest canopies, and the creak of strained timbers.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The presence of Vetušis was feared across open, storm-exposed landscapes:
* **Exposed Hilltops & Ridges**: Wind-scoured heights across Samogitia where incoming squalls struck settlements with unobstructed violence (`samogitian-tempest-hilltop`).
* **Forest Blowdown Clearings (*Vėjovartos*)**: Tangled tracts of uprooted timber where violent gales tore paths through dense woodland.
* **Coastal & Lake Margins**: Coastlines and large lake shores along the Curonian Lagoon and inland waters where sudden gusts imperiled fishermen.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Customary practices toward Vetušis were propitiatory and defensive rather than worshipful:
* **Whirlwind Calming (*Viesulo raminimas*)**: Casting a pinch of salt or a crust of rye bread directly toward an oncoming whirlwind while muttering protective warding incantations.
* **Storm Barricading**: Fastening barn doors, blessing the hearth embers, and placing iron implements on window sills to deflect the storm demon's destructive entry.
* **Appeasement Libations**: Pouring small sips of dark ale at the threshold during sudden ferocious gales to persuade the tempest to pass by without tearing roofs.
