## 1. Etymology & Historical Chronicles

Medziojna (Lithuanian: *Medziojna / Medžiojna / Medeina*; Latvian: *Medziojna / Meža māte*; Latin/Polish chronicles: *Modeina / Medzioyna / Diana*) is the Baltic goddess of primeval forests, wildlife, and the sacred hunter's chase. The name derives from the Proto-Baltic root *\*med-* (attested in Old Lithuanian *medė*, modern Lithuanian *medis* "tree, forest", Old Prussian *median*, and Lithuanian *medžioti* "to hunt, pursue forest game").

In Baltic mythography and historical sources, the deity represents the hunting and woodland manifestation of the sovereign forest goddess **Medeina** (also paired in 13th-century chronicles with **Žvorūna / Žvėrūnė**, the mistress of wild beasts):

* **Hypatian Codex (1252 / 1258)**: Records that King Mindaugas secretly maintained worship of the ancestral pantheon, making offerings to *Medeina* and *Žvorūna* whenever entering sacred forests or fields.
* **Chronograph of John Malalas (1261 Slavic translation)**: Mentions the Lithuanian veneration of the forest and beast goddess *Zveruna*, glossed with classical Roman *Diana*.
* **Jan Długosz (*Annales seu cronicae incliti Regni Poloniae*, 15th c.)**: Describes the Lithuanian forest goddess under the classical name *Diana*, noting that sacred groves and wild animals were protected under her patronage.
* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum...*, written 1582, pub. 1615)**: Explicitly catalogs her among the forest deities of Samogitia under the name *Modeina* (*"Modeina et Ragaina sylvestres sunt dii"*).
* **Teodor Narbutt (*Dzieje starożytne narodu litewskiego*, 1835)**: Codified the regional literary theonym *Medžiojna / Medzioyna*, depicting her as the specialized patroness of the forest chase and hunting fortune.
* **Comparative Baltic Context**: In Latvian folklore, the equivalent spiritual role is held by **Meža māte** ("Mother of the Forest"), who holds the keys to the woodland pens and regulates game for hunters.

Her theological role, chronicle attestations, and folklore layers have been analyzed by Algirdas Julius Greimas (*Apie dievus ir žmones*, 1979), Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, Vol. 2, 2001; *Senovės lietuvių pasaulėžiūra*), Gintaras Beresnevičius (*Lietuvių religija ir mitologija*, 2004), Pēteris Šmits (*Latviešu mitoloģija*), Haralds Biezais (*Die Hauptgöttinnen der alten Letten*), and *Visuotinė lietuvių enciklopedija*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, Medziojna presides over the untamed wilderness (*giria / medė*), wild animals, and the moral boundaries of the chase.

### Sovereign of the Primeval Forest & Hunting Balance

* **Guardian of Forest Beasts & Carrying Capacity**: Acts as the sovereign protectress of wild fauna rather than a mere servant of human hunters. She ensures the ecological balance of the forest, shielding young animals, pregnant does, and nursing beasts from slaughter.
* **Patroness of the Ethical Chase**: Grants fortune, clear animal tracks, and successful game to hunters who honor the forest code, while concealing animals or leading astray (*paklydimas*) hunters who display greed or cruelty.
* **Mistress of the Wolf Pack (*Vilkmergė*)**: Closely associated with sacred wolves, roaming the wilderness accompanied by packs of wolves and woodland hounds that serve as her sentinels.
* **Sanctuary of Sacred Groves (*Alkai / Gojai*)**: Presides over protected forest sanctuaries where the felling of trees, breaking of boughs, and unauthorized hunting are strictly forbidden.
* **Liminal Boundary Mediator**: Governs the threshold between cultivated agricultural lands (*laukas*) and the untamed wilderness (*giria*), requiring formal propitiation before entry.

## 3. Character & Visuals

Baltic folklore and historical chronicles depict Medziojna through evocative woodland and zoomorphic imagery:

* **The Wild Forest Maiden**: Visualized as a swift, agile young huntress roaming the primeval groves with unbound hair and sharp, vigilant hazel eyes.
* **Woodland Garb**: Clothed in rustic linen shifts bordered with fur trims, or wrapped in the skins of wolves and bears, blending seamlessly into the forest canopy.
* **Traditional Nautical & Hunting Attributes**: Associated with animal tracks, wooden quivers, arrows, hunting horns (*ragas*), and the forest keys that unlock game from woodland thickets.
* **Zoomorphic Manifestations**: Frequently appears in the guise of a she-wolf, a sacred hare, or a magnificent stag moving silently between ancient oaks.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred domain of Medziojna comprises the ancient primeval woodlands and sacred groves of the Baltic landscape:

* **Samogitian Primeval Woodlands (*Žemaitijos sengirės*)**: Dense mixed oak and spruce forests in western Lithuania where hunting rituals and forest taboos persisted through early modern centuries.
* **Sacred Groves (*Alkai / Gojai*)**: Untouched woodland sanctuaries preserved from the axe, where hunters gathered at the forest boundary to seek permission before tracking game.
* **Medvėgalis & Šatrija Forest Belts**: Historic woodland expanses surrounding prominent Samogitian hillforts and sacred hills.
* **Forest Boundary Stones & Mossy Stumps**: Boundary markers along woodland trails that served as natural altars for pre-hunt dedications.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Folk Practices

Historical sources and ethnographic accounts record specific ceremonial protocols performed to honor Medziojna:

* **Pre-Hunt Boundary Libations**: Hunters gathered at the forest edge before dawn, offering fresh bread, salt, or draughts of beer onto mossy stones or tree roots to appease the goddess and secure safe passage.
* **First-Catch Dedications (*Pirmasis laimikis*)**: The first portion of meat, fat, or blood from any harvested animal was dedicated back to the goddess at a sacred tree stump before the game could be divided among the hunting party.
* **Observation of Animal Omens & Taboos**: As documented in 13th-century chronicles, hunters strictly observed animal signs; encountering a hare crossing the trail or hearing an ominous bird cry required halting the pursuit to avoid defiling the goddess's sanctuary.
* **Hunting Code of Restraint**: Hunters were forbidden from taking more game than their households required, leaving portions of grain or salt in winter clearings as reciprocal offerings to wild herds.
