## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Dalia (also *Dalya*, Latvian *Dēla*) is the Baltic goddess of allotted fortune, personal share, portion, and material destiny. Her name stems from the Baltic root *dalis* / *dalyti* (portion, share, to divide). She is documented in 17th-century historical accounts by Jan Łasicki and Matthäus Praetorius as dispenser of personal fortune.

In Algirdas Julius Greimas' research *Of Gods and Men*, Dalia is distinguished from Laima: while Laima decrees overall lifespan and general fate, Dalia dispenses the specific *portion* of material goods, cattle, bread, and luck allotted to an individual.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Dalia governs the distribution of wealth, personal fortune, crop shares, livestock increase, and moral recompense. She is depicted as an elderly matriarch measuring grain or bread slices from a sacred sack.

Every person receives their allotted share (*dalia*) at birth. Generous and hardworking individuals maintain a rich Dalia, whereas greed or laziness causes one's Dalia to depart into the wilderness.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Customs honoring Dalia accompanied crop division and hospitality:

- **The Portion Slicing Rite**: When slicing fresh bread, the first portion was set aside for Dalia or given to a wandering beggar (through whom Dalia tested human generosity).
- **Supplications for Fair Trade**: Merchants and fishermen offered grain or ale to Dalia before trade to secure a fair return.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Baltic folktales feature the motif of searching for one's lost Dalia in the forest to regain prosperity.

In Lithuanian folk song:
> "Dalia, my gentle portion,  
> Where have you wandered?  
> Bring me a handful of grain,  
> Fill my cup with ale."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Dalia's concept survives in Baltic idioms and linguistic expressions regarding personal destiny.
