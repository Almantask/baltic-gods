## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Dalia is a Lithuanian personified fate-being whose name rests on the Baltic noun *dalis* / *dalia* (“portion, share”) and the verb *dalyti* (“to divide, allot”). In folklore she embodies personal success and the material share of fortune allotted to an individual. Tales, legends, and songs preserve her as Laimė–Dalia — the portion of luck that walks with a person through life.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Dalia governs the individual’s allotted *portion* of material goods, prosperity, and household fortune. Folk thought separates two planes of the luck–share complex: *laimė* as the global field of life’s possibilities, and *dalis* / *dalia* as the metonymic share that concerns material values. At birth, Senelis Dievas or Laima assigns each person a proper share, as a father divides an estate among children; Dalia is the figurative form in which that personal share may be dressed and carried rather than the sole decree-maker of lifespan.

**Relations:**
- **Laima / Laimė**: companion, aspect, or close counterpart — Laima states or prophesies fate; Dalia figures the personal material share.
- **Dievas Senelis**: allotting instance who, in tales, answers what *dalys* a newborn receives.
- **Nelaimė / Vargas**: opposing personifications of misfortune and want; in one figurative pair the kindly share appears as a fair maiden while Nelaimė appears as an old woman.
- **Swan (*gulbė*), duck, lamb (*avinėlis*), dog**: zoomorphic dwellings or forms of the personal *laimė–dalia*.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folk tradition shows Dalia less as a fixed temple icon than as a mobile portion of fortune:
- **Looks**: Anthropomorphic woman; the kindly share appears as a *daili merga* (fair maiden).
- **Forms**: Woman; swan; duck; lamb; dog.
- **Dwelling of the share**: The *laimė–dalia* parcel may live apart from the person — in a swan, a lamb, or a chosen woman — and still bind owner and fortune.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Dalia’s sphere is personal and domestic rather than a mapped sanctuary network. Song tradition places the sought Laimė–Dalia “on the other side of the seas” (*anoj pusėj marelių*). Her presence is felt in the household fortune of people and livestock and in the abstract share assigned at birth.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Documented practice clusters around naming, seeking, and petitioning one’s share:
- **Songs of seeking**: Mothers and singers go calling for Laimė–Dalia when the portion seems lost or distant.
- **Prayers for a share of happiness**: Archaic petitions ask heavenly powers for *dalis šcėsties* — a share of fortune expressed as wealth and well-being.
- **Birth allotment belief**: The newborn’s material lot is thought already portioned by Senelis Dievas or spoken by Laima; Dalia is the share that then accompanies life.
