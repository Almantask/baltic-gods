## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Mėnulis (also *Mėnuo*, Latvian *Mēness*, Old Prussian *Menig*) is the Baltic god of the moon, night, warrior protection, and time calculation. His name stems from Proto-Indo-European *\*mēnōs-* (moon, measure; cf. Latin *mensis*, Greek *mēn*, Sanskrit *māsa*). Unlike many mythologies where the moon is female, in Baltic tradition the Moon is masculine—a knightly celestial warrior clad in silver armor.

Historical accounts and folklore record Mėnulis as the divine night counter who guides nighttime military raids, tracks lunar phases, and oversees nocturnal healing rites.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Mėnulis rules the night sky, lunar cycles (new moon, full moon, waning moon), nocturnal protection, and agricultural sap flow. He rides a silver steed across the night firmament, wielding a silver sword.

The phases of Mėnulis governed all agricultural decisions: new moon (*jaunatis*) was reserved for tasks requiring growth and abundance (planting, housebuilding, weddings), while waning moon (*delčia*) was designated for clearing, timber cutting, and pest control.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Veneration of Mėnulis occurred at the appearance of the new crescent:

- **Greeting the New Moon**: Upon sighting the new crescent, travelers and villagers removed their hats, bowed, and recited ancient incantations: "Hail, young King Moon! Grant me health, fill my purse!"
- **Wealth and Health Incantations**: Coins and purses were shaken under the light of the new moon so prosperity would expand in tandem with the waxing crescent.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Baltic mythic songs detail the celestial marriage of Mėnulis and Saulė:
> "Mėnulis wedded Saulė  
> In the first spring of creation.  
> Mėnulis wandered alone  
> And fell in love with Aušrinė.  
> Perkūnas flew into wrath  
> And cleaved him with his sword."

In Latvian *Dainas*, Mēness appears as a warrior leading stars into battle:
> "Mēness counts his stars,  
> Checking if all are present."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Crescent symbols (*rageliai*) remain prominent in Baltic blacksmithing, distaff carvings, and traditional wrought-iron roof crosses.
