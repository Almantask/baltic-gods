## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Aušrinė (Latvian *Auseklis*, Old Prussian *Aušra*) is the Baltic goddess of dawn, the morning star (Venus), youth, beauty, and renewal. Her name derives from Proto-Baltic *\*aušra* (dawn, morning light; cognate with Sanskrit *Uṣas*, Latin *Aurora*, Greek *Ēōs*). Historical accounts document her in Jan Łasicki’s 1582 work as *Aušra*, sovereign of dawning light.

In research by Marija Gimbutas and Haralds Biezais, Aušrinė is celebrated as the maiden of morning light whose arrival heralds the triumph of day over night.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Aušrinė governs the dawning sky, ignites Saulė’s morning fire, and scatters dew across meadows. She is depicted as a radiant maiden adorned with a star crown and silk garments.

In mythic tradition, she forms the celestial triangle with Saulė and Mėnulis. She is also paired with Vakarinė (the Evening Star) as dual aspects of Venus.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Dawn supplications were performed at first light:

- **Morning Dew Washing**: Maidens washed their faces in dawn dew under Aušrinė's light to gain health, youth, and radiant beauty.
- **Auseklis Eight-Pointed Star**: The eight-pointed star motif (*Auseklītis*) was woven into textiles and carved into wood to ward off dark forces.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Folk songs describe Aušrinė preparing the hearth for Saulė's arrival:
> "Aušrinė, bright morning star,  
> Rose early in the east,  
> Kindle the morning fire  
> For mother Saulė."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

The Auseklis star remains one of the most prominent motifs in Baltic weaving, embroidery, and folk symbology.
