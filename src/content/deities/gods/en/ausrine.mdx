## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Aušrinė is the Lithuanian goddess of the morning star (the planet Venus), linked in folk belief with beauty, youth, and health. Her name is formed on the Baltic dawn-light word family (*aušra* / *aušti*, “to dawn”), but she is not the same figure as **Aušra**, the personified atmospheric glow of daybreak. Indo-European dawn cognates (Sanskrit *Uṣas*, Latin *Aurora*, Greek *Ēōs*) attach to that dawn lexeme; Aušrinė is the morning-star derivative. The Latvian counterpart is the masculine morning-star figure **Auseklis** (first written in Georg Mancelius’s 1638 *Lettus*), from the same *aus-* root.

Personified Aušrinė is reconstructed chiefly from song and belief, not from a secure early chronicle teonym. The celestial-wedding plot is fixed in Liudvikas Rėza’s 1825 daina *Mėnesio svodba* (“Aušrinę pamylėjo”; “Perkūns … kardu perdalijo”).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Aušrinė is the morning aspect of Venus: she appears in the eastern sky before sunrise and, in Lithuanian song tradition, kindles Saulė’s morning fire and lights the Sun’s path. Farmers watched the morning and evening stars to begin and end the day’s work.

**Relations:**
- **Saulė**: mother or mistress whom Aušrinė serves; Aušrinė is called Saulė’s daughter and also her morning handmaiden who kindles the fire.
- **Vakarinė**: sister / evening-star counterpart; together they attend the solar household (Aušrinė kindles fire; Vakarinė spreads Saulė’s bed).
- **Mėnulis**: lover in the celestial-wedding myth — the Moon abandons Saulė for Aušrinė.
- **Perkūnas**: judge who cleaves the unfaithful Moon with his sword in the Rėza tradition.
- **Auseklis** (LV): male morning-star *Dieva dēls*, third most frequent heavenly figure after Saule and Mēness; suitor, escort, or wedding guest of a *Saules meita* in daina cycles (bride-theft variants differ from the Lithuanian Aušrinė love plot).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Lithuanian tradition shows Aušrinė as a maiden of extraordinary beauty:
- **Looks**: Radiant beautiful maiden (*nepaprasto grožio mergelė*).
- **Accessories / attributes**: Stars and dew named as her adornments (*papuošalai – žvaigždės, rasa*); comb and rake motifs read in textile and wedding-symbol studies as morning-star signs.
- **Latvian Auseklis dress** (male youth): sews himself brown coats by the sea; silk, velvet, or gold coats in Vāczeme; sews Saule striped gold-and-silver coats and shoes; owns a single horse he buys himself; youngest / least of the heavenly household in Biezais’s reading of the dainas.
- **Iconography (LV ornament)**: the eight-pointed *auseklītis* star motif in textiles and craft, later linked by name to the morning-star deity.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Her natural domain is the eastern pre-sunrise sky, where Venus is visible as the morning star.

- **Aušrinės kalnas (near Kaltinėnai / Kaušai, Šilalė district)**: Samogitian hill remembered in twentieth-century folklore as a place of prayers and burnt offerings to Aušrinė; a local legend retells the Moon’s love for Aušrinė and Perkūnas’s blow that turns the Moon into a crescent.
- **Baltic textile landscape (Latvia)**: *auseklītis* motifs in gloves, sashes, blankets, and related *raksti*.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

- **Hill offerings at Aušrinės kalnas**: Folk memory records prayer and burnt offerings for fortune at first light, when the morning star was said to bless the hill.
- **Dawn work-timing**: Households used the morning and evening stars to schedule labour.
- **Beauty, youth, health**: Folk belief ties Aušrinė to these goods.
- **Auseklis / *Lietuvēna krusts* protection (LV)**: Eight-pointed star marks used as apotropaic signs on buildings and in textiles against harm and night danger.
- **Heavenly bath (LV dainas)**: Auseklis pours steam in the heavenly bathhouse (*garu lēja*) with a silver cup.
