## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Krūminė (recorded in sixteenth-century chronicles as *Kruminie Pradziu Warpu* / *Kruminie Pradziamuarpu*) is the Baltic agricultural deity presiding over cereal tillering (*krūmijimasis*) and the emergence of the first grain ears.

Historical sources and comparative mytho-linguistic studies document her lineage:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicles**: Recorded by Maciej Stryjkowski in his 1582 chronicle (*Kronika Polska, Litewska, Żmódzka...*) and Jan Łasicki in *De diis Samagitarum* (1582, published 1615) as the deity praised during winter rye sowing to grant lush, bushy tillering and full grain ears.
* **Agrarian Etymology**: The teonym is a feminine formation from the Baltic root *\*krūm-* (*krūmas* "bush, clump"), directly describing the agricultural phenomenon of *krūmijimasis* (cereal tillering), where multiple sturdy grain stalks shoot forth from a single sown seed.
* **Baltic Parallels**: Corresponds conceptually to the broader Baltic agricultural sphere of seasonal vegetative mothers and the Latvian *Krūmu māte*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic agricultural cosmology, Krūminė governs the critical transitional stage between grain germination and mature growth, ensuring the vegetative density of winter crops.

### Sovereign of Cereal Tillering and Ear Emergence

* **Guardian of Crop Tillering (*Krūmijimasis*)**: Blesses newly sown winter rye and barley, ensuring that seedlings branch into robust, bushy clumps capable of withstanding winter frost.
* **Presider over First Grain Ears (*Pradžių varpos*)**: Oversees the development of the earliest grain ears, securing plump grain kernels and preventing empty husks.
* **Subordinate Hypostasis of Žemyna**: Functions within the agricultural circle of Žemyna (the Earth Mother) alongside fellow field and grain spirits such as Lauksargis, Javinė, and Dirvolira.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic agrarian tradition conceives Krūminė through living vegetative symbolism:

* **Embodiment of Dense Grain Clumps**: Visualized as the vital sprouting force pulsating through green autumn rye shoots and flourishing spring stalks.
* **Full Grain Ears**: Represented iconographically by heavy, golden rye and barley ears emerging from multi-stemmed grain clusters.
* **Sowing Vessel**: Associated with the wooden grain hopper (*sėtuvė*) and fresh loam soil enriched for autumn planting.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Krūminė encompasses arable grain fields and early seasonal sowings:

* **Winter Rye Fields of Samogitia (*Žemaitija*)**: The ploughed agricultural fields where historical sowing rites were documented by early chroniclers.
* **Sown Furrows and Ridge Tops**: The freshly tilled soil beds where winter rye germinates before first snowfall.
* **Crop Field Expanses**: Arable communal lands across Lithuania where collective agricultural labor was performed.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic farmers performed targeted ritual practices during autumn rye sowing:

* **Sympathetic Magic Poultry Sacrifice**: Worshippers slaughtered hens or chickens characterized by dense, low combs, chopping the meat finely before the sowing bee as an act of sympathetic magic to induce dense tillering and full grain heads.
* **Sowing Consecrations**: Chanting traditional invocations at the opening of rye sowing to ensure bushy germination before the onset of winter cold.
* **First Shoots Observance**: Inspecting the first emerged shoots at autumn's end to divine the strength of the upcoming harvest.
