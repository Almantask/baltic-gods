## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Veļu māte is the Latvian Mother of the Dead and sovereign guardian of the realm of departed ancestral shades (*Veļi*). Her name combines Latvian *velis* (shade, ancestral spirit, deceased soul; plural *veļi*, derived from Proto-Indo-European *\*wel-*, associated with the realm of the dead) and *māte* (mother). Closely related functional titles in traditional dainas include *Kapu māte* (Mother of Graves) and *Smilšu māte* (Mother of the Sand / Sandy Hill).

Historical attestation begins in seventeenth-century chronicle accounts:
- **1636 Paul Einhorn, *Reformatio gentis Letticae in Ducatu Curlandiae***: Documents the deep-rooted Latvian veneration of the dead, ancestral customs, and offerings made to mother spirits.
- **1649 Paul Einhorn, *Historia Lettica***: Records the autumn ancestral spirit season as *Wälla-Mänes* (Veļu mēnesis) and describes the sacred ancestral feeding ceremony (*Dwessel Meley* / *dvēseļu mielošana*) held between Michaelmas and Martinmas.
- **Late nineteenth to early twentieth century Krišjānis Barons, *Latvju dainas* (Vol. 3)**: Catalogues numerous funeral songs (*bēru dainas*) detailing the unlocking of the grave, the laying of the soft bed, and the arrival of ancestral shades.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Veļu māte rules the Baltic underworld realm (*Aizsaule* or *Viņsaule*), envisioned as the peaceful Sandy Hill (*Smilšu kalns* or *Baltais kalns*) beyond the boundary waters. She personifies death not as punitive terror, but as a dignified and natural transition from earthly labor into ancestral rest.

She welcomes newly departed souls at the threshold of the otherworld, unlocks the cemetery gates (*veļu vārti*), and prepares a soft resting place in the sand. During the autumn ritual season (*Veļu laiks* / *Dvēseļu dienas*), she leads the ancestral shades back to visit their living descendants' farmsteads to be feasted, warmed, and honored.

**Relations:**
- **The *Mātes* Complex**: Operates within the chthonic mother spirit matrix alongside **Kapu māte** (Mother of Graves), **Smilšu māte** (Mother of Sand), and **Zemes māte** (Earth Mother).
- **Laima**: Laima weaves individual destiny and decrees human lifespan (*mūžs*); once Laima's allocated thread concludes, Veļu māte receives the departed soul.
- **Māra**: In Baltic funerary eschatology, the physical flesh (*miesa*) returns to Māra and the earth, while the spiritual shade (*velis*) passes into the custody of Veļu māte.
- **Veliona and Giltinė** (LT): Functional and comparative Lithuanian counterparts; *Veliona* (*dea animarum*, documented in sixteenth-century sources) shares the common Proto-Baltic ruler of the shades archetype, while *Giltinė* embodies the act of death.
- **Retinue and Psychopomps**: The community of ancestral shades (*veļi*), the white horse that carries souls across threshold waters into *Aizsaule*, and black fowl (roosters and hens) offered at liminal rites.

## 3. Character and Visuals

- **Form and Bearing**: Manifests anthropomorphically as a solemn, calm, and motherly presence (*baltā māte*) presiding over the ancestral mound.
- **Wearables**: Dressed in white woolen shawls and garments (*balta villaine, baltas drēbes*), embodying the archaic Baltic tradition of white mourning attire (*baltās bēres*).
- **Attributes and Held Items**: Holds the golden or silver keys to the underworld gates (*kapa atslēdziņa*), white linen sheets (*balts paladziņš*), and a ceremonial candle or light basket (*uguns vācelīte*) that illuminates the pathway into the afterlife.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Her sacred domain is situated at the thresholds between the living world and the afterlife across the Baltic landscape:

- **The Sandy Hill (*Smilšu kalns*) / White Hill (*Baltais kalns*)**: The sacred mythological landscape of the otherworld where the dead reside in peace.
- **Ancestral Cemetery Groves and Burial Mounds**: Ancient mound cemeteries (*kapu kalniņi*) and sacred forest groves (*alki*) treated as physical meeting points between living kin and ancestral shades.
- **Threshold Springs and Boundary Waters**: Spring-fed pools and river crossings (such as sacred sites along the Gauja valley and Sigulda) where offerings and floating lights were placed for crossing souls.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

- ***Veļu laiks* (Autumn Spirit Season)**: Observed between Michaelmas (late September) and Martinmas (November). Families thoroughly clean and heat the farmstead bathhouse (*pirts*) with warm water, whisks, and clean linen, or lay a banquet table in the granary (*rija*).
- **The Saimnieks's Address and Silent Feast**: The master of the house (*saimnieks*) formally summons departed ancestors by name, invites them to eat and warm themselves by candlelight in solemn silence (strictly avoiding loud noise or iron clatter), and afterward bids them a peaceful return with prayers for household prosperity.
- **Sacrificial Offerings**: Thick barley or rye porridge (*biezputra*), freshly baked bread, boiled beef or pork, salt, beer, milk, and wax candles left in the bathhouse, granary, or at cemetery mounds.
- **Funeral Grave Offerings**: Depositing coins (tribute to Veļu māte / Kapu māte for the grave plot), bread, linen cloth, and beer inside the grave.
- **Seasonal Taboos**: Strict prohibition during *Veļu laiks* of loud labor, flax spinning (to prevent threads from tangling visiting shades), threshing, shouting, or swearing.
