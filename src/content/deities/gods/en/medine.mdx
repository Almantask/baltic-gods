## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Medinė (also *Medeina*, Latvian *Meža māte*) is the Baltic goddess of forests, wild nature, trees, and game animals. Her name stems from the Baltic root *medis* / *medė* (tree, forest, wood). Medinė is recorded in the earliest Baltic historical chronicles: the 1252 Hypatian Codex states that King Mindaugas worshipped *Medeina*. The 1261 Malalas chronicle entry lists her alongside Žvorūna (goddess of beasts). Jan Łasicki’s 1582 *De Diis Samagitarum* notes rituals dedicated to forest guardians.

In research by Marija Gimbutas and Norbertas Vėlius, Medinė is presented as the sovereign mother of the wilderness who maintains forest equilibrium and punishes greedy hunters.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Medinė governs deep woodlands, sacred oak groves, wolves, bears, deer, and forest berries. She is depicted as a swift maiden accompanied by a pack of wolves or manifesting as a she-wolf.

She is not merely a hunting deity; she is the soul of the forest itself. Hunters entering her realm were required to request her permission and take only what was strictly necessary for survival.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Veneration of Medinė accompanied autumn hunting seasons and spring forest rites:

- **First-Catch Offerings**: Hunters left the first portion of game (or blood libation) upon sacred stones or beneath ancient oak roots.
- **Sacred Grove Inviolability**: In sacred groves (*alkai*), cutting trees or harming wildlife was strictly forbidden to preserve Medinė's sanctuary.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Folktales describe Medinė transforming greedy hunters into stone or trees. In Latvian *Dainas*, *Meža māte* (Mother of Forests) watches over forest creatures.

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Medinė's cult lives on in the dense woodlands of the Baltic region (Labanoras Forest, Karšuva Forest, Sigulda woods).
