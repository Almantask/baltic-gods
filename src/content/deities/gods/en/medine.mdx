## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Medinė (also *Medeina*, *Medeinė*) is a Baltic forest and wild-animal goddess. Her name rests on the Baltic root *medis* / *medė* (tree, forest); Latvian *mežs* and Old Prussian *median* are cognate woodland words. Scholars treat Latvian *Meža māte* (Forest Mother) as a related forest-mistress type rather than the same historical theonym.

Written sources place her in the thirteenth–sixteenth centuries:
- **1252 Hypatian / Volhynian Chronicle**: King Mindaugas secretly worships *Medeina* among other gods (including Nunadievis); the same passage also speaks of a hare god and of a hare crossing the path as a woodland omen.
- **1261 Malalas chronicle insertion**: Lists *Žvorūna* (with Andajus, Perkūnas, and Teliavelis). Later scholarship often identifies Žvorūna with Medeina as one forest–beast goddess under two names.
- **Jan Długosz (15th c.)**: Compares Medeina with Roman Diana.
- **Jan Łasicki, *De Diis Samagitarum* (c. 1582; printed 1615)**: Records *Modeina et Ragaina sylvestres sunt dii* — Modeina and Ragaina as forest deities. Mikalojus Daukša later mentions *medeinas* as forest nymphs.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Medinė’s primary charge is the guardianship of forests and wild creatures. Reconstruction from Greimas onward stresses that she is not a helper of the hunt: she protects the woodland and may hinder those who kill within it. In the thirteenth-century warrior pantheon she is the sole major goddess named beside male gods. Latvian *Meža māte* lore likewise prizes woodland balance and may lead ill-intent visitors astray.

**Relations:**
- **Žvorūna / Žvėrūna**: Often treated as the same goddess under a beast-name (*žvėris*); Malalas’ *Žvorūna* supplies the zoomorphic epithet (*kalė*, read as bitch / she-wolf).
- **Meža māte** (LV): Comparative Latvian forest mother — cognate woodland vocabulary and parallel function within the *mātes* system, not a shared chronicle name.
- **Meža tēvs** (LV): Male forest counterpart who calls birds and beasts beside Meža māte in *dainas*.
- **Ragaina**: Named with Modeina by Łasicki as a fellow forest deity.
- **Wolves**: Escort and companion pack; she may appear as a she-wolf (*vilkmergė*).
- **Hares**: Sacred animals, heralds, and escorts (*šaukliai ir palydovai*); linked with the Hypatian hare omen and with Hare Church sites.
- **Nightingale**: Spring zoomorphic form associated with lingering woodland rites in later reconstruction.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Lithuanian scholarly reconstruction shows Medinė as an unmarried, beautiful huntress-maiden of both divine and demonic traits:
- **Looks**: Young woman; alternatively a she-wolf (*vilkmergė*).
- **Dress**: Beautifully adorned maiden’s clothing in *medeinė* tale material (silk garments as a divine-form marker in Greimas’s reading).

Latvian *Meža māte* folklore (parallel type) adds:
- **Hair**: Long fair hair with flowers, leaves, and berry sprigs braided in.
- **Clothing**: Long seasonal gown — green in spring and summer, variegated (*raiba*) in autumn, white in winter; barefoot even in snow.
- **Forms**: Forest beast, bird, or great tree (aspen “speaking”); the oldest local tree, often a linden, may be addressed as Meža māte.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Her cult belongs to woodland and eastern Lithuanian sacred geography rather than invented tourist forests:
- **Sacred forests and *alkai***: Groves where cutting timber or hunting was forbidden; Advent folklore also warns against felling wood when a grey forest woman weeps in the thicket.
- **Hare Churches (*Kiškio bažnyčios*)**: Stones, hills, and groves in eastern and southern Lithuania (e.g. Genionys / Šilaitis forest near Varėna) tied to hare and Medeina cult.
- **Wolf-footprint stones (*Vilkpėdės*)**: Stones with hollows resembling wolf tracks, likewise mapped to her cult landscape between the Nemunas–Neris woodland belt and related ducal forests.
- **Latvian woodland**: Meža māte offerings at old lindens and stumps; hunter and woodcutter petitions in *dainas* across forest districts.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Source material for a full calendar cult is thin; attested practices cluster around forest taboo and hunter approach:
- **Sacred-grove taboos**: In consecrated woodland, trees were not to be cut and game not to be taken.
- **Hare omen**: A hare crossing the path before the chase could forbid entry into that forest.
- **Latvian hunter and woodcutter offerings**: Before the hunt, Meža māte receives an offering; when cutting wood for a cradle bow, a coin may be driven into the stump.
- **Dainas**: Songs address *bagāta Meža māte*, ask her to favour the trap or journey, and picture her allotting each creature its share (*Lāčam sieksta, briežam blīgzna, Zaķam sīki žagariņi*).
