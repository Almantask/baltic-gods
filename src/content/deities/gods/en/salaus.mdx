## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Salaus (in historical chronicles – *Salaus*, *Szalawis*) is the Baltic numen of sweetness, malt saccharification, mead brewing (*midus*), and honey fermentation.

Historical chronicles and comparative philology document the following foundations:

* **16th-Century Chronicle Attestation**: Recorded in Jan Łasicki's *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (1582 / publ. 1615, BRMŠ Vol. 2, pp. 576, 592, 604): *"Salaus pro dulcamine"* ("Salaus for sweetness / sweet flavour"). Later described in the Prussian-Lithuanian ethnographic compendia of Matthäus Praetorius (*Deliciae Prussicae*, c. 1690, BRMŠ Vol. 3).
* **Etymology & Brewing Lexicon**: Derived from Baltic *\*sal-d-* ("sweet", Lithuanian *saldus*, Latvian *salds*, Old Prussian *saldus*, from PIE *\*seh₂l-* / *\*sh₂ld-*). Semantically rooted in the terminology of sprouted grain and malt mashing: Lithuanian *salyklas* ("malt"), verb *salauti* / *salvėti* ("to undergo saccharification, become sweet in mashing"), and *salduvė* ("sweet ceremonial wort").
* **Comparative Baltic Context**: In Latvian tradition, the domain of honey and sweet ritual drinks is governed by **Bišu māte**, **Medus māte**, and the sacral brewing of sweet festival beer (*saldais alutiņš*, LD 30274-0, LD 30140-0).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic domestic and ritual ecology, honey and sweet malt were revered as condensed solar essence:

### Numen of Malt Saccharification, Mead Fermentation, and Sacred Feasts

* **The Catalyst of Sweet Fermentation**: Presides over the biochemical transition during brewing, ensuring that boiling wort (*misa*) and raw honey convert into rich, fragrant mead (*midus*) rather than turning sour or bitter.
* **Allied Pantheon Relationships**: Operates in close ritual harmony with the bee goddess **Austėja** (social hive order), **Bubilas** (drone abundance and honeycomb yield), and **Rugučys** (fermentation yeast and sourdough acidity).
* **Sanctifier of Feast Hospitality**: Sweet mead was the preeminent ceremonial drink of Baltic weddings (*vestuvės*), solemn treaties, and seasonal banquets, embodying community harmony and divine goodwill.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folk tradition conceives Salaus through warm, golden, and honeyed imagery:

* **Golden-Complexioned Spirit of the Vat**: Depicted as a genial, golden-visaged spirit with amber hair, exuding the aroma of linden blossoms and warm malt steam.
* **Brewing Attire and Accessories**: Clad in linen garments dusted with sprouted barley flour and beeswax, holding a carved wooden mead tankard (*kaušas*) and a stirring paddle.
* **Sacred Attributes**: Connected with the honeycomb (*koris*), the linden tree blossom, sweet clover (*barkūnas*), and flowing golden amber drops.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

Salaus's sacred realm encompasses domestic brewhouses, apiary clearings, and festive feast halls:

* **The Household Brewhouse and Hearth**: The brewing vats, mash tuns, and warm hearthstones where wort was boiled and fermented.
* **Linden Groves and Forest Apiaries**: Wild beekeeping trees (*drevės*) and sunny glades filled with nectar-bearing flowers.
* **Communal Banquet Tables**: The high table of the farmstead where ceremonial horns and wooden ladles of mead were shared.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic beekeepers and brewers observed dedicated libation rituals to ensure sweetness and successful fermentation:

* **The First Wort Libation (*Salduvės auka*)**: Before adding yeast or serving guests, the brewer scooped the first warm, sweet liquid (*salduvė*) and poured it upon the hearthstones or earth for Salaus and Austėja.
* **Autumn Honey Harvesting (*Medukopis*)**: Beekeepers offered fresh honeycombs at the base of linden trees to guarantee hive health through winter.
* **Wedding Mead Blessings**: The wedding couple was greeted with honeycombs and sweet mead cups, invoking Salaus for a sweet, peaceful marital life.
