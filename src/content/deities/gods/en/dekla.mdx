## 1. Etymology and Historical Tradition

Dēkla is the Baltic fate goddess of newborns, cradle rites, and infant fortune, venerated primarily in Kurzeme (Courland) and Western Vidzeme. Her name is etymologically derived from the Latvian verb *dēt* (to lay, place, or nurse) or *dēklis* (cradle / rocking a baby). In *Latvju dainas* collected by Krišjānis Barons, Dēkla functions as a distinct regional fate goddess alongside Laima and Kārta.

Academic scholar Haralds Biezais (*Die Hauptgöttinnen der alten Letten*, 1955) demonstrated that while Laima determines overall destiny (*mūžs*) at birth, Dēkla specifically presides over the immediate post-natal phase, swaddling the infant and securing early health and cradle care.

## 2. Cosmology and Functions

Dēkla protects infants from illness, cradle curses, and early childhood hazards. She is envisioned in folk songs as a gentle, benevolent matriarch entering the sauna (*pirts*) right after birth:
- **Cradle Laying**: Dēkla spreads silk swaddling cloths over the cradle (*"zīda drānas rociņā"*).
- **Post-Natal Sauna Rite (Pirtsāža)**: Midwives prayed to Dēkla during the post-natal sauna cleansing, placing silver coins and wool under the cradle.

## 3. Comparative Mythological Context

In the Kurzemian fate triad (Laima – Dēkla – Kārta), Dēkla corresponds functionally to the Scandinavian Norn *Verdandi* or the Greek Moira *Clotho*, taking charge of the newborn's immediate care and growth.
