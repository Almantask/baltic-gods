## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In historical documentation of Samogitian religion, Tiklis is recorded as a patron of successful endeavors:
* **Chronicle Record**: Explicitly listed in Jan Łasicki's treatise *De diis Samagitarum* as *Tiklis*, defined by the precise Latin gloss *Tiklium pro fortunato successu* (Tiklis for a fortunate outcome and success).
* **Early Modern Synthesis**: Further elaborated in 17th-century Prussian historical compendiums as a divine power through whose favor crops sprout and flourish with fortunate vigor.
* **Linguistic Derivation**: Etymologically derived from the Baltic verb *tikti* (signifying "to fit, be suitable, succeed, or turn out right"), closely related to concepts of belief (*tikėjimas*) and aptness (*taiklumas*).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

Tiklis embodies the Baltic conviction that good fortune arises when human effort fits harmoniously into cosmic timing:
* **Favorable Outcomes**: Invoked at critical transitional moments when an endeavor's outcome is still hanging in the balance—during seasonal sowing, departures on long journeys, and marriage agreements.
* **Agrarian Auspiciousness**: Presided over the timely germination of seeds and the favorable weather necessary for ripening grain.
* **Pantheon Relationships**: Functions alongside the great fate deities *Laima* (destiny) and *Dalia* (allotment of share), standing as the guardian of positive realization against spirits of misfortune.

## 3. Character & Visuals

The deity was envisioned not through elaborate mythic drama, but through auspicious physical signs:
* **Auspicious Tokens**: Symbolized by smooth lucky stones, four-leaf clover sprigs, and the first scattered handful of seed grain.
* **Divination Implements**: Closely associated with carved divination sticks, marked tokens, and cast lots (*burtai*) used to read favorable omens.
* **Gesture of Reception**: Visualized conceptually as an open, welcoming hand ensuring that honest labor finds its rightful reward.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred presence of Tiklis was invoked across the working landscape of Western Lithuania:
* **Cultivated Sowing Fields**: Plowed furrows and arable lands where the first grain seeds were committed to the soil.
* **Thresholds & Crossroads**: Village gates, homestead boundary stones, and departure crossroads where journeys began.
* **Samogitian Assembly Grounds**: Community meeting clearings where barter agreements, trade ventures, and collective decisions were settled.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Customary practices honored Tiklis through practical invocations and auspicious divination:
* **Departure & Sowing Invocations**: Spoken formulas recited before undertaking strenuous labor, setting out on travel, or turning the first spring furrow.
* **Divination by Lots**: Casting wooden lots or stones to seek reassurance that an intended enterprise was aligned with favorable circumstances.
* **First-Fruit Offerings**: Leaving small symbolic gifts—a pinch of salt, a fragment of loaf, or the first grain gathered—to express gratitude for a fortunate harvest.
