## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Apidėmė (recorded in 16th-century historical sources by Jan Łasicki as *Apidome mutati domicilii deum*, the deity of changed dwellings) is the Baltic guardian spirit presiding over homestead boundaries, fence enclosures, and domestic threshold perimeters.

Historical chronicles and critical philological studies record the authentic textual lineage:

* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum...*, 1582; publ. 1615)**: Records *Apidome* as the deity of changed residence, noting that when farmstead animals were born weak or deformed, the family changed their dwelling site (*Habentque Apidome mutati domicilii deum. Nato cuiusvis generis, vel coeco vel debili pullo, actutum sedes mutantur*).
* **Early Baltic Lexicography and Postils (Wolfenbüttel Postil 1573, Jonas Bretkūnas 1591)**: References the term *apidėmė* in connection with old farmstead plots and abandoned homestead grounds.
* **Etymological and Philological Analysis**: The teonym derives directly from the Lithuanian noun *apidėmė* / *apydėmė* (composed of the prefix *api-* "around, enclosing" and the root *dėti* / *dėmė* "place, layer, putting down"), historically designating an enclosed farmstead area, yard perimeter, or ancestral homestead plot.

The authentic history of Apidėmė is critically analysed by Vytautas Ališauskas (*Jono Lasickio pasakojimas apie žemaičių dievus*, 2012), Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, Vol. 2), and Gintaras Beresnevičius.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic domestic cosmology, the boundary fence and gate marked the sacred threshold separating human agricultural order (*sava erdvė*) from the wild forest (*svetima erdvė*).

### Sovereign of Homestead Enclosures and Relocation Rites

* **Guardian of the Domestic Perimeter**: Protects fences, entrance gates, and boundary marker stones from hostile intrusions, wild beasts, and malicious spirits.
* **Overseer of Changed Dwellings**: Presides over the relocation of the household, ensuring that the protective spiritual blessing of the old homestead accompanies the family to their new land.
* **Ally of Domestic Guardians**: Works in harmony with Dimstipatis (hearth-and-home protector), Gabija (sacred fire), and Žemėpatis (earth master) to safeguard the farmstead community.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic domestic folklore and ethnographic traditions portray Apidėmė through protective matriarchal iconography:

* **Watchful Homestead Matron**: Conceived as a dignified, vigilant elder woman standing quietly at the entrance gate or fence boundary.
* **Linen Headcloth and Traditional Apron**: Wears a bleached white linen headcloth (*nuometas*) and a hand-woven checkered apron bordered with geometric protective patterns.
* **Boundary Marker Staff and Iron Key**: Carries an ash-wood boundary staff and a heavy forged iron key symbolizing enclosure authority.
* **Watchful Domestic Companions**: Often accompanied by a faithful farmstead watchdog and a vigilant rooster perched upon the boundary fence post.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Apidėmė is situated within the domestic landscape of Baltic farmsteads:

* **Telšiai and Medvėgalis Homestead Enclosures (Samogitia)**: Traditional Samogitian wooden farmsteads characterized by split-rail fences and carved gateway posts.
* **Homestead Boundary Gates and Thresholds**: The wooden swinging gates (*vartai*) and oak threshold beams dividing yard from road.
* **Corner Boundary Stones (*Ežios akmenys*)**: Natural granite boulders placed at the four corners of the farmstead to mark property limits.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic farming families observed dedicated threshold and relocation rituals:

* **Relocation Gate Offerings**: When moving to a new homestead, pouring libations of fresh milk or beer at the entrance gates to invite the boundary guardian along.
* **Four-Corner Bread Burial**: Placing consecrated bread crusts and salt beneath the four cornerstones of a newly built fence perimeter.
* **Threshold Anointing Rites**: Smearing honey or pouring clean spring water upon the threshold beam before crossing with newborn livestock.
