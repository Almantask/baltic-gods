## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Māra is the supreme Latvian and Baltic mother goddess of the earth, material life, cattle, bread, and female health. Her pre-Christian roots stem from the Baltic root *\*mar-* / *\*mar-ē* (earth, moisture, waters). During Christianization, the attributes of the ancient Baltic Earth Mother (Žemyna / Laima) syncretized with the Virgin Mary, resulting in the powerful, uniquely Baltic figure of Māra.

Academic studies by Haralds Biezais and Janīna Kursīte establish that while Dievs governs the spiritual sphere, Māra governs the physical realm: "Dievs gave the soul, Māra gave the body."

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Māra's domain encompasses the material world, physical health, motherhood, cattle breeding (especially dairy cows), bread baking, and the reception of bodies at death.

She manifests in various animal and natural forms: a black snake, a dairy cow, a woman in a white veil, or a gentle breeze.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Veneration of Māra accompanied every domestic task:

- **Māra’s Cross (Māras krusts)**: A crossed symbol was cut onto raw bread dough before baking to invoke her blessing upon the household food supply.
- **Milk and Butter Offerings**: Spring’s first milk and butter were deposited at sacred stones or springs to ensure cattle health.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

In Latvian *Dainas*, Māra is invoked over 6,000 times:
> "Dear Māra herds the cows,  
> Wearing a silk gown.  
> Protect, Māra, my dairy cow,  
> As you protect your own life."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Māra's sacred stones, springs, and sacred sanctuaries (such as Aglona in Latvia) remain core pilgrimage sites.
