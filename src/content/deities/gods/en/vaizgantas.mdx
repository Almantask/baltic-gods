## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Vaižgantas (also *Wajzgantos*) is the Baltic god of flax, hemp, spinning, weaving, and fiber crops. His name is derived from *vaisius* (fruit, abundance) and *ganyti* / *ginti* (to foster, protect). He is documented in Jan Łasicki’s 1582 *De Diis Samagitarum*, which provides a detailed description of the autumn flax-brewed beer ritual performed in his honor.

In research by Marija Gimbutas and Norbertas Vėlius, the ritual of Vaižgantas is highlighted as one of the clearest expressions of agrarian death and rebirth in Baltic religion.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Vaižgantas rules flax fields, pulling, retting, scutching, spinning, weaving, and garment production. He is depicted as a tall figure standing on one foot amid flax stalks, holding a bundle of flax.

Flax cultivation was revered as a sacred metaphor: flax undergoes intense trial (pulled, soaked, beaten, combed) before becoming pure white linen—symbolizing the purification of the human soul.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

The Autumn Ale Festival of Vaižgantas:

- **The One-Legged Standing Rite**: During the festival, the tallest maiden stood on a bench on one leg, holding high a bundle of flax and ritual cakes, praying: "God Vaižgantas, grow our flax long and tall, so we may be clean and well-clad!"
- **Ale Libations**: Following the prayer, ale was poured upon the floor as a libation, and ritual flax-cakes were shared among all present.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Baltic folk songs portray flax as a brother enduring trials:
> "O flax, my gentle brother,  
> You endured so many trials:  
> They pulled you, they soaked you,  
> They scutched and combed your stalks."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Vaižgantas' cult lives on in traditional Baltic linen weaving and autumn harvesting heritage.
