## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Vaižgantas (Łasicki *Waizganthos*; also *Vaisgamta* / *Vaisgamtis* in later Lithuanian tradition) is the Lithuanian god of flax and hemp. Matthäus Praetorius interpreted the Łasicki form as *Vaisgamtis*, from the phrase *vaisius gauti* (“to obtain fruit / yield”).

Written sources:
- **Jan Łasicki, *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum*** (composed about 1582; printed 1615): Records Samogitian girls honouring *Waizganthos* on the third day after Ilgės so that, by his favour, they may have flax and hemp; he transmits the autumn one-legged maiden rite with cakes (*sikės*) and ale.
- **Matthäus Praetorius (*Deliciae Prussicae*)**: Describes him as a fertility and abundance deity to whom men and women offer a cock and a lamb.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Vaižgantas governs the growth and yield of flax and hemp — the fibre plants from which Baltic households made clothing and household textiles. His cult belongs to the agrarian autumn cycle after the harvest is gathered.

Flax processing (*lino kančia*, “the passion of the flax”) is mythologized in connection with him: the plant is pulled, soaked, beaten, and combed before becoming linen. In that cycle the god is born from the earth in flax form, suffers, dies, and rises again from the earth.

**Relations:**
- **Flax and hemp**: sacred fibre plants of his domain and the focus of the Ilgės-tide rite.
- **Cock and lamb**: zoomorphic offerings attested in the Praetorius tradition.
- **Companion *dievukai***: minor divine companions fed with the scattered cakes in Łasicki’s rite description.

## 3. Character and Visuals

No independent portrait of the god’s hair, eyes, beard, clothing, or armour survives in the chronicles. Cult imagery centres on the fibre plant and the ritual props of the maiden rite:
- **Sacred plant**: Flax (and hemp) as living stalk and fibre.
- **Ritual attributes**: Bench or chair; apron filled with *sikės* cakes; ale jug; broad lime or elm bast (*karna*) held high in the rite.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

- **Samogitia (Žemaitija)**: Primary written setting of the cult in Łasicki’s Samogitian treatise.
- **Flax fields after harvest**: The autumn rite is performed once the fields have been cleared, on the third day after Ilgės (late October / early November in the ethnographic calendar).
- **Household ritual space**: The one-legged offering is staged on a chair or bench indoors among the gathered maidens, not at a named temple precinct.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

- **Third day after Ilgės**: Maidens honour Vaižgantas for flax and hemp.
- **One-legged standing rite**: The tallest maiden stands on one foot on a chair or stool; her apron holds *sikės* cakes; one hand raises lime or elm bast, the other holds an ale jug.
- **Prayer for tall flax**: She asks the god to grow flax as tall as she is and not to let the people go naked / poorly clad.
- **Ale libation**: After drinking from the jug, she refills it and pours ale onto the ground for the deity.
- **Cake offering**: The cakes are cast down so that Vaižgantas and any companion *dievukai* may eat.
- **Harvest omen**: If she keeps her balance, the next year’s flax crop is expected to be good; if she falls or steadies herself with the other foot, the harvest is doubted.
- **Later blood offerings**: Cock and lamb offered by men and women in the Praetorius account.
