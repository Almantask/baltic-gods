## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Atlaibos (recorded in sixteenth-century chronicles as *Atlaibos* / *Atłajbos*) is a Samogitian deity documented in early modern historical catalogues of Baltic religion.

Historical sources and comparative philological research record the following:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicle Catalogue**: Recorded by Jan Łasicki in his treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (composed circa 1582, published 1615) among the agricultural and communal deities venerated by the Samogitians (*Žemaičiai*).
* **Baltic Linguistic Analyses**: Analyzed by twentieth- and twenty-first-century linguists (Kazimieras Būga, Norbertas Vėlius, and Rolandas Kregždys), who trace the theonym to Baltic verbal and nominal roots linked to seasonal respite, easing of agricultural labor (*atlaida* / *atlaidai*), or ceremonial post-harvest gatherings.
* **Samogitian Tradition**: Preserved in historical lists reflecting early modern rural Samogitian folk religion and communal village customs.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In sixteenth-century Baltic folk religion, Atlaibos occupies a specialized place within the communal agrarian pantheon:

### Presider over Communal Respite and Agricultural Relief

* **Guardian of Seasonal Respite (*Atlaida*)**: Associated with periods of rest and relief following intensive agricultural labor (such as haymaking and rye harvesting).
* **Patron of Communal Village Harmony**: Functions within the social sphere of rural gatherings where neighbors assemble for mutual aid and festive rest.
* **Sphere of Agrarian Relief**: Operates alongside agricultural deities governing the seasonal rhythms of farm labor and communal celebration.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Historical chronicles and comparative folklore associate Atlaibos with festive gathering and seasonal rest:

* **Embodiment of Village Respite**: Visualized through the quiet tranquility of fields following the completion of communal labor.
* **Communal Festivity**: Associated with simple rural linen attire worn during post-labor village gatherings.
* **Harvest Offerings**: Iconographically linked to shared food, freshly brewed beer, and seasonal farmstead blessings.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The historic sphere of Atlaibos is situated in the rural landscapes of western Lithuania:

* **Rural Farmsteads of Samogitia (*Žemaitija*)**: Historical sixteenth-century village communities where chroniclers documented traditional pagan survivals.
* **Village Common Lands and Assembly Grounds**: Gathering sites where agrarian communities converged after seasonal work.
* **Harvest Field Borders**: The resting places at field boundaries where reapers rested after labor.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic communities observed communal customs celebrating labor completion:

* **Post-Harvest Feasting and Libations**: Sharing celebratory beer and bread upon concluding major agricultural seasons.
* **Seasonal Invocations for Respite**: Chanting traditional blessings and formulas during communal village assemblies.
* **Communal Hospitality Rites**: Honoring mutual assistance and hospitality among neighboring farmsteads.
