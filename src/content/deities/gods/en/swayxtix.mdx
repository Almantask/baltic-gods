## 1. Etymology & Historical Chronicles

Swayxtix (Old Prussian: *Swayxtix / Suwayxtix / Swaxtix / Suaixtix / Swaixte*; Lithuanian: *Svaikstikas / Švaistikas*; Latvian: *Svaikstiks / Zvaigždtiks*) is the Old Prussian deity of celestial daylight, solar radiation, and illumination. The theonym is an authentic Western Baltic derivative formed from the root *\*swāigst-* (directly attested in the Old Prussian III Catechism as the accusative noun *swāigstan*, meaning "light, radiance, sheen" < Proto-Baltic *\*śvāigstā-* < Proto-Indo-European *\*(s)ḱweit- / \*(s)ḱweyt-*, "to shine, bright, white"). It is cognate with Lithuanian *šviesa* ("light"), *šviesti* ("to shine"), *švytėti* ("to gleam"), *švaistyti* ("to radiate, flash"), and Latvian *švīst / svīst* ("to dawn, grow light"). The theonym literally translates to "The Light-Giver", "The Radiant One", or "The Illuminator".

In Baltic comparative cosmology, Swayxtix personifies active celestial light and daytime illumination, serving as a dynamic generative power essential for life, growth, and the agricultural cycle.

The deity is documented across primary 16th-century Prussian historical records:

* **Constitutiones Synodales (1530)**: Decreed in the synodal statutes of the Sambian and Pomesanian dioceses under Bishops Georg von Polentz, Erhard von Queis, and Paul Speratus, listing him as *"Suaixtix / Swaixte"*, glossed with classical Roman *Sol*.
* **Sudauer Büchlein (*Sudovian Book*, c. 1520s–1530, printed 1561)**: Records him in Position #2: *"Suwaixtix [Swayxtix], der ander Gott des Liechts / Sol"*.
* **Jan Malecki (*Libellus de sacrificiis et idolatria veterum Borussorum*, 1551/1563)**: Lists him as *"Swayxtix deus lucis"*.
* **Maciej Stryjkowski (*Kronika Polska, Litewska, Żmódzka...*, 1582)**: Documents *Swaistiks* as the deity of celestial daylight.
* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum...*, written c. 1582, pub. 1615)**: Incorporates Prussian inventories of light veneration.

His linguistic etymology, liturgical placement, and functional morphology have been analyzed in studies by Vladimir Toporov (*Prusskij Jazyk*), Vytautas Mažiulis (*Prūsų kalbos etimologijos žodynas*), Rolandas Kregždys (*Baltų mitologemų etimologijos žodynas II*), Gintaras Beresnevičius (*Prūsų religija ir mitologija*, 2004), Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, Vol. 2), Wilhelm Mannhardt (*Letto-Preussische Götterlehre*), and *Visuotinė lietuvių enciklopedija*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In Old Prussian theology, Swayxtix occupies the second rank in the celestial hierarchy, governing the active illumination that sustains life on earth.

### Sovereign of Celestial Daylight & Agrarian Vitality

* **Position #2 in the Pantheon**: Positioned directly below the supreme sky creator **Okopirmus** and above the aquatic and terrestrial deities (**Auschauts**, **Autrimpus**, **Potrimpus**, **Bardoayts**, **Perkūnas**), representing the active light radiating from the celestial sphere.
* **Catalyst of Agricultural Ripening**: Historical chronicles record that Prussian farmers prayed to Swayxtix to grant illuminating warmth and daylight (*schein*), ensuring that cereal crops (*Getreide*), pasture grass (*Graß*), and domestic herds (*Vieh*) flourished and ripened.
* **Mediator of Cosmic Energy**: Acts as a generative intermediary force between the transcendent firmament of Okopirmus and earthly vegetation, providing the daily biological catalyst for crops.
* **Dispeller of Gloom and Frost**: Banishes nocturnal darkness, unseasonal cold, and damp chthonic decay associated with winter and the underworld.

## 3. Character & Visuals

Baltic religious tradition conceptualizes Swayxtix through aniconic natural light and traditional solar symbolism:

* **Aniconic Radiance**: Veneration was directed toward the living phenomenon of daylight, dawn illumination, and direct sunbeams rather than anthropomorphic statues.
* **Manifestations of Morning Light**: Manifests in the golden shafts of morning sunlight breaking across the eastern horizon and the clear glow of the noon sky.
* **Sacred Geometric Symbols**: Evoked in Baltic folk textiles, woodwork, and metalcraft through radial sunbursts, rosettes, multi-pointed star glyphs, and wheel motifs.
* **Attributes of Light**: Symbolized by the illuminating sun-disc, morning dawn fires, and radiant solar beams.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred geography of Swayxtix encompasses the daytime atmosphere and Western Baltic agricultural landscapes:

* **The Daytime Sky (*Coelum / Dangus*)**: The illuminated celestial dome and eastern horizon where the dawn first appears.
* **Sambia Peninsula (*Semba / Samland*)**: The coastal territory where 16th-century Lutheran synods documented the active reverence of *Suaixtix / Swaixte*.
* **Sudovia (*Sūduva / Sudauen*)**: The agrarian heartland whose communal prayers for crop-ripening light were preserved in the *Sudovian Book*.
* **Sunrise Sanctuary Ridges (*Alkakalniai*)**: High open ridges and eastern hillslopes providing unobstructed sightlines of the rising morning sun.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Practices

Historical sources describe the distinct liturgical role of Swayxtix in Baltic agrarian ceremonies:

* **Second Liturgical Invocation**: In communal gatherings (such as the *Ožys / Bockheiligung* festival recorded in the *Sudovian Book*), the presiding priest (*Wurschait / Vuršaitis*) recited the pantheon list, addressing Swayxtix in the second position immediately following Okopirmus.
* **The Second Ceremonial Libation**: The second ceremonial cup of mead or beer was poured out to Swayxtix, accompanied by petitions for favorable sunlight (*schein*) to bless the growing crops.
* **Dawn and Sunrise Invocations**: Worshippers greeted the rising sun facing east at daybreak, offering prayers to protect fields against unseasonal frost, blight, and rain rot.
* **Seasonal Grain Offerings**: Consecration of freshly baked bread loaves and grain porridge during spring and summer festivals to celebrate the victory of daylight.
