## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Ūsiņš (Lithuanian scholarly form *Ūsinis*) is the Latvian spring and horse deity of the night-pasture season. Written attestation begins in the early seventeenth century:

- **1606 Joannis Stribingius**: The Jesuit report from Livonia records *Dewing Vschinge* as a god to whom farmers offered two coins, two loaves, and a piece of fat cast into the fire.
- Later Jesuit annals still name him as overseer of horses (*praeses equorum*), confirming the horse-cult frame across the early modern period.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Ūsiņš governs spring light and the return of green growth, and he is above all the patron of horses and the first *pieguļa* (night pasturing). Dainas cast him as the herdsman who rides out to night pasture and brings leaves to the trees and green clover to the earth.

**Relations:**
- **Horse**: sacred companion and mount; he rides gray (*sirms*) and other steeds and is repeatedly addressed as foal-herder.
- **Stone foal (*akmeņa kumeliņš*)**: recurring mount image in the song tradition.
- **Yellow foal (*dzeltens kumeļš*)**: solar-energy horse motif in Latvian folk symbolism tied to his spring return.
- **Rooster**: principal offering animal of Ūsiņš Day; blood mixed with oats in the manger.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folk address and song imagery present him as a rider and herdsman rather than a fixed painted portrait:
- **Looks & address**: Often called *vecais tēvs* (“old father”) or a good man / brother in dainas.
- **Mounts**: Gray (*sirms*) horse; stone foal; yellow foal in symbolic spring imagery.
- **Symbol**: The *Ūsiņa zīme* (paired back-to-back E / key-like sign) used as a protective mark on objects and thresholds.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

His cult belongs to the stable, the night-pasture meadow, and liminal outdoor offering places:
- **Vidzeme highland**: Core zone of Ūsiņš belief and *pieguļa* rites in the ethnographic record.
- **Stables and manger**: Blood-oats offerings and protective marks at doors and thresholds.
- **Groves, large trees, and stones**: Places where first portions and eggs were left; an egg in an oak hollow appears in horse-strength lore.
- **Ūsiņakmens (Vidzeme)**: Large hollowed stone whose folk name invokes Ūsiņš within the same highland landscape of night-pasture offerings.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Ūsiņš Day (*Ūsiņdiena*), syncretized with Jurģi around 23 April, opens the horse and pasture year:
- **Rooster offering**: The master kills a rooster; blood is mixed with oats in the horse manger so the horses eat it.
- **Pantāgs**: Night herders cook the ritual egg dish over a campfire on the first *pieguļa*; the first spoonful is given to Ūsiņš (poured into a stake or axe-helve hole, or set in a small pit, then covered).
- **Beer and eggs**: Accompanying festive foods of the spring horse rites.
- **Protective marks**: *Ūsiņa zīme* on objects and thresholds; rooster-blood crosses on stable doors against harmful spirits.
