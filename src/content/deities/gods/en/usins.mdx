## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Ūsiņš (Lithuanian *Ūsinis*) is the Baltic god of spring light, horses, nighttime pasture herding (*naktigonė*), and bees. His name stems from the Proto-Baltic root *\*aus-* / *\*ūs-* (to shine, dawn; cf. *Aušrinė*). Historical accounts document Ūsiņš in Dionysius Fabricius' 1610 Livonian chronicle, describing spring egg and rooster sacrifices offered by farmers to protect horses at the start of agricultural work.

In Haralds Biezais' research, Ūsiņš is characterized as the deity of spring warmth who brings green grass and guards horses.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Ūsiņš governs spring sunshine, horse breeding, night pasturing, blooming meadows, and bee swarms. He is depicted as a youthful rider in golden garments mounted on a yellow steed.

On Ūsiņš Day (late April), he opens the gates for spring pasture and leads horses to their first night grazing.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Festivities on Ūsiņš Day centered around night herders and horses:

- **The Ritual Omelet (Pantāgs)**: On the first night of pasture herding, herders built a campfire and cooked a ritual scrambled egg dish (*Pantāgs*), offering the first portion to Ūsiņš at a sacred tree.
- **Bathing and Wreathing Horses**: Horses were washed in rivers and adorned with green leafy wreaths.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Latvian *Dainas* celebrate Ūsiņš as the guardian of steeds:
> "Ūsiņš rides over the hill,  
> Wearing a bright yellow coat.  
> Come, Ūsiņš, let us speak  
> Of my cherished horses."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Ūsiņš traditions endure in Baltic night-herding songs (*naktigonės dainos*) and equestrian folk customs.
