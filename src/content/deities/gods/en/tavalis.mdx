## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In historical documentation of 16th-century Baltic religion, Tawals appears in the Samogitian catalog:
* **Chronicle Record**: Recorded in Jan Łasicki's treatise *De diis Samagitarum* as *Tawals*, accompanied by the explicit Latin definition *Deus, auctor facultatum* (the deity who bestows capabilities, means, and resources).
* **Philological Analysis**: Linguistic scholarship associates the name with Proto-Baltic roots of ancestral kinship (*tėvas*, father/ancestor) or the archaic ritual clapper *tabalas* linked to winter mutual aid customs.
* **Property & Patrimony**: The early modern Latin term *facultates* denotes inherited means, capabilities, and patrimonial wealth maintained within the lineage.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

Tawals represents divine guardianship over communal and ancestral sustenance:
* **Bestower of Means**: Invoked to preserve the practical capability of the family and community to meet livelihood needs.
* **Patrimonial Security**: Associated with the inviolability of inherited property, ancestral homestead boundaries, and household solvency.
* **Communal Solidarity**: Functions within the social sphere of communal mutual assistance during winter collective gatherings.

## 3. Character & Visuals

The deity exists in the chronicle tradition as a functional patron of lineage welfare:
* **Ancestral Presence**: Conceived as an invisible divine guarantor behind the stability of the ancestral home and hearth.
* **Attributes of Measure**: Symbolized conceptually by inherited measures of grain, boundary marks, and the wooden clapper (*tabalas*).
* **Custody of Resources**: Associated with stored provisions, seed grain, and the enduring vitality of the farmstead treasury.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The realm of Tawals corresponds to human settlement geography in Western Lithuania:
* **Ancestral Homesteads**: The central living and storage buildings where inherited family property was preserved.
* **Village Assembly Grounds**: Community meeting clearings in Samogitia where collective mutual aid agreements were ratified.
* **Historic Samogitian Settlements**: The regional farming communities documented in 16th-century historical chronicles.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Customary practices focused on the preservation and blessing of patrimonial property:
* **Communal Winter Customs**: Participation in seasonal cooperative customs involving ritual sounding of the clapper to foster fellowship.
* **Inheritance Benedictions**: Customary formulas invoked upon transferring the homestead management to the succeeding generation.
* **Conservation of Means**: Ritual care taken during autumn storage to safeguard grain and patrimonial resources against loss.
