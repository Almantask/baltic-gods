## 1. Etymology & Historical Chronicles

Pliuktis (Lithuanian: *Pliuktis / Pliukštis*; Latin chronicle gloss: *Plusczius / Lituwanis*; Latvian: *Pliuktis / Pļūktis*) is the Baltic atmospheric deity presiding over rain showers, sudden downpours, and soil moisture. The name is etymologically derived from the Lithuanian onomatopoeic verbal root *\*pliuk-* / *\*pliūp-* (attested in *pliukšti* "to gush, splash, deflate with water" and *pliūptelėti* "to burst out pouring, splash heavily"), embodying the sudden, heavy drumming of summer rain upon parched fields.

In Baltic mythography and 16th-century source documentation:

* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum...*, 1582, pub. 1615)**: Records specialized atmospheric numina invoked for precipitation and crop protection, noting that communities performed solemn petitions to ensure timely rains for crops.
* **Maciej Stryjkowski (*Kronika Polska, Litewska...*, 1582)**: Describes ancestral Baltic drought rituals where agricultural communities made offerings at field boundaries to secure rain without destructive thunderbolts.
* **Comparative Baltic Context**: Functionally parallels the Latvian atmospheric mother deity **Lietus māte** ("Mother of Rain"), who dispenses clouds and morning mists to nourish growing vegetation.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In Baltic cosmology, Pliuktis occupies the atmospheric middle sphere between the celestial thunder realm and the plowed earth.

### Dispenser of Precipitation & Drought Alleviator

* **Master of Soil Moisture**: Regulates the gentle, continuous summer rain showers (*šiltas lietus*) that soak into the root layer of grain and flax fields without flattening the stalks.
* **Drought Relief Benefactor**: Invoked by agricultural communities during severe summer heat waves when rivers dry up and young crops begin to wither.
* **Atmospheric Functional Differentiation**: Functions distinct from the fiery wrath of **Perkūnas**, embodying the purely beneficial, life-giving dimension of falling rain.
* **Patron of Flax & Spring Crops**: Specifically associated with the delicate water requirements of flowering flax (*linai*) and spring barley (*miežiai*).

## 3. Character & Visuals

Baltic folklore and mythological reconstructions characterize Pliuktis through atmospheric, water-laden imagery:

* **Fluid Atmospheric Form**: Visualized as an agile, misty-bearded spirit moving rapidly beneath dark grey rain clouds, with streaming hair resembling falling rain curtains.
* **Water-Dripping Garments**: Clothed in billowing, fog-grey linen robes drenched with sparkling water drops, shifting in hue from vaporous white to deep slate blue.
* **Emblematic Implements**: Holds a wooden water-sieve (*rėtis* / rain-making sieve) through which cloudwaters are dispersed evenly, and a wide-mouthed clay pitcher.
* **Accompaniment of Showers**: Preceded by the refreshing scent of wet soil (*ozonas / žemės kvapas*) and the sudden coolness of pre-storm downdrafts.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred domain of Pliuktis comprises elevated hilltops and weather-facing fields across Lithuania:

* **Samogitian Ritual Hilltops**: Prominent upland ridges near Medvėgalis and Šatrija where farmers gathered to observe weather omens and cloud movements.
* **Flax Fields & River Meadows**: Low-lying agricultural basins where moisture-sensitive crops were cultivated.
* **Sacred Rain Springs & Stream Confluences**: Forest springs and river bends where drought libations were gathered and poured onto hot stones.
* **Field Boundary Markers**: Ritual stones situated along parish boundaries where collective rain-calling processions assembled.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Folk Practices

Historical accounts and ethnographic records document specific ceremonial protocols performed to invoke Pliuktis during droughts:

* **Water-Pouring Libations (*Aplaistymas*)**: Processions of village women and youths climbed to sacred hilltops carrying wooden vessels, pouring clean spring water onto dry boundary stones while chanting rain-calling incantations.
* **Sieve Rituals & Weather Divination**: Pouring water through wicker sieves (*rėtis*) over hot hearthstones or field stones to sympathetically induce cloud condensation.
* **Grain & Mead Dedications**: Leaving small clay bowls filled with fresh mead, beer, and sprouted grain ears at field margins during prolonged spring dry spells.
* **Drought Taboos**: Observing strict silences and prohibiting the burning of pasture brush during peak vegetative growing weeks to avoid driving rainclouds away.
