## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Vėjopatis (also *Vėjų dievas*, Latvian *Vēja māte*) is the Baltic lord of winds, atmospheric currents, and soul usher. His name is a compound of *vėjas* (wind) and *patis* / *viešpats* (lord, master; cf. Sanskrit *Vāyu*, Latin *Ventus*). He is documented in Jan Łasicki’s 1582 *De Diis Samagitarum* as *Vėjopatis*, ruler of atmospheric movements.

Research by Norbertas Vėlius portrays Vėjopatis as a two-faced winged elder holding a rooster and a fish, commanding the four cardinal winds.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Vėjopatis governs winds, cloud drift, maritime sails, and the escort of departed souls (*vėlės*) to the afterlife (*Anapilis*).

In Baltic mythology, wind is linked to human spirit (*vėlė*). When a person dies, their soul flies with the wind, guided by Vėjopatis as a celestial shepherd.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Rituals honoring Vėjopatis:

- **Flour and Feather Offerings**: During severe gales, fishermen and farmers tossed handfuls of flour or feathers into the air calling: "Lord Vėjopatis, be calm, fill our sails gently!"
- **Weather Vane Consecration**: Roof and mast weather vanes served as sacred symbols invoking Vėjopatis' favor.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Folktales place Vėjopatis' glass castle on an island in the middle of the sea.

In Lithuanian folk song:
> "Vėjopatis, father of winds,  
> Carrying clouds across the sky,  
> Blow away the dark storm,  
> Reveal the sun's warm rays."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Vėjopatis' imagery lives on in Baltic coastal weather vanes and maritime folklore.
