## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Bentis (historically chronicled as *Bentis*) is the Baltic deity of travel fellowship, road solidarity, and mutual trust among wayfarers.

Sixteenth-century historical chronicles and Baltic linguistic research document this tradition:

* **Sixteenth-Century Chronicle Attestations**: Documented in Jan Łasicki's treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (composed c. 1582, published Basel 1615, reprinted in BRMŠ Vol. 2) with the explicit gloss: *Bentis... qui viatores in via conjungunt* ("Bentis... who joins travelers together on the road"). Łasicki pairs Bentis structurally with **Derintojas**: *Derintoy, qui discordes concordant* ("Derintojas, who reconciles those in dispute").
* **Baltic Linguistic Etymology**: Derived from the Proto-Indo-European root *\*bhendh-* ("to bind, tie, join together", cognate with Sanskrit *bandhati*, Germanic *binden*, English *bind* and *bond*), yielding Lithuanian *bendras* ("companion, common, partner"), *bendrystė* ("fellowship, communion"), and Latvian *biedrs* ("companion, comrade") / *bendre* ("communal farming partner").
* **Comparative Folklore Parallels**: Corresponds in Latvian folk tradition to the protective domain of **Ceļa māte** (LD 31789-0, Mother of Roads) and legends of **Dievs** walking the roads disguised as a wayfarer (*ceļavīrs / ciemiņš*) to test hospitality and mutual peace among travelers.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic social ethics and travel practice, Bentis regulates encounters beyond the domestic homestead:

### Patron of Wayfarers, Road Solidarity, and Peaceful Encounters

* **Uniter of Travel Companions**: Transforms potential suspicion or conflict between strangers meeting on forest roads into mutual solidarity and joint defense.
* **Sanctity of the Highway**: Sacralizes travel pacts, trade journey alliances, and shared provisions among wayfarers navigating dense wilderness tracts.
* **Social Harmony Complementarity**: Functions in tandem with *Derintojas* (the divine arbitrator and peacemaker) and *Keliukas* (guardian of road paths).

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folk tradition conceives Bentis through symbols of fellowship and shared journeys:

* **Clasped Hands and Handshake**: Symbolized by joined right hands sealing mutual travel assistance pacts.
* **The Shared Road Loaf (*Kelio duona*)**: Represented by broken rye bread and salt shared at road junctions.
* **Ribboned Walking Staff**: Evoked through the traveler's rowan staff bound with woven linen ribbon and two converging trails forming a single highway.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Bentis encompasses travel corridors, crossroads, and resting shrines:

* **Forest Crossroads and Trade Routes (*Kryžkelės ir vieškeliai*)**: Traditional road junctions and historic trade pathways across Samogitia (`samogitia-traveler-shrine`).
* **River Fords and Wayfarer Shelters**: River crossings where travelers gathered to wait for safe passage and form convoys.
* **Boundary Rest Stones**: Large roadside boulders where travelers rested, shared provisions, and exchanged news.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Ethnographic and historical sources record established traveler customs:

* **Bread-Breaking at Crossroads**: Breaking and sharing a piece of bread upon meeting a travel partner before entering deep forest tracks.
* **Peaceful Road Invocations**: Uttering sacred greetings (*„Padėk Dieve“ / „Palīdz Dievs“*) and raising hands in peace when crossing paths with strangers.
* **Crossroad Offerings**: Leaving small bread crusts or pinches of salt at boundary markers to ensure an unobstructed and harmonious journey.
