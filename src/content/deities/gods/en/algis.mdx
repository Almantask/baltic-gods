## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In historical accounts of Samogitian pre-Christian religion, Algis is documented as a divine messenger:
* **Chronicle Record**: Explicitly recorded in Jan Łasicki's 1582 treatise *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* as *Algi*, glossed with the Latin definition *Algi tanquam angelum* ("Algis, acting like an angel") and characterized as an angelic messenger of the highest gods.
* **Linguistic Derivation**: Etymologically derived from the Baltic root *\*alg-* (seen in Lithuanian *alga* meaning "wage, reward, dedication to service", and archaic *algoti* meaning "to hire, summon, serve"), closely connected with archaic Baltic personal names such as *Algis*, *Algimantas*, and *Algirdas*.
* **Comparative Functions**: Functions in Baltic tradition as an intermediary between worlds, ensuring that the spoken words and sacrifices of mortals reach the higher celestial sphere.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

Algis embodies the Baltic understanding of spiritual communication and prayer transmission:
* **Messenger of Supplications**: Conveys human prayers, petitions, and ritual requests upward from the domestic and community spheres to celestial deities.
* **Divine Intermediation**: Operates at the threshold between the human world and the divine realm, ensuring that spoken vows, vows of debt, and seasonal requests are faithfully delivered.
* **Pantheon Relationships**: Serves the supreme celestial god *Dievas* and thunder ruler *Perkūnas*, while acting in functional complementarity with *Prigirstis* ("the listener", the spirit who hears whispered human words).

## 3. Character & Visuals

The deity is manifested through the visual symbolism of spiritual ascent and swift transmission:
* **Ascending Hearth Smoke**: Symbolized by columns of clean white smoke rising straight upward from domestic hearths and outdoor ritual fire pits.
* **Tokens of Flight**: Conceptually represented by white bird feathers, wings of migrating birds, and the morning light breaking through tree canopies.
* **The Whispered Word**: Evoked through the open ear and the focused breath of prayer spoken at sacred boundaries.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred presence of Algis was acknowledged at boundary points and ritual gathering sites:
* **Crossroads & Settlement Exits**: Rural Samogitian crossroads where travelers paused before long journeys to send prayers upward for divine guidance.
* **Sacred Fire Hearths**: Domestic and communal fireplaces where offerings were burned so that ascending smoke carried the petitioner's words into the sky.
* **High Hilltop Clearings**: Elevated clearings where clear horizons and rising winds facilitated direct communion with the upper celestial sphere.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Customary practices honored Algis through devotional invocations and smoke offerings:
* **Smoke Offerings (*Dūmų auka*)**: Casting fragrant dried herbs, small pieces of wax, or grain into the hearth fire so that the rising smoke carried prayers heavenward.
* **Crossroads Invocations**: Reciting spoken formulas and pausing in silence at crossroads before undertaking hazardous enterprises or communal negotiations.
* **Morning Petitions**: Uttering quiet prayers at dawn facing eastward, asking Algis to convey the household's needs to the guardian deities of the day.
