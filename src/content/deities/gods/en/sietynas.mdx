## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Sietynas (Latvian: *Sietiņš*, "the Celestial Sieve") is the Baltic ethnoastronomical personification of the Pleiades open star cluster (M45 in the constellation Taurus), serving as the supreme astral calendar and seasonal timekeeper across Lithuanian and Latvian traditions.

Historical sources and ethnoastronomical studies document the authentic lineage:

* **Baltic Ethnoastronomical Corpus**: Comprehensive field studies by Jonas Vaiškūnas (*Skaitant dangaus ženklus*, 2012) and Libertas Klimka (*Senovės lietuvių kosmologija*, 1994) establish Sietynas as the central constellation governing traditional Baltic calendar calculations.
* **Latvian Daina Tradition (*Latvju dainas*, ed. Krišjānis Barons)**: Over fifty authentic folk songs record *Sietiņš* as a nocturnal clock (*debesu pulkstenis*) for herdsmen and a celestial regulator of spring and autumn farm labor (LD 3018, LD 6809, LD 6756).
* **Early Historical Chronicles (Jan Łasicki 1582 / 1615)**: Records Baltic veneration of celestial constellations and stars guiding agricultural seasons.
* **Etymological and Philological Analysis**: The teonym derives directly from the Baltic root *\*siet-* (*sietas* "sieve, riddle; net"), inspired by the perforated mesh arrangement of the stars filtering celestial light and rain.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In Baltic celestial cosmology, Sietynas occupies a vital place in the upper sky, regulating seasonal precipitation and guiding the rhythmic labor of Baltic farmsteads.

### Astral Clock of the Farming Year and Sieve of Celestial Rain

* **Spring Plowing Marker (*Jorė / St. George*)**: The heliacal setting of Sietynas into the evening twilight in late April signals the opening of spring plowing and cattle grazing (*Sietynas žaroje – kumelė vagoje*).
* **Midsummer Turning Point (*Septyni miegantys broliai*)**: The dawn reappearance of Sietynas around July 10 (the Seven Sleeping Brothers) marks the turning of summer toward autumn.
* **Autumn Rye Sowing Regulator**: When Sietynas culminates in the southern sky before dawn in late August and early September, it announces the mandatory window for sowing winter rye (*Sietynas ateina į pietus – metas rugius sėti*).
* **Cosmic Rain Filter**: Conceived as the heavenly sieve through which Perkūnas sifts torrential atmospheric waters into gentle, nourishing raindrops that bless the soil without flattening crops.
* **Mythological Fraternity**: Personified as seven brothers (*Septyni broliai*) or celestial maidens who assist the gods in maintaining celestial order against chthonic chaos.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic astral folklore portrays Sietynas through radiant celestial iconography:

* **Seven Radiant Celestial Brothers**: Conceived as seven youthful celestial riders or star-maidens traversing the dark blue bowl of the night sky.
* **Silver Perforated Sieve**: Held in the hands of the celestial elder as a circular silver riddle with glowing mesh points scattering drops of dew.
* **Starry Azure Cloak**: Clothed in a deep midnight-blue mantle embroidered with crystalline starlight diamonds and silver clasps.
* **Sparkling Crystalline Gaze**: Visualized with clear, diamond-bright eyes that pierce through night mists and autumnal frost.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Sietynas spans the open night skies and ancient observation vantage points:

* **Kulionys Astronomical Sanctuary (Aukštaitija)**: The historic ethnoastronomical observation post near Lake Želva where pole-alignments tracked the heliacal risings of Sietynas.
* **Eastern Horizon Hilltops and Mounds**: Elevated ridges across Lithuania and Latvia used by village elders to observe dawn and dusk star appearances.
* **Open Fallow Furrows**: The agricultural ploughed fields whose seasonal transformation is directly governed by Sietynas's movement.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic farmers and night-watchers maintained systematic observational and seasonal customs:

* **Twilight Sowing Vigils**: Farmers watching the southern sky at dawn to time the exact morning for casting the first handful of consecrated winter rye.
* **Night-Herdsmen Watches (*Pieguļa*)**: Latvian and Lithuanian horse-watchers tracking the elevation of Sietynas through the night to measure elapsed hours before dawn.
* **Seasonal Sieve Divination**: Performing riddle and sieve rotations during winter solstice evenings to divine future rainfall and crop yields.
