## 1. Etymology & Historical Chronicles

Lygyna (Lithuanian: *Lygyna / Ligyčius / Lyginytė*; Latin chronicle gloss: *Lyginitem / Ligiczus*; Latvian: *Lygyna / Ligītis*) is the Baltic agricultural deity presiding over field purity, crop evenness, weed eradication, and agrarian harmony. The theonym is formed from the Baltic root *\*lyg-* (attested in Lithuanian *lyginti* "to level, smooth out, make equal, reconcile", *lygus* "level, smooth, uniform", and Latvian *līdzināt* / *līdzīgs*), reflecting the agricultural necessity of maintaining cleanly sifted, level furrows free from weeds.

Historical chronicle documentation includes:

* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum...*, 1582, pub. 1615)**: Records *Lyginitem* as the deity invoked specifically against corn cockle and noxious darnel (*"Lyginitem orant pro lolio"*) to preserve the purity of grain fields. In the same catalogue, Łasicki records *Ligiczus* as the divine arbiter of peace and conciliation (*concordiae deus*), reflecting the close ideological linkage between equitable communal boundaries and peaceful clan farming.
* **Comparative Baltic Context**: Parallels the Latvian field guardian **Lauka māte** ("Mother of the Field") and **Tīruma māte**, who guard sown furrows against noxious weed infestations and blight.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

In Baltic agrarian theology, Lygyna governs the geometric and biological order of cultivated fields, mediating the delicate balance between sown domestic grain and invasive wild weeds.

### Guardian of Field Purity & Crop Harmony

* **Eradicator of Noxious Weeds**: Protects rye and wheat fields against poisonous corn cockle (*Agrostemma githago*) and darnel (*Lolium temulentum* / *raugės*), which taint flour and cause dizziness.
* **Preserver of Level Furrows (*Lygi vaga*)**: Oversees the straight, uniform plowing and harrowing of fields, ensuring even depth of seed germination.
* **Arbiter of Field Boundaries & Land Peace**: Guards the unplowed balks (*ežios / rubežiai*) separating neighboring farms, preventing boundary disputes and theft of topsoil.
* **Sifting & Seed Cleansing Patron**: Presides over the pre-sowing preparation of seed corn through hand sieving and winnowing.

## 3. Character & Visuals

Mythological analysis and folklore accounts depict Lygyna as a serene, meticulous guardian of agricultural order:

* **Orderly & Serene Demeanor**: Visualized as a calm, precise maiden of youthful grace with clear green-hazel eyes and golden-brown hair neatly braided and wrapped with a flax band.
* **Neat Linen Garments**: Dressed in immaculate, unwrinkled white linen garments bordered with geometric green and brown diamond embroidery (*vytinės juostos*).
* **Emblematic Implements**: Carries a wooden grain sieve (*rėtis*) for separating weed seeds from clean rye, a curved bronze weeding hook, and a smooth leveling rod.
* **Gentle Field Presence**: Walks slowly between grain furrows at twilight, inspecting the height of stalks to ensure balanced, uniform growth across the field.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The sacred domain of Lygyna encompasses the plowed fields, boundary balks, and arable plains of Samogitia and Western Lithuania:

* **Varniai & Kražiai Arable Plains**: Flat fertile agricultural basins in central Samogitia where intensive grain cultivation and communal land divisions were practiced.
* **Field Boundary Balks (*Ežios*)**: The unplowed grassy ridges marking clan property lines where weed-warding rituals were conducted.
* **Threshing Barn Winnowing Floors**: Open timber floors where drafts of wind were used to separate weed seeds (*raugės*) from clean grain.
* **Sacred Boundary Stones (*Ribos akmenys*)**: Flat stones embedded at field intersections where peace offerings were left before spring sowing.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Folk Practices

Historical sources and ethnography detail specific rites performed to secure Lygyna's protection:

* **Pre-Sowing Seed Purification Rites**: Sifting seed grain through traditional horsehair sieves while chanting prayers to Lygyna to cast out every weed kernel (*raugių atskyrimas*).
* **Boundary Peace Agreements (*Lygintuvės*)**: Farmers met at field borders in early spring, sharing a loaf of bread baked from pure rye flour over the boundary stone to ensure harmonious coexistence and straight plowing.
* **Weed-Gathering Sacrifices**: Pulling the first sprouted wild cockle by hand and burning it at the field margin as a prophylactic offering against wider field contamination.
* **Evenness Taboo**: Prohibiting crooked, rushed plowing; irregularly plowed fields were believed to invite weed swarms and divine disfavor.
