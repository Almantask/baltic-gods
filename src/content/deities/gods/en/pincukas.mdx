## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Pinčukas (also *Pinčia*, *Paudruvės velnias*; Latvian: *Purva velns*) is the named regional trickster devil of Northern Lithuanian folklore. Linguistically, the theonym *Pinčukas* has been analyzed by folklorists and onomasticians (Birutė Jasiūnaitė, *Lietuvių velniavardžiai*) as deriving from the ethnonymic root *pinskas* (designating an itinerant merchant or stranger from the Pinsk marshes in Polesia), reinforced in popular etymology by the verb *pinti* / *pingti* ("to braid, tangle, confuse").

Historical folklore collections and literary adaptations record Pinčukas across several centuries of Baltic tradition:
- **Folklore Catalogs**: Jonas Balys cataloged regional legends (*sakmės*) from the Pasvalys and Biržai districts in *Lietuvių pasakojamosios tautosakos motyvų katalogas* (1936) and *Lietuvių mitologiškos sakmės* (1956), documenting tales of the bumbling marsh devil who enters into blood pacts with local millers.
- **Literary Masterpiece**: Immortalized in 20th-century literature by Kazys Boruta in his 1945 novel *Baltaragio malūnas* (*Whitehorn's Mill*), which brought authentic Paudruvė folklore into Lithuanian national consciousness.
- **Baltic Comparative Context**: In Latvian folklore, this regional trickster figure corresponds functionally to the *purva velns* (marsh devil) and *viltīgais velniņš* (cunning little devil) of folk tales (*pasakas par velniem*).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Unlike the primordial cosmic Velnias who rules the underworld and herds the dead, Pinčukas represents the localized, tangible trickster spirit (*liekno velnias*):
- **Bargains and Domestic Interference**: Dwelling in local wetlands, Pinčukas makes rash pacts with farmers, millers, and blacksmiths—such as turning watermill wheels without wind or water in exchange for promises of bridehood or souls.
- **Duel Against Perkūnas**: In weather folklore, Pinčukas dreads thunderstorms, trembling before Perkūnas and fleeing into dense quagmires and hollow tree trunks to escape lightning bolts aimed directly between his eyes (*į tarpukaktę*).
- **Trickster Demeanor**: Prone to deceit, arrogance, and pettiness, yet easily duped by quick-witted human tricksters, losing wagers and fleeing in humiliation.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Folklore accounts and traditional narratives provide consistent visual characteristics for Pinčukas:
- **Anthropomorphic & Zoomorphic Form**: Depicted as a squat, limping little man (*šlubas vokietukas*) concealing miniature horns (*ragiukai*), a bristly goat tail (*uodega*), and a single horse hoof (*arklio kanopa*).
- **Foreign Attire**: Dressed in city or foreign-style clothing—a tight green or black German frock coat (*vokiškas surdutas* / *frokas*) and a cocked cap adorned with a bright rooster feather (*skrybėlaitė su gaidžio plunksna*).
- **Accessories**: Often seen leaning on a walking cane, smoking a short clay pipe (*pypkė*), or carrying a greasy deck of playing cards (*kortos*).

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Pinčukas is intimately tied to specific wetland geography in Northern Lithuania:
- **Paudruvė Marshes (*Pinčuko lieknas*)**: The sodden quagmires, alder bogs, and waterlogged peat hollows of the Paudruvė region between Pasvalys and Biržai.
- **Watermills and Mill Ponds**: The dammed mill ponds and grinding chambers of historic wooden mills (*Baltaragio malūnas*), where chthonic tricksters congregate around spinning millstones.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Because Pinčukas is an adversarial demon rather than a revered deity, folk traditions center on protective evasion and comedic outwitting:
- **Dawn & Rooster Crow**: Waiting for the first morning cockcrow (*gaidžio giedojimas*), which instantly dissolves devilish contracts and breaks phantom illusions.
- **Apotropaic Charms**: Using blessed salt, chalk crosses, and hazel twigs to seal doorways against demonic intrusion.
- **Impossible Tasks & Card Tricks**: Imposing impossible labor upon the devil—such as carrying water in a willow sieve (*rėtis*) or spinning rope from wetland sand—to annul demonic bargains.
