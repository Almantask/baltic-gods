## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

In the historical sources of early modern Lithuania, rural feminine deities left distinctive documentary records:
* **Jesuit Missionary Documentation**: Documented in early 17th-century missionary annual letters from Samogitia, which record the veneration of sacred stones explicitly termed *Deyves* (*lapides non parvi... quos Deyves appellant*), as well as sacred trees.
* **Linguistic Etymology**: Directly derived from Baltic *deivė* (cognate with Latvian *dieve*, from Proto-Indo-European *\*deyw-*, signifying a divine female being or goddess).
* **Folkloric Continuity**: Represents the localized domestic and agrarian stratum of Baltic female divinities, functioning as the immediate historical predecessor to the folkloric *Laumės* and *Laimos*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, & Sacred Domain

The Deivės held an essential protective role across agrarian and female life cycles:
* **Agricultural Abundance**: Revered in farmsteads as custodians of grain fertility (*grūdų saugotojos*) and domestic livestock health.
* **Women's Crafts & Lifecycle**: Presided over women's threshold experiences: conception, safe childbirth, marriage matchmaking, and domestic craft labor—spinning, weaving, and linen preparation.
* **Kinship with Nature**: Connected closely with forest ecology, sacred trees, and woodland creatures, most notably the horned deer (*briedis, elnias*).

## 3. Character & Visuals

The sacred presence of the Deivės was anchored in venerated physical epiphanies:
* **Sacred Cult Stones**: Incarnated in prominent smooth or cup-marked boulders (*deivių akmenys*) venerated within the homestead or in forest clearings.
* **Textile Attributes**: Closely accompanied by the tools and products of female craft: wooden spindles, flax distaffs, skeins of spun wool, and patterned woven sashes.
* **Offerings of Sustenance**: Manifested conceptually through gifts of freshly baked rye bread and clear spring water placed upon the stone altars.

## 4. Natural Habitat & Related Locations

The cult of the Deivės was rooted in the rural landscapes of Western and Central Lithuania:
* **Farmstead Granaries**: Stones were reverently kept inside granaries (*svirnai*), wrapped or cushioned with clean straw to protect harvest yields.
* **Hollow Forest Oaks & Lindens**: Deep woodland groves and ancient hollow trees where rural women gathered away from missionary oversight.
* **Samogitian Sacred Sites**: Documented historically across regional settlements around Varniai, Kražiai, and Luokė where ancestral cultic practices persisted.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, & Cult Rites

Customary rites maintained respectful contact with the deities through female mediation:
* **Votive Offerings**: Peasant women brought carefully prepared offerings to sacred stones and tree hollows: balls of dyed wool, folded linen cloth, woven belts, bread loaves, and coins.
* **Domestic Consecration**: Ceremonial cleaning and covering of cult stones with unthreshed straw sheaves during autumn threshing and harvest storage.
* **Historical Counter-Reformation**: Early modern ecclesiastical accounts describe missionary priests searching out granary stones, removing them, and holding rituals of exorcism in sacred groves.
