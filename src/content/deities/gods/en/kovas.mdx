## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Kovas (historically recorded in 16th-century source criticism alongside the warhorse spirit *Chaurirari / Kauriraris*; and synthesized in 19th-century historiography as *Kowas*) is the Baltic numen of combat, spring military campaigns, and warrior courage. The teonym stems directly from the Proto-Baltic nominal stem *\*kav-* / *\*kāvā* (Lithuanian *kova* "battle, struggle, duel", *kauti* "to fight, strike, slay", Latvian *kaut* "to slaughter, strike", *kauja* "battle" < Proto-Indo-European *\*kewH-* "to strike, hew"), and is homonymous with Lithuanian *kovas* ("March", the seasonal month when campaigns opened) and the corvid bird *kovas* (the rook, *Corvus frugilegus*).

Historical chronicles and comparative Baltic research document the martial mythologeme:

* **Jan Łasicki (*De diis Samagitarum...*, 1582; publ. 1615)**: Recorded *Chaurirari* as the deity invoked for warhorses (*Chaurirari equis bellicis praeest*), representing the authentic 16th-century equestrian martial spirit of Samogitia.
* **Teodor Narbutt (*Dzieje starożytne narodu litewskiego*, 1835)**: Systematized *Kowas* as the Lithuanian god of war (analogous to Roman Mars), associating him with military banners, weapon dedications, and spring expeditions.
* **Linguistic & Mythological Scholarship**: Kazimieras Būga, Norbertas Vėlius (*Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai*, Vol. 2), Vytautas Ališauskas (*Jono Lasickio pasakojimas apie žemaičių dievus*, 2006), and Gintaras Beresnevičius examine the mythologeme of Kovas as an embodiment of the spring campaign opening when thawing river ice allowed warrior expeditions to commence.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

In the Baltic seasonal warfare cycle, combat expeditions were strictly tied to astronomical and environmental cycles, opening in March (*Kovas*) with the spring thaw.

### Sovereign of Combat and Spring Campaign Awakening

* **Patron of the Spring Muster (*Pavasario žygiai*)**: Presides over the seasonal awakening of military campaigns in March when roads thawed and ice broke on the rivers.
* **Protector of Warhorses and Cavalry (*Karo žirgai*)**: Safeguards battle steeds, armor, and harness, ensuring speed and endurance during mounted charges.
* **Numen of Warrior Valor and Duel**: Inspires unyielding resolve in hand-to-hand combat, strengthening the warrior's arm during the clash of swords.
* **Augury of the Rook (*Kovas / Varnas*)**: Associated in folklore with the early spring arrival of rook flocks, signaling the melting of snow and the time for military assembly.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Baltic folklore and historical military traditions visualize Kovas through dynamic warrior imagery:

* **Stern Armored Cavalry Master**: Conceived as a fierce, resolute warrior chief clad in mail armor with a bronze helmet and flying crimson cloak.
* **Iron Battle Sword and Spear**: Wields an iron longsword (*kalavijas*) and a heavy thrusting spear tipped with tempered steel.
* **Black War Steed**: Mounted upon a powerful black battle charger with silver-embossed bridle and leather trapping.
* **Rook Emblem**: Accompanied by a soaring black rook bird circling over the battlefield assembly ground.

## 4. Natural Habitat and Related Locations

The sacred space of Kovas is centered on Baltic military strongholds and assembly valleys:

* **Kernavė Hillfort Military Center (Pajauta Valley)**: The strategic hillfort complex along the Neris river where armies mustered before major campaigns.
* **Battlefield River Crossings**: Strategic river fords along the Nemunas, Neris, and Dubysa where armies crossed into enemy territory.
* **Tribal Assembly Ridges**: High earthen mounds and ramparts where warriors gathered under tribal banners.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Folk Practices

Baltic military tradition preserved dedicated rituals before setting out for campaign:

* **Pre-Campaign Weapon Consecration**: Warriors assembled on sacred mounds to touch their blades to sacred fire or consecrated whetstones before March expeditions.
* **Warhorse Dedication (*Žirgo apeigos*)**: Pouring ritual beer over the mane and hooves of the lead warhorse to ensure speed and sure-footedness in battle.
* **Observance of Spring Bird Omens**: Reading the flight patterns of the first returning rooks and corvids to determine auspicious days for crossing border rivers.
* **Victory Spoils Dedication**: Hanging captured armor, reins, and swords in sacred oak groves in thanksgiving for survival and military triumph.
