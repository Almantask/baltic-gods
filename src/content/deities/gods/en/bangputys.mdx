## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Bangpūtys (also *Vėjopatis*, Latvian *Jūras māte* / *Vēja māte*, Old Prussian *Autrimpas*) is the Baltic god of the sea, stormy waves, winds, and seafaring fishermen. His name is a compound of *banga* (wave) and *pūsti* / *pūtėjas* (the wave-blower / wind-blower). Historical records document Bangpūtys in 17th–18th century Lithuania Minor chronicles and coastal Curonian oral lore.

In archaeological and mythological research by Norbertas Vėlius and Vladas Žulkus, Bangpūtys is depicted as a stern, winged sea giant hovering over the Baltic Sea, wielding a horn and fishing nets.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Bangpūtys rules the Baltic Sea, the Curonian Lagoon, storm winds, amber deposits, and maritime navigation. He commands the four cardinal winds (Šiaurys, Pietys, Rytai, Vakaris).

He capsizes arrogant seafarers who disrespect the sea, but opens abundant amber beds and guides fish shoals into the nets of honorable fishermen.

## 3. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Maritime offerings to Bangpūtys were mandatory before setting sail:

- **Ale and Amber Offerings**: Before raising sails, fishermen poured ale, tossed bread and amber into the sea, or burned a black rooster on coastal sands asking for calm waters.
- **Curonian Weather Vanes (Vėtrungės)**: Carved wooden weather vanes atop boat masts served as sacred petitions asking Bangpūtys for favorable winds.
- **Storm-Calming Rites**: During gales, fishermen hurled iron blades into cresting waves while shouting invocations to calm Bangpūtys.

## 4. Folklore, Dainas, and Oral Tradition

Curonian Spit lore ties Bangpūtys to the legend of Jūratė and Kastytis. When Perkūnas shattered Jūratė’s amber palace with lightning, Bangpūtys washed pieces of the palace onto Baltic beaches.

Fishermen's songs invoke him:
> "Bangpūtys, father of winds,  
> Calm the fierce waves,  
> Bring our fishermen safely  
> Back to the sandy shore."

## 5. Sacred Geography and Cultural Legacy

Bangpūtys' legacy endures along the Baltic coast: Curonian Spit (Nida, Juodkrantė), Palanga beach, and Šventoji ancient fishing sanctuaries.
