## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

Bangpūtys (also restored as *Bangputis* / *Bangputys*; Old Prussian *Bangputtis*) is a western Lithuanian and Old Prussian god of sea storms, lagoon waves, and the sea wind. His name is a transparent compound of Lithuanian *banga* (“wave”) and *pūsti* (“to blow”) — the wave-blower.

Written attestation begins late:
- **Matthäus Praetorius (*Deliciae Prussicae*, finished about 1690)**: First records *bangputtys* as *ein Gott des Sturms* (“a god of the storm”), drawing also on Johannes Bretke for fishermen’s water rites.
- **Eighteenth-century dictionaries**: Jakob Brodowski and Pilypas Ruigys call him a lagoon / sea god and liken him to Neptune.
- **Ludwig Rhesa (1825)**: Prints the Lithuania Minor fishermen’s song *Išbėg’ išbėgo / Iš Rusnės kiemo*, where he appears as *Bangų dievatis* / *Bangputys*; Vilius Kalvaitis still recorded variants in the Klaipėda region in the 1880s–1890s.

Encyclopedic tradition treats him as closely related to *Vėjopatis* (the wind lord).

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

Bangpūtys governs storm weather over the Curonian Lagoon and Nemunas delta waters known to Lithuania Minor fishermen. He is remembered as an easily angered, hard-to-appease male water power: when fishermen take his “sea calves,” the folk song says the north wind rises and the boat is put at risk. Dictionaries and chroniclers class him among storm and sea gods; living memory of the name among Pamarys fishermen had largely faded by the mid-nineteenth century.

**Relations:**
- **Close counterpart**: *Vėjopatis*, described in encyclopedias as near to Bangpūtys in function.
- **Latvian functional parallels**: *Jūras māte*, *Vēja māte*, and the rarer *Bangu māte* carry sea- and wind-mother roles in Latvian song.
- **Herald companion**: *Jumpira*, a half-woman / half-fish water being said to announce his coming in later Pamarys folk reconstruction.

## 3. Character and Visuals

Sources portray Bangpūtys as a severe, hot-tempered masculine storm power whose favour is sought by caution and offering rather than intimacy:
- **Character**: Austere, sudden to anger, and feared by Prussian and Lithuania Minor fishermen as a malevolent water spirit when provoked.
- **Cult image**: Hints survive of a wooden Bangpūtys figure to which Prussian coastal dwellers made offerings.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

Bangpūtys belongs to the Lithuania Minor / Pamarys fishing world:
- **Curonian Lagoon and Nemunas delta**: Local centre of the mythologeme (Rusnė and neighbouring lagoon shores).
- **Klaipėda region song geography**: Rhesa’s Rusnė song and later Kalvaitis variants keep the name in coastal fishermen’s repertoire.
- **Latvian coast (comparative)**: Sea and wind mothers (*Jūras māte*, *Vēja māte*) hold the parallel coastal domain.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

Praetorius and later mythographers preserve sparse but concrete fishermen’s rites:
- **Burned spoon**: A spoon is stolen and burned in Bangpūtys’s honour.
- **First pike (or other first fish)**: In spring lagoon fishing, the first pike or other fish is baked on coals with hot scales, and the surrounding fire is entreated so the fish is wholly embraced by heat.
- **Ritual bathing**: Fishermen bathe at set times for the water deity’s honour.
- **Naručiai (*naruttes*)**: Special underwater *vaidilučiai* who dive and claim to speak with water beings on the god’s behalf.
- **First catch to beggars**: Favour is also sought by giving the first catch to the poor.
- **Storm song petition**: In the Rusnė song, panicked fishermen cry to cast a golden anchor and climb the mast when *Bangų dievatis* grows angry.
