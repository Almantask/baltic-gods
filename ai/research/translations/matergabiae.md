# Matergabiae (Matergabia)

## Comprehensive Overview

**Matergabiae** (or *Matergabia*) is a specialized Lithuanian (Samogitian) hearth and bread-baking goddess recorded by the 16th-century historian Jan Łasicki. The name translates to "Mother Gabija" (*matrem ignis*), emphasizing her connection to **Gabija**, the primary goddess of the sacred fire and domestic hearth. She specifically guards the process of bread baking, ensuring that the dough rises properly and the hearth fire bakes the bread without burning or ruining it.

### English (EN)
**Matergabiae** is the sacred protectress of the hearth fire and the art of bread baking (*pro panis coctione*). Recorded in 1582 by Jan Łasicki, she represents an aspect of **Gabija**, the fire goddess, revered as the "Mother of Fire." Peasants performed specific rituals for Matergabiae when preparing bread: the first loaf removed from the bread trough (*duonkubilis*) was marked with sacred finger indents or symbols and placed in the oven as a dedicated offering (*ugniakuro auka*). This first baked loaf was then reverently eaten by the master or mistress of the house to secure domestic abundance and hearth harmony. In Latvian folklore, her functions correspond to **Uguns māte** (Mother of Fire) and **Mājas māte** (Mother of the House).

### Lithuanian (LT)
**Matergabiae** (arba *Matergabia*) lietuvių (žemaičių) mitologijoje yra namų židinio ir duonos kepimo deivė (*pro panis coctione*). Jono Lasickio 1582 m. traktate aprašyta kaip „motina Gabija“ (*matrem ignis*), ugnies deivės Gabijos apraiška. Šeimininkės, kepdamos duoną, atlikdavo specialias apeigas: pirmąjį duonos kepalėlį, paimtą iš duonkubilio, paženklindavo pirštų įspaudais arba šventais ženklais ir aukodavo krosnyje kaip ugnies bei židinio auką. Šį kepalą vėliau iškilmingai padalindavo namų šeimininkui ir šeimininkei, kad namuose netrūktų duonos ir ramybės. Latvių tradicijoje atitinka **Uguns māte** bei **Mājas māte**.

### Latvian (LV)
**Matergabiae** (vai *Matergabia*) lietuviešu un žemaišu mitoloģijā ir pavarda uguns un maizes cepšanas aizbildne. Jāņa Lašicka 1582. gada traktātā viņa minēta kā „māte Gabija” (*matrem ignis*), kas uzrauga maizes cepšanas procesu (*pro panis coctione*). Maizes cepšanas laikā pirmā maizes kukulīša mīkla tika ieiezīmēta ar svētām zīmēm un ziedota ugnskuram. Latviešu mitoloģijā šīs funkcijas pilda **Uguns māte** un **Mājas māte**, kas nodrošina maizes svētību un sētas labklājību.

## Primary Source Details

- **Jan Łasicki (Łas):** *De diis Samagitarum caeterorumque Sarmatarum et falsorum Christianorum* (1582; publ. 1615). Records *Matergabiam* as the goddess invoked for bread baking (*pro panis coctione*) and identifies her as *matrem ignis* ("mother of fire").
- **Algirdas Julius Greimas (Grei):** Analyzed the sacralization of domestic functions in *Of Gods and Men* (1992), detailing how bread baking was viewed as a holy transformation overseen by hearth fire deities.
- **Norbertas Vėlius (Vel):** *Baltų religijos ir mitologijos šaltiniai* (1996–2005). Discusses Matergabiae as an epithet/hypostasis of Gabija representing maternal hearth protection.
- **Visuotinė lietuvių enciklopedija (VLE):** Notes Matergabiae as one of the authentic domestic ritual figures in 16th-century Samogitian ethnography.

## Key Metadata
- **Region**: Samogitia (Žemaitija) / Lithuania
- **Tribe**: Samogitians, Lithuanians
- **Period**: 16th Century (Recorded), ancient hearth cult roots
- **Category**: Domestic / Hearth / Agriculture
- **Symbols**: Loaf of Bread, Bread Trough (*duonkubilis*), Oven Flame, Finger Marks
- **Function**: Protection of bread baking, hearth fire, and domestic food security
- **Etymology**: *matė/motina (mother) + *Gabija (goddess of fire, from *gaubti / to cover fire)
