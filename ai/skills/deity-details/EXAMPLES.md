# Deity Entry: Perkūnas (Thunder God)

## 1. Etymology and Historical Chronicles

### Etymology & Name Variants
- **Lithuanian**: *Perkūnas* (dialectal *Perkūns*, *Pūkis* in atmospheric contexts)
- **Latvian**: *Pērkons*
- **Old Prussian**: *Percunis* / *Parkuns*
- **Linguistic Root**: Derived from Proto-Indo-European *\*per-kw-u-* ("oak tree") or *\*per-g-* ("to strike / beat"). Cognate with Slavic *Perun*, Vedic *Parjanya*, Norse *Fjörgynn*, and Latin *quercus* (oak).
- **Epithets**: *Dundulis*, *Dundus*, *Brumblis*, *Tarškulis*, *Grumulis*, *Kaitinis*, *Dievaitis*, *Perkūno tėvelis*.

### Historical Chronicles
1. **Malalas Chronicle (1261 Slavonic translation insertion)**: Earliest slavonic gloss mentioning Baltic deities: Sovijus sacrifices to *Perkūnas*, *Teliavelis*, *Andajus*, and *Žvorūna*.
2. **Chronicon Terrae Prussiae (Peter of Dusburg, 1326)**: Notes the supreme sanctuary at Romuva in Nadruvia, where *Parkuns* was worshiped alongside *Peckols* (underworld) and *Patrimpas* (fertility/spring).
3. **Annales seu Cronicae incliti Regni Poloniae (Jan Długosz, 15th c.)**: Compares *Perkūnas* to Roman Jupiter, noting perpetual oak wood fires maintained in his honor in Vilnius.
4. **De Diis Samagitarum (Jan Łasicki, 1582)**: Lists Samogitian cult practices, sacred oaks, and sacrifices of black roosters during thunder storms.
5. **Kronika Polska, Litewska, Żmudzka (Maciej Stryjkowski, 1582)**: Describes the idol of Perkūnas in Vilnius holding a stone hammer/lightning bolt with a sacred flame tended by *vaidelotės*.

## 2. Cosmology, Functions, and Sacred Domain

### Cosmological Position
- **Ruler of the Upper Atmosphere & Celestial Order**: Perkūnas occupies the active middle-upper celestial sphere, acting as the divine judge and enforcer of moral order (*Darna*).
- **Rivalry with Velnias**: Represents the fundamental Baltic cosmic dualism — Perkūnas (celestial fire, rain, order, justice) versus Velnias/Velns (chthonic waters, earth, chaos, wealth, magic).

### Primary Functions
- **Thunder, Lightning & Meteorological Control**: Sends spring rain to open the earth (*atrakinti žemę*) for agriculture.
- **Divine Justice & Law Enforcement**: Punishes evil spirits, oath-breakers, proud mortals, and demonic chthonic forces with his thunderbolts.
- **Fertility & Agricultural Awakening**: The first spring thunder (*pirmasis perkūnijos griausmas*) sanctifies the earth, making it fertile and ready for sowing.

## 4. Natural Habitat, Related Locations

### Sacred Geography & Habitats
- High places: Mountain tops, hillforts (*piliakalniai*), sky clouds, isolated ancient oak groves.
- Waterside oaks: Oak trees standing near rivers or hit by lightning are considered doubly sanctified.

### Specific Historical & Sacred Sites
1. **Romuva Sanctuary (Nadruvia / East Prussia)**: Central Prussian sanctuary with a multi-trunked evergreen oak housing idols of Parkuns.
2. **Vilnius Cathedral Hill / Šventaragis Valley (Lithuania)**: Site of the reported central altar of Perkūnas with an eternal oak-fire shrine.
3. **Rambynas Hill (Pagėgiai municipality, Lithuania)**: Sacred Skalvian hillfort associated with Perkūnas worship and sacrifice altars.
4. **Pērkonkalns & Pērkona ozols (Latvia)**: Numerous Latvian sacred hills and ancient lightning-struck oaks recorded in folk topography.

## 5. Sacred Rites, Offerings, and Cult Rites

### Cult Practices & Festivals
- **Jorė / Jurginės (Spring Awakening)**: Celebration of the first spring thunder. Ground must not be plowed or disturbed prior to first thunder.
- **Summer Solstice (Rasa / Līgo / Jāņi)**: Lighting sacred bonfires on hills to honor celestial fire and Perkūnas's protection over crops.
- **Perkūnas Days (Thursdays / *Ketvirtadienis*)**: Thursday was historically dedicated to Perkūnas across Baltic lands; no spinning or wood-chopping permitted during storms.

### Sacrificial Offerings
- **Traditional Sacrifices**: Black goats, roosters, beer, mead, bread, wax, and silver coins left at the roots of sacred oaks.
- **Emergency Storm Rituals**: Burning sacred herbs (e.g., juniper / *kadagys*) and placing bread/salt on windowsill during severe lightning storms to turn Perkūnas's wrath away.
